Hofsós – A Picturesque Fishing Village on Skagafjörður

Hofsós is a small, picturesque fishing village on the eastern shore of Skagafjörður in North Iceland and one of the country's oldest trading ports. By the harbour stands the Pakkhúsið warehouse from 1777, one of Iceland's oldest buildings, a reminder of the village's long trading history. Hofsós is best known for its stunning swimming pool that seems to merge with Skagafjörður, and for the Icelandic Emigration Center, which preserves the story of Icelandic emigration to North America. This small village blends history, nature and a unique atmosphere, making it an ideal stop for travellers seeking an authentic Icelandic fishing village away from the crowds.

Things to See and Do in Hofsós

The swimming pool in Hofsós is world-famous for its spectacular view, where the pool's edge appears to merge with the fjord and the island of Drangey in the distance. The Icelandic Emigration Center tells the moving story of the thousands of Icelanders who sailed west across the ocean in the 19th and 20th centuries in search of a better life. The old Pakkhúsið warehouse by the harbour is among Iceland's oldest buildings and lends the village a historic character. Nearby lie the basalt columns of Staðarbjörg, a natural wonder worth exploring.

Where Is Hofsós and How to Get There

Hofsós lies on the eastern shore of Skagafjörður in Northwest Iceland, on Route 76, which runs north along the fjord toward Siglufjörður. From the ring road it is a short drive into Skagafjörður and on north to the village, with the route offering lovely views over the fjord and toward Drangey. The nearest larger towns are Sauðárkrókur across the fjord and Siglufjörður further along the road. Hofsós makes an ideal stop on a journey through North Iceland, whether driving between Skagafjörður and the Tröllaskagi peninsula or continuing north along the coast.

Best Time to Visit Hofsós and Practical Tips

The best time to visit Hofsós is from May to September, when the weather is mildest, museums are open and the swimming pool is at its finest in the bright, long summer evenings. The pool is open year-round, but the view over Skagafjörður is especially magical on calm summer evenings and at sunset. Bring swimwear and a towel, as the pool is the highlight of any visit. Outside peak season services are more limited, so it is wise to check museum opening hours and accommodation in advance. Warm clothing is useful, as the breeze off the fjord can be cool.
