Hespuhúsið (The Yarn House)

An open plant-dyeing studio near Selfoss where Icelandic wool is dyed with plants the traditional way.

At Hespuhúsið, Guðrún Bjarnadóttir dyes Icelandic einband yarn with plants following old traditions but with modern technique. She uses Icelandic plants such as rhubarb, lichen, lupine and flowers, along with indigo and other dyes. The studio welcomes visitors by arrangement, where you can peer into the dye pots and buy plant-dyed yarn.

Plant-dyed yarn

Hespuhúsið is a small studio just outside Selfoss where Guðrún Bjarnadóttir dyes Icelandic wool with plants in the old way. She uses plants such as rhubarb, lichen, lupine and flowers, along with indigo, and the result is unique plant-dyed einband yarn in natural colours. For knitters and craft lovers it is local produce found nowhere else, made with patience and knowledge of Iceland's dyeing tradition.

Old tradition, new technique

Plant dyeing has a long history in Iceland, and Hespuhúsið keeps it alive with a blend of tradition and modern technique. Visitors can look into the dye pots, learn about the plants that give the colours and see how the yarn takes on different tones. It is a personal and informative visit that offers insight into a craft many thought had vanished.

Visits by arrangement

The studio is close to Selfoss, right by a roundabout on the South Iceland route, and welcomes guests by arrangement. It is best to call or email before setting out so that someone is there to greet you. The visit suits anyone who wants to buy unique Icelandic yarn straight from the person who dyes it, while supporting a living craft tradition in the south.
