South Iceland – the Golden Circle, glaciers and black beaches

South Iceland (Suðurland) is the country's most visited region, holding some of its most famous natural wonders within easy reach of the capital. Here is the Golden Circle – Þingvellir, Geysir and Gullfoss – along with a long South Coast of black-sand deserts, towering waterfalls, glacier lagoons, volcanoes and lush farmland. Inland open highland gems such as Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk. Selfoss is the largest town and the region's service hub. Few parts of Iceland offer such variety over so short a distance: erupting geysers, canyons, glacier tongues and black beaches blend into one unforgettable experience that draws travellers all year round.

Top places – the Golden Circle and the South Coast

The Golden Circle links three gems: Þingvellir, where the Alþingi parliament was founded in 930 and the continental plates pull apart; Geysir and Strokkur, which erupts every few minutes; and Gullfoss, which plunges in two tiers into a great canyon. Along the South Coast, Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss fall beneath Eyjafjallajökull, the black beach of Reynisfjara shows off basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks, and further east Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon glitters with icebergs beside the Diamond Beach. Offshore lie the Vestmannaeyjar islands with the Eldfell volcano, and in the highlands wait the rainbow rhyolite peaks of Landmannalaugar and the green valley of Þórsmörk.

Getting around South Iceland – the Golden Circle and Ring Road

The Golden Circle can be driven in a single day from Reykjavík, a loop of about 300 km taking in Þingvellir, Geysir and Gullfoss, suited to both self-drive and guided tours. The Ring Road follows the South Coast eastward, past waterfalls, black sands and on to Jökulsárlón, and many allow two to three days or more for that stretch. A ferry runs to the Vestmannaeyjar islands from Landeyjahöfn, while highland routes to Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk require sturdy 4x4s and are passable only in summer across unbridged rivers. Lowland roads are paved and easy to drive year-round.

Best time to visit and practical tips

South Iceland welcomes visitors all year: the Golden Circle, the South Coast and Jökulsárlón are accessible in every season, and in winter you can join blue ice-cave tours beneath the glaciers and chase the northern lights. Summer, June to September, opens the highlands – Landmannalaugar, Þórsmörk and the Laugavegur trail – and brings puffins to the Vestmannaeyjar islands and Dyrhólaey. Take care at Reynisfjara, where dangerous sneaker waves can catch the unwary, keep your distance from waterfall edges and hot springs, and book popular accommodation and tours early. Dress for changeable weather and check road conditions on highland routes in summer.

Places & nature

  • Þingvellir Birthplace of the Alþingi and the meeting point of the continental plates.
  • Geysir og Strokkur The original erupting hot spring — Strokkur erupts every few minutes.
  • Gullfoss The golden waterfall plunges in two tiers into a vast canyon.
  • Reynisfjara og Vík Famous black-sand beach with basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks.
  • Jökulsárlón og Breiðamerkursandur A magical glacier lagoon full of icebergs, with the Diamond Beach alongside.
  • Skógafoss og Seljalandsfoss Two magnificent waterfalls at the foot of Eyjafjallajökull.
  • Vestmannaeyjar A volcanic archipelago with a huge puffin colony and the volcano Eldfell.
  • Landmannalaugar Rainbow rhyolite mountains and a natural hot spring in the heart of the Highlands.
  • Þórsmörk A lush mountain valley surrounded by three glaciers.
  • Kerlingarfjöll Colourful rhyolite mountains with a steaming geothermal area in Hveradalir.
  • Fjaðrárgljúfur A dramatic, winding canyon near Kirkjubæjarklaustur.
  • Kerið A red volcanic crater with blue-green water, often a Golden Circle add-on.
  • Sólheimajökull An accessible glacier tongue popular for guided glacier tours.
  • Sólheimasandur A black-sand plain on the South Coast with the wreck of a US DC-3 plane.

Hiking trails

  • Laugavegurinn One of the world's most famous hiking trails, from Landmannalaugar to Þórsmörk.
  • Fimmvörðuháls Between glaciers, past new craters, from Skógar down into Þórsmörk.
  • Reykjadalur A hike up into a valley to a warm river you can bathe in.
  • Skaftafell A green oasis in Vatnajökull National Park with Svartifoss and glacier views.

Pools & baths

  • Gamla laugin (Flúðir) The oldest swimming pool in the country, a natural geothermal pool in Flúðir.
  • Laugarvatn Fontana Geothermal baths and natural steam rooms on the shore of Lake Laugarvatn.
  • Sundlaugin í Laugaskarði A historic 50-metre outdoor pool tucked in a sheltered gap in Hveragerði.
  • Sundhöll Selfoss One of South Iceland's largest bathing complexes in the centre of Selfoss.
  • Hrunalaug Small stone-built natural pools on private farmland near Flúðir.
  • Seljavallalaug One of Iceland's oldest pools, hidden in a narrow valley under Eyjafjöll.
  • Sundlaug Vestmannaeyja A lively family pool with waterslides on Heimaey island.
  • Sundlaug Kirkjubæjarklausturs A cosy village pool with a view of Systrafoss waterfall from the hot tub.

Restaurants

  • Fjöruborðið A nationally famous langoustine restaurant in the little fishing village of Stokkseyri.
  • Friðheimar Dining inside a tomato greenhouse on the Golden Circle.
  • Gott A popular family-run spot on Heimaey with fresh local ingredients.
  • Lindin A small dining spot by Laugarvatn known for game.
  • Rauða húsið A well-established seafood restaurant in a historic red house in Eyrarbakki.
  • Minilik An Ethiopian restaurant in the rural village of Flúðir on the Golden Circle.

Cafés

  • Kaffi Krús A well-established café in a charming older timber house in Selfoss.
  • Skool Beans A specialty coffee café inside an old yellow school bus in the heart of Vík.
  • Efstidalur II A family-run farm on the Golden Circle with an ice cream barn and restaurant.
  • Systrakaffi A cozy café and restaurant in the heart of Kirkjubæjarklaustur.
  • Eldstó Art Café A café and ceramics gallery where food is served on handmade pottery.
  • Almar Bakari A well-established bakery and café in Hveragerði with fresh pastries.
  • Halldórskaffi A cozy café and restaurant in Vík's historic Brydebúð building.
