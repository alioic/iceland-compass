Laugavegurinn

One of the world's most famous hiking trails, from Landmannalaugar to Þórsmörk.

The Laugavegur runs some 55 km from the colourful rhyolite mountains of Landmannalaugar, across plateaus, geothermal areas and black sand deserts, down into the lush valley of Þórsmörk. It is hiked between the mountain huts of the Iceland Touring Association and the trail is marked, but severe weather can strike without warning.

The Laugavegur Trail – one of the world's most famous hikes

The Laugavegur is one of the world's most celebrated hiking trails and a crown jewel of the Icelandic highlands, a roughly 55-kilometre route running from Landmannalaugar to Þórsmörk. Along the way, hikers experience an astonishing variety of Icelandic nature: colourful rhyolite mountains, steaming geothermal fields, vast plateaus, black sand deserts, glittering glaciers and green valleys. Few trails combine so many contrasts over such a short distance. The Laugavegur is marked and well travelled, yet it remains a genuine highland trek that demands preparation, drawing both seasoned mountaineers and adventurous travellers from all over the world.

What you'll see on the Laugavegur

The Laugavegur is usually divided into four stages between the mountain huts of the Iceland Touring Association. From Landmannalaugar the trail climbs to Hrafntinnusker through snow-flecked rhyolite mountains and geothermal areas, then descends to the beautiful mountain lake Álftavatn. It continues across rivers and sands to Emstrur (Botnar) and finally through birch woodland down into Þórsmörk. Along the way you may see glittering obsidian, colourful peaks, glaciers and deep canyons. Many extend the hike by continuing over Fimmvörðuháls to Skógar, turning the route into one of the most memorable mountain-hiking experiences available anywhere.

The start and end of the Laugavegur – how to get there

The Laugavegur usually starts at Landmannalaugar in the southern highlands and ends in Þórsmörk, though it can also be walked in the opposite direction. Both trailheads lie in the highlands and require travel along mountain roads. From Reykjavík, scheduled buses run to Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk during summer, which is the most common and convenient option for hikers. Those who prefer to drive need a capable four-wheel-drive vehicle and experience with highland driving, as unbridged rivers cross the route. Most people leave the car behind and use the buses to avoid the difficult drive to and from the trail.

Best time, huts and difficulty on the Laugavegur

The Laugavegur is only passable in summer, typically from late June to early September, once the snow has melted and the huts are open. The hike usually takes three to four days and is considered moderately difficult, with several rivers to wade across. The Iceland Touring Association runs mountain huts along the route with campsites beside them, but hut accommodation must be booked well in advance. Highland weather can change suddenly, even in summer, so bring windproof and waterproof clothing, warm layers, sturdy boots and food. Always check the forecast before setting out.
