East Iceland – fjords, reindeer and artist villages

East Iceland (Austurland), home to the East Fjords, is one of the country's calmest and most beautiful regions – a chain of graceful fjords where steep mountains rise straight from the sea and small fishing villages nestle along the shore. This is where Iceland's only wild reindeer herd roams, and above the fjords opens the Fljótsdalshérað district, centred on Egilsstaðir, with the country's largest forest and a thriving cultural life. The region is known for its artist villages, folklore and tranquillity, well away from the busiest tourist trails. The Norræna ferry arrives here directly from mainland Europe, and for anyone wanting authentic fjord life, colourful nature and creative communities, East Iceland is irresistible.

Top places and highlights of the East Fjords

Seyðisfjörður is one of the loveliest villages of the East Fjords, ringed by steep mountains and waterfalls, with its world-famous rainbow street leading to the blue church and a buzzing arts scene. In Stöðvarfjörður, Petra's Stone Collection holds one of the world's largest private mineral collections, full of colourful crystals from the eastern mountains. Hallormsstaður forest is Iceland's largest national forest, set beside the storied Lagarfljót lake, said to be home to a serpent, with Hengifoss falling nearby. Borgarfjörður eystri boasts an accessible puffin colony at Hafnarhólmi and the Víknaslóðir hiking trails, while Djúpivogur, Iceland's first Cittaslow town, is adorned by the Eggin í Gleðivík artwork.

Getting around East Iceland – fjords and mountain roads

The Ring Road runs through East Iceland, crossing heaths or hugging the fjords, and rewards you with sweeping views at every turn. From Egilsstaðir, the region's transport hub, roads branch out to the fjords: over a mountain pass to Seyðisfjörður, south along the coast through Fáskrúðsfjörður and Stöðvarfjörður to Djúpivogur, and north to Borgarfjörður eystri. The region is geographically large, so it is wise to allow at least two to three days. Roads are mostly easy, but some mountain passes, including the one to Seyðisfjörður, close in snow during winter, so keep an eye on road conditions.

Best time to visit and practical tips

Summer, from May into September, is the ideal time to visit East Iceland: puffins arrive at Hafnarhólmi, the trails of Víknaslóðir and Hallormsstaður forest open up, and the village museums welcome visitors. This is when the fjords are at their most colourful and the light lasts deep into the night. In winter the landscape turns majestic, but mountain passes can close, so anyone travelling outside summer should watch the weather and road conditions closely. Fuel up in Egilsstaðir, pack good clothing for changeable weather, and book accommodation early in the smaller villages, where options are limited.

Places & nature

  • Seyðisfjörður An artists' village by a fjord with a rainbow street and beautiful timber houses.
  • Stöðvarfjörður Petra's world-famous private stone collection in a small East Fjords village.
  • Hallormsstaður og Lagarfljót The country's largest forest beside the legend-rich lake Lagarfljót.
  • Borgarfjörður eystri A remote fjord with a puffin colony and some of the best hiking trails in the country.
  • Djúpivogur A peaceful Cittaslow village with the artwork Eggin í Gleðivík.
  • Höfn í Hornafirði The langoustine town at the foot of Vatnajökull with spectacular glacier views.
  • Stokksnes og Vestrahorn A majestic mountain mirrored in a black sand beach — a photographer's dream.
  • Stuðlagil A spectacular basalt-column canyon with a blue-green glacial river in Jökuldalur.

Hiking trails

  • Stórurð An adventurous valley of giant boulders and turquoise pools beneath Dyrfjöll.
  • Hengifoss A hike to one of Iceland's tallest waterfalls, framed by red rock strata.
  • Snæfell A climb of Iceland's highest free-standing mountain on the edge of Vatnajökull.
  • Gerpir A hike to the easternmost point of mainland Iceland with towering sea cliffs.

Pools & baths

  • Vök Baths Iceland's only floating geothermal pools set out in Lake Urriðavatn near Egilsstaðir.
  • Laugarfell Two stone-built natural hot pools on the edge of the highlands beneath Mt. Snæfell.
  • Selárlaug A historic geothermal pool on the bank of the Selá river in Selárdalur valley.
  • Sundlaug Eskifjarðar A friendly town pool with waterslides and mountain views in Eskifjörður.
  • Stefánslaug A hillside town pool with panoramic views over Norðfjörður.
  • Sundlaug Egilsstaða A well-equipped town pool in the heart of Egilsstaðir.
  • Hoffell heitir pottar Geothermal hot tubs with views of Hoffellsjökull glacier near Höfn.
  • Seyðisfjörður Swimming Hall A historic 1948 indoor pool in the arts village of Seyðisfjörður.
  • Reyðarfjörður Swimming Pool Fjarðabyggð's town pool in Reyðarfjörður with good family facilities.
  • Fáskrúðsfjörður Swimming Pool A small indoor pool in the French-heritage town of Fáskrúðsfjörður.
  • Stöðvarfjörður Swimming Pool A small outdoor pool below the school in Stöðvarfjörður, open in summer.
  • Breiðdalsvík Swimming Pool An oval outdoor pool from 2002 in Breiðdalsvík, open in summer.
  • Djúpivogur Swimming Pool The Djúpivogur sports-centre pool, a relaxed bathing spot.
  • Höfn Swimming Pool A large family pool in Höfn with three water slides.
  • Borgarfjörður eystri Swimming Pool A small geothermal pool in Bakkagerði with mountain and fjord views.
  • Vopnafjörður Swimming Pool Vopnafjörður's town pool, separate from the Selárlaug nature pool.

Attractions

  • Petra's Stone Collection One of the world's largest private stone collections.

  • Papey Island Boat Tours A boat trip to the puffin island of Papey from Djúpivogur.
