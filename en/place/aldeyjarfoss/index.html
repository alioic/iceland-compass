Aldeyjarfoss

A powerful waterfall framed by black basalt columns in Skjálfandafljót.

Aldeyjarfoss in the Skjálfandafljót river drops about 20 metres into a pool surrounded by striking dark basalt columns that stand in stark contrast to the white torrent of water. The waterfall lies on the edge of the highlands in Bárðardalur.

Aldeyjarfoss – A Powerful Waterfall Framed by Black Basalt Columns

Aldeyjarfoss is one of North Iceland's most majestic waterfalls, where the Skjálfandafljót river plunges around 20 metres into a deep pool surrounded by striking dark basalt columns. The contrast between the jet-black basalt and the frothing white water makes it one of the most sought-after photography spots on the edge of the highlands. Aldeyjarfoss lies where farmland meets the highlands in Bárðardal, on the way into the untouched wilderness of the Sprengisandur route. Though less famous than many of Iceland's larger waterfalls, it is spectacular and gives visitors a real sense of the raw, wild nature of the Icelandic interior.

Things to See and Do at Aldeyjarfoss

The main attraction at Aldeyjarfoss is the waterfall itself and the extraordinary basalt columns that frame it, the dark hexagonal pillars forming a natural work of art around the white cascade. Short walking paths and viewpoints run along the canyon, allowing you to admire the falls from several angles, making it a true paradise for photographers. A little further up the Skjálfandafljót lies Hrafnabjargafoss, another intriguing waterfall well worth a visit. The area is perfect for nature viewing and photography, and makes a great stop on a journey through Bárðardal or onto the Sprengisandur highland route.

How to Get to Aldeyjarfoss

Aldeyjarfoss lies at the head of Bárðardal in Northeast Iceland, south of Goðafoss and the Ring Road. From the Ring Road you drive south into Bárðardal, and the final stretch to the falls is a gravel road that can be rough. In summer most vehicles can manage the route with care, though a robust four-wheel-drive makes the trip most comfortable. From Akureyri the drive takes about one and a half to two hours. The falls sit on the natural gateway to the highlands via Sprengisandur, so they fit well into a summer journey through Iceland's wilderness.

Best Time to Visit Aldeyjarfoss and Practical Tips

The best time to visit Aldeyjarfoss is from June to September, when the gravel road into Bárðardal is passable and the light is well suited to photography. In winter the road can be impassable due to snow and conditions. Bring warm, waterproof clothing and good footwear, as it can be slippery and wet near the falls and the spray can drift over you. Take care near the canyon edge, which is steep and unfenced. Combine your visit with nearby Goðafoss and allow time to enjoy the tranquillity of this natural gem.
