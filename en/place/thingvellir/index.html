Þingvellir

Birthplace of the Alþingi and the meeting point of the continental plates.

Þingvellir is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the Alþingi, one of the world's oldest parliaments, was founded here in 930. Geologically, the boundary of the Eurasian and North American plates runs through the area, and in Silfra you can dive between the continents in crystal-clear water.

Þingvellir National Park – the heart of the Golden Circle

Þingvellir is one of Iceland's most significant historic sites and the classic first stop on the Golden Circle route. This is where the Alþingi, one of the world's oldest parliaments, was founded in 930 AD, and the site is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List for its cultural and geological importance. At Þingvellir two continents meet, as the boundary between the Eurasian and North American tectonic plates runs across the plain. The blend of history, national heritage and dramatic scenery makes Þingvellir an essential destination for anyone travelling through South Iceland.

Things to see and do at Þingvellir

A walking path leads through Almannagjá, the striking rift between the continental plates, to Lögberg where laws were once proclaimed. Nearby, Öxarárfoss waterfall cascades over the canyon wall and is a popular photo spot. Adventurous visitors can dive or snorkel in Silfra, a fissure filled with crystal-clear glacial water offering some of the best underwater visibility on Earth. Marked trails, a visitor centre and viewing platforms make it easy to explore the park, whether your interest lies in Icelandic history, plate tectonics or photographing this remarkable landscape.

Getting to Þingvellir

Þingvellir lies about 40 minutes' drive from Reykjavík and is the first link in the Golden Circle alongside Geysir and Gullfoss. The route follows Road 36 and is accessible year-round by regular car. Ample parking is available at the visitor centre, where a parking fee applies, and paths lead down into Almannagjá from there. Numerous organised day tours and coach trips from the capital include Þingvellir, making the site convenient for independent travellers and those who prefer a guided experience alike.

Best time to visit Þingvellir and practical tips

Þingvellir welcomes visitors all year, though the experience shifts with the seasons. Summer offers long daylight, greenery and comfortable walking conditions, while winter brings snow-covered scenery and the chance of northern lights. Dress in waterproof layers and sturdy shoes, as the weather can change quickly and paths may become slippery. Diving in Silfra requires a licensed operator and a drysuit. Arriving early in the day helps you avoid the largest crowds and enjoy the tranquillity of this historic place at its finest.
