Vaglaskógur

One of the largest and most beautiful birch forests in the country, in Fnjóskadalur.

Vaglaskógur in Fnjóskadalur is one of the country's largest and most lush forests, with tall birch and conifer trees, walking trails and a campsite by the river Fnjóská. A popular stop for families and outdoor enthusiasts all year round.

Vaglaskógur Forest in Fnjóskadalur — one of Iceland's largest birch woodlands

Vaglaskógur in the Fnjóskadalur valley is one of the largest and most verdant forests in Iceland and a genuine natural gem in North Iceland, a short drive from Akureyri and Goðafoss. Unusually tall, straight birch trees grow here alongside planted conifers, giving the woodland a deep, tranquil character rarely found on the largely treeless island. Vaglaskógur has long been a favourite stop for families and outdoor enthusiasts and is something of a symbol of Icelandic forestry. Few places in Iceland offer such a dense, high birch forest, with the calm Fnjóská river flowing through.

Things to do in Vaglaskógur — walking trails and the outdoors

Vaglaskógur is laced with walking trails suited to all ages, from short family strolls to longer routes deeper into the trees and along the Fnjóská river. The riverside campsite is ideal for an overnight stay, picnics and summer recreation, while autumn brings golden foliage and good berry picking. Visitors love wandering beneath the tall birch canopy, enjoying the quiet, fishing in the river or simply relaxing in the shelter of the trees. Birdlife is varied and the forest is a rewarding spot for photography throughout the year, in every season.

Getting to Vaglaskógur — location in the Fnjóskadalur valley

Vaglaskógur lies in the Fnjóskadalur valley in North Iceland, east of the Vaðlaheiði ridge and only about a half-hour drive from Akureyri. It is easy to reach by road, whether you travel through the Vaðlaheiði tunnel or over the Víkurskarð pass, and the forest sits close to the Ring Road. Its location makes Vaglaskógur a convenient stop between Akureyri and Lake Mývatn or Goðafoss. There is parking by the campsite and the forest is well signposted, so access is straightforward for both day trips and longer stays in the Eyjafjörður area.

Best time to visit Vaglaskógur and practical tips

Vaglaskógur is worth visiting year-round, but summer and autumn are the most popular seasons. In summer the forest is lush and green and the campsite is full of life, while autumn brings golden birch colours and berry picking. In winter the snow-clad woodland can feel magical, though road conditions depend on the weather. Bring a picnic, sturdy footwear and insect repellent on warm summer days. Please respect local rules, keep dogs on a lead and tread lightly on the landscape. Check the campsite opening times and the weather forecast before you set out.
