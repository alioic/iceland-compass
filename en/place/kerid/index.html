Kerið – the red volcanic crater on the Golden Circle

Kerið is one of Iceland's most photogenic volcanic craters and a popular addition to the classic Golden Circle route. Located in the Grímsnes area, this roughly 3,000-year-old explosion crater has steep red and pink slopes of volcanic scoria surrounding a vivid aquamarine lake at its base. The contrast between the red rock, green vegetation and colourful water makes Kerið one of the region's most memorable sights. Part of the wider Grímsnes crater field, it is thought to have formed when a magma chamber emptied and the land subsided, rather than in a single violent eruption.

What to see and do at Kerið

A comfortable walking path runs both around the upper rim of Kerið and down to the water's edge. The rim walk takes only a short while and offers ever-changing views over the colourful walls and the mirror-like lake, which shifts hue with the light and the seasons. Those wanting a closer look can descend the steps to the shoreline. Kerið is an ideal stop for families and travellers short on time, being a short and easy visit. The crater has even served as a striking natural venue for concerts, thanks to its remarkable acoustics.

Kerið's location and how to get there

Kerið lies in the Grímsnes area of South Iceland, beside Route 35, the same road that carries the Golden Circle towards Geysir and Gullfoss. From Reykjavík it is only about an hour's drive to the crater along a good road, making Kerið one of the most accessible stops on the circuit. There is a well-kept car park by the crater and a short walk to the rim. Thanks to its proximity to Selfoss and the other Golden Circle sights, it is easy to combine a visit to Kerið with the region's other natural attractions in a single day.

Best time to visit Kerið

Kerið is open year-round and makes a rewarding short stop in every season. In summer the lake glows bright blue and the vegetation around the crater is green and lush, while autumn colours and winter snow give the site an entirely different, more dramatic character. A small admission fee is charged to support maintenance and conservation of the area. Paths can be slippery when wet or frozen, so good walking shoes are helpful. Arrive early in the day or later in the afternoon to enjoy the crater in greater peace, away from the busiest Golden Circle traffic.
