Reynisfjara og Vík

Famous black-sand beach with basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks.

Reynisfjara is the most striking black-sand beach in the country, with tall basalt columns, the Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore and a puffin colony in summer. Caution: dangerous sneaker waves — keep well away from the sea. The village of Vík is close by.

Reynisfjara – the famous black sand beach on the South Coast

Reynisfjara is Iceland's most striking black sand beach and one of the great highlights of the South Coast, located near the village of Vík í Mýrdal. The beach captivates visitors with its jet-black sand, towering basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks that rise mysteriously out of the ocean. Shaped by volcanic activity and the force of the Atlantic, Reynisfjara has become one of the most photographed places in the country. It is an essential stop for anyone travelling along South Iceland who wants to experience raw, dramatic nature where sea, rock and sky collide.

Things to see and do at Reynisfjara

At Reynisfjara you can admire the magnificent basalt columns of the Hálsanefshellir cave, which form natural pillars resembling a giant pipe organ. The Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore are tied to folk tales of petrified trolls and lend an air of legend to the view. In summer, puffins nest along the grassy cliff tops, offering fine birdwatching. Nearby lies Dyrhólaey, where a huge rock arch and a lighthouse provide sweeping views over the black coastline. The village of Vík is just minutes away, with dining and accommodation on offer.

Location and how to reach Reynisfjara

Reynisfjara lies on the South Coast, about two and a half hours' drive from Reykjavík along the Ring Road, just west of Vík í Mýrdal. Turn off Route 1 onto Road 215, which leads straight down to the car park by the beach. The road is passable year-round by regular car and there are facilities at the beach, including a restaurant and toilets. Reynisfjara is often part of organised day tours of the South Coast alongside the Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss waterfalls, but it also suits travellers exploring on their own.

Best time to visit and a warning about sneaker waves

Reynisfjara is open all year, but the most important thing of all is to take great care near the sea. The beach is notorious for dangerous sneaker waves that can surge far up the sand without warning, even in calm conditions, and drag people into the freezing ocean. Always keep a safe distance from the waves, never turn your back on the sea, and heed the warning signs and light system on site. Dress in warm, waterproof clothing, as the weather can be windy and cold. With proper caution, Reynisfjara is an unforgettable experience in every season.
