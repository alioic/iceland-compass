Eldheimar

A volcano museum in the Westman Islands built around a house buried in ash by the 1973 eruption.

Eldheimar is a powerful volcano museum in the Westman Islands telling the story of the 1973 eruption on Heimaey. The museum is built around a house that was buried in ash and later excavated, offering a striking insight into the night the townspeople had to flee. Interactive displays and personal accounts make the visit moving for all ages.

The house beneath the ash

Eldheimar in the Westman Islands is built around a house buried in ash during the 1973 eruption on Heimaey and later excavated. This unique approach makes the story tangible, as visitors walk through the exhibition and see for themselves how the eruption transformed the town. The museum combines personal accounts, photographs and interactive displays that captivate children and adults alike. A visit to Eldheimar gives a deeper understanding of one of the most dramatic events in twentieth-century Icelandic history.

The night the town fled

At Eldheimar the eruption's story is told from the perspective of residents who had to flee Heimaey overnight when the ground split open. The exhibition captures both the fear and the solidarity of those days, as well as the town's rebuilding once the eruption ended. The accounts make the events personal and moving, helping younger visitors grasp what was at stake. The museum also covers the birth of nearby Surtsey, placing the islands' volcanism in a wider context.

Visiting the Westman Islands

Eldheimar is in the Westman Islands, easily reached by ferry from Landeyjahöfn or by flight from Reykjavík. The museum is indoors, so it works well regardless of weather, an advantage on the windy islands. A visit pairs nicely with other island attractions, such as hikes on the Eldfell volcano and the harbour area. For families it is both an educational and moving way to discover the history and nature of the Westman Islands in one place.
