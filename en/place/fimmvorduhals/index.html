Fimmvörðuháls

Between glaciers, past new craters, from Skógar down into Þórsmörk.

Fimmvörðuháls runs between Eyjafjallajökull and Mýrdalsjökull, past the craters Magni and Móði formed in the 2010 eruption. The trail begins at Skógafoss and follows the river Skógá uphill past countless waterfalls before descending into Þórsmörk.

Fimmvörðuháls – a hike between two glaciers

Fimmvörðuháls is one of Iceland's most spectacular hiking trails, a roughly 25-kilometre route running between the Eyjafjallajökull and Mýrdalsjökull glaciers. The trail combines remarkable variety: a majestic string of waterfalls, recent volcanic terrain, a snow-covered pass and a lush mountain valley. Midway, hikers pass the craters Magni and Móði, formed in the 2010 eruption and still warm underfoot in places. Fimmvörðuháls is demanding but unforgettable, taking walkers from the green valleys of the South Coast up onto bare highland and back down into one of the country's most beautiful oases, Þórsmörk.

Waterfalls, craters and views on Fimmvörðuháls

The trail begins at the magnificent Skógafoss and follows the Skógá river uphill past countless waterfalls, each more beautiful than the last, which many regard as the highlight of the hike. Higher up, bare highland takes over and the path passes the new craters Magni and Móði, where warmth can still be felt in the ground. From there the route crosses the steeper Heljarkambur ridge and descends into Þórsmörk with sweeping views over the valley and surrounding glaciers. Few day hikes offer such a dramatic blend of water, fire, ice and greenery over so short a distance as Fimmvörðuháls.

Location and access to Fimmvörðuháls

Fimmvörðuháls links the South Coast with the highland valley of Þórsmörk. Most hikers start at Skógafoss, right beside the Ring Road about a two-hour drive east of Reykjavík, and finish in Þórsmörk. The route can also be walked in reverse. The Þórsmörk end is only accessible by capable four-wheel-drive vehicles crossing unbridged rivers, so most travellers use scheduled buses to and from Þórsmörk. The trail also connects to the Laugavegur, and many combine both routes into a longer trek from Landmannalaugar all the way down to Skógar.

Best time, difficulty and tips for Fimmvörðuháls

Fimmvörðuháls is a challenging trail passable only in high summer, typically from July through September, once the snow has cleared from the pass. Many walk the route in one long day, but you can also stay overnight in the Baldvinsskáli hut on the pass and split the hike over two days. The trail is intended only for experienced, well-equipped hikers. Weather can change quickly, and snow and fog may linger on the highest section even in summer. Bring windproof and waterproof clothing, warm layers, sturdy boots and food, and check the forecast carefully before departure.
