Mývatn

A volcanic landscape of pseudo-craters, hot springs and natural baths.

The Mývatn area is a unique blend of volcanic activity and birdlife. Here you'll find the pseudo-craters at Skútustaðir, the lava formations of Dimmuborgir, the colourful geothermal field at Námafjall and the Mývatn Nature Baths - a calmer alternative to the Blue Lagoon.

Lake Mývatn – North Iceland's Volcanic Gem

Lake Mývatn is a shallow, bird-rich lake set in an extraordinary volcanic landscape and one of the great highlights of the Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn) in North Iceland. Shaped by volcanic activity and geothermal heat, the area is dotted with pseudocraters, lava formations and rising columns of steam that give it an almost otherworldly feel. The lake is famous for its abundant birdlife, especially the many species of ducks that nest here in summer. Mývatn sits at the centre of a cluster of natural wonders, drawing travellers who want to experience fire, water and wildlife all in one place.

Things to See and Do at Mývatn

The Mývatn area is packed with remarkable natural sights. The pseudocraters at Skútustaðir formed when lava flowed over wetland, and easy walking paths loop around them. Dimmuborgir offers eerie, cathedral-like lava formations and winding trails, while Grjótagjá is a cave sheltering a warm, crystal-clear pool. Hverfjall is a striking explosion crater you can climb for sweeping views over the region. To finish, the Mývatn Nature Baths invite you to soak in mineral-rich, milky-blue geothermal water. Together, these sights make the Mývatn area an essential stop on any North Iceland journey.

Getting to Mývatn on the Diamond Circle

Mývatn lies within easy reach of Akureyri along Route 1, the Ring Road, and forms the very heart of the Diamond Circle in North Iceland. From here, roads continue to Dettifoss, Ásbyrgi and Húsavík, making the area an ideal base from which to plan the loop. The roads circling the lake are well maintained and connect the main attractions, including Skútustaðir, Dimmuborgir and Hverfjall. Reykjahlíð is the area's main service hub, offering accommodation and dining. Its central location makes Mývatn a perfect overnight stop for travellers wanting to explore the north at a relaxed pace.

Best Time to Visit Mývatn and Practical Tips

Summer is the most popular time to visit Mývatn, when birdlife is at its peak, walking trails are open and daylight lasts around the clock. Keep in mind, though, the midges that give the lake its name; a head net can be worth its weight in gold on still summer days. Winter brings a quiet, serene beauty and good chances of seeing the northern lights, but requires warm clothing and attention to road conditions. The Mývatn Nature Baths are wonderful in any season and offer the perfect way to unwind after a full day of exploring.
