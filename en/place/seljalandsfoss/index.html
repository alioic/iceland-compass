Seljalandsfoss

The waterfall you can walk behind, with the hidden Gljúfrabúi next door.

Seljalandsfoss is a roughly 60-metre waterfall beneath Eyjafjöll, fed by glacial meltwater from Eyjafjallajökull as it drops over an ancient sea cliff. What makes it unique is that a path leads all the way behind the cascade into the hollow behind it. A short walk to the north hides its neighbour Gljúfrabúi, tucked away inside a narrow canyon. The path is often wet and slippery from the spray, so good footwear and waterproofs are essential. A parking fee is charged at the site.

Seljalandsfoss – the waterfall you can walk behind

Seljalandsfoss is one of Iceland's most distinctive waterfalls, about sixty metres high, where glacial meltwater from Eyjafjallajökull drops over an ancient sea cliff on the South Coast. What makes it unique is the path that leads all the way behind the cascade, letting visitors walk behind the falls and look out through a misty wall of water. The waterfall sits just off the ring road, a little west of Skógafoss, so it is easy to see both on the same trip. Seljalandsfoss is an unmissable stop and one of the most photographed sites in the country, especially when the evening sun lights up the spray.

Gljúfrabúi – the hidden neighbour in the canyon

A few hundred metres north of Seljalandsfoss hides Gljúfrabúi, a waterfall that many travellers walk straight past without noticing. It tumbles into a narrow canyon and stays largely concealed behind a rock face, but you can reach the chamber by squeezing through a slim opening and wading across a shallow stream. Inside, a mossy hollow opens around the cascade as it pours down from above. Be ready to get wet on the way in and wear waterproof footwear. Together, Seljalandsfoss and Gljúfrabúi form a remarkable pair of waterfalls beneath Eyjafjöll well worth exploring.

Location, parking and access at Seljalandsfoss

Seljalandsfoss lies beneath Eyjafjöll right by the ring road, about a two-hour drive from Reykjavík toward the South Coast. The waterfall is visible from the road and only a short walk from the car park, which charges a parking fee that usually covers the whole day. Access is good all year in an ordinary car, and the falls are a fixture on most South Coast day tours. From the car park it is also a short walk north to Gljúfrabúi, so it makes sense to allow plenty of time to see both waterfalls and enjoy the area at a relaxed pace.

Practical tips and safety at Seljalandsfoss

The path behind Seljalandsfoss is often wet and slippery from the constant spray, so good hiking shoes with grip and waterproof clothing are essential for an enjoyable visit. Take extra care in winter, when the path can turn icy and dangerous and the walk behind the falls is sometimes closed for safety reasons. Cameras and phones need protection from the mist. Arrive early in the day or in the evening to avoid the biggest crowds and enjoy the calm, and give yourself time to explore Gljúfrabúi nearby while you are in the area.
