Saga Centre

A saga exhibition in Hvolsvöllur dedicated to Njáls saga, with a Viking hall and a giant tapestry.

The Saga Centre in Hvolsvöllur is a museum dedicated to Njáls saga, one of the best-known Icelandic sagas, and the events that unfolded in the Rangárvellir region. Visitors walk through an exhibition of artefacts and images and a Viking hall that brings the saga's world to life. It is also home to the ambitious Njála tapestry, a long embroidered artwork that guests can even help create.

Njáls saga comes to life

The Saga Centre in Hvolsvöllur is an ideal destination for those wanting to discover the Icelandic sagas, especially Njáls saga, which unfolds across the Rangárvellir region. In the exhibition, visitors walk through halls of artefacts and images and a Viking hall that makes the saga's setting tangible. This turns a complex, powerful narrative into something accessible for children and adults alike. For families touring South Iceland, a visit to the Saga Centre is a fun way to connect the landscape with the ancient stories that took place there.

The Njála tapestry and the Viking age

Among the things that make the Saga Centre special is the Njála tapestry, an ambitious embroidered work depicting the events of Njáls saga in images. The piece has been created with the participation of many people, and visitors can even lend a hand. The Viking hall offers insight into the dwellings and lifestyle of early Icelanders and enriches the experience. It combines craft, history and art in one place. For children it is a lively, interactive way to discover the Viking age and the heritage held in the sagas.

Visiting Hvolsvöllur

The Saga Centre is centrally located in Hvolsvöllur along Route 1, making it easy to visit while travelling the south coast. The museum is indoors and works well regardless of weather. A visit pairs nicely with other attractions in the area and gives cultural context to the landscape where Njáls saga takes place. It is worth checking opening hours before you go. For families wanting to blend history, culture and entertainment, the Saga Centre is an engaging stop that deepens understanding of Icelandic heritage.
