Reykjavík

The world's northernmost capital, buzzing with culture, design and nightlife.

Reykjavík is a vibrant cultural city with colourful houses, restaurants and a wealth of museums. Hallgrímskirkja towers over the town centre, and its tower offers sweeping views across the city. The Harpa concert hall, the Sun Voyager sculpture and the old harbour are among its main landmarks.

Reykjavik – the World's Northernmost Capital and Heart of Iceland

Reykjavik is Iceland's capital and the northernmost capital city in the world, a vibrant cultural hub where colourful houses, design and a lively nightlife meet the sea and the surrounding mountains. Around two thirds of the nation lives in the greater Reykjavik area, and the city is the starting point for most trips around Iceland. Hallgrimskirkja towers over the centre, and its tower offers sweeping views over the rooftops, Faxafloi bay and Mount Esja. Reykjavik blends small-town ease with a cosmopolitan feel, with museums, restaurants, pools and untouched nature all close at hand.

Top Things to See and Do in Reykjavik

There is plenty to explore in Reykjavik year-round. Laugavegur and the downtown streets buzz with shops, cafes and restaurants, while the Harpa concert hall lends the old harbour a glittering glass facade. The Sun Voyager sculpture on the waterfront is one of the city's best-loved landmarks, and Perlan features exhibitions on Icelandic nature plus a viewing deck. Don't miss the geothermal pools, the museums and the downtown food scene. In winter, northern lights tours are easy to arrange, and whale watching from the old harbour rounds out the itinerary all year.

Getting to Reykjavik – Location and Access

Reykjavik sits on Faxafloi bay in southwest Iceland and is the country's transport hub. From Keflavik International Airport it is roughly a 45–50 minute drive or an easy airport-bus transfer into the city. Domestic flights use Reykjavik Airport, right beside the centre. Within the city it is easy to walk between the main landmarks, and the Straeto bus network links the neighbourhoods. Reykjavik is also the natural base for the Golden Circle, the Reykjanes Peninsula and the South Coast, so many visitors stay in the city and take day trips from there.

Best Time to Visit Reykjavik and Practical Tips

Reykjavik welcomes visitors all year. Summer (June–August) brings the midnight sun, milder weather and numerous festivals, while winter offers festive charm, cosy cafes and northern lights on clear nights. Spring and autumn are quieter and often better value. Pack wind- and waterproof clothing whatever the season, as the weather changes quickly. Book accommodation early during peak months, enjoy the geothermal pools like the locals do, and remember that the compact city centre is best explored on foot.
