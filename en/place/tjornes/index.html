Tjörnes — a fossil paradise between Skjálfandi and Öxarfjörður

Tjörnes is a peninsula north of Húsavík in North Iceland, best known for rock strata that preserve shell fossils millions of years old. It is among the country's most celebrated fossil sites and a unique window into Iceland's geological history and the climate shifts of the distant past. Reaching out to sea between the bays of Skjálfandi and Öxarfjörður, Tjörnes is dotted with lighthouses and bird cliffs and offers sweeping views toward Grímsey and the mountains of Northeast Iceland. For lovers of geology, nature and quiet, Tjörnes is an unforgettable stop close to the whale-watching town of Húsavík.

What to see at Tjörnes — fossils, birds and lighthouses

At Tjörnes you can examine the famous sedimentary layers of shell fossils that record the story of the sea millions of years ago — a natural book written in the rock. The bird cliffs teem with seabirds in summer, and the lighthouses on the peninsula give the landscape its character. From Tjörnes there are magnificent views over Skjálfandi bay, out to Grímsey and toward the mountains of Northeast Iceland. Gentle trails and viewpoints make the peninsula ideal for those seeking nature away from the crowds. Remember that the fossils are protected and may not be removed — look, photograph and leave them in place.

Getting to Tjörnes — location near Húsavík

Tjörnes lies just north of Húsavík, making it easy to visit as part of a tour of Northeast Iceland. You drive north from Húsavík along the road that crosses the peninsula, a route that links Skjálfandi and Öxarfjörður on the way to Ásbyrgi and the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon. The drive from Húsavík takes only a short while and the road is comfortable for a standard car in summer. Tjörnes makes an excellent stop on the so-called Diamond Circle, between Húsavík, Ásbyrgi and Lake Mývatn, for travellers wanting to add a geological gem to their route.

Best time to visit Tjörnes and practical tips

Summer and autumn are best for visiting Tjörnes, when roads are clear, birdlife is at its peak and the light is good for studying the rock layers and enjoying the views. Bring sturdy shoes, as access to the fossil beds can be rocky and steep near the shore, and take care close to cliff edges. Check the weather and tides before walking down to the beach. Remember the fossils are protected; respect the landscape, leave no trace and take nothing but photographs and memories from this remarkable geological site.
