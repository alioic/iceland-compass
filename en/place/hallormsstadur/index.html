Hallormsstaður og Lagarfljót

The country's largest forest beside the legend-rich lake Lagarfljót.

Hallormsstaðaskógur is Iceland's largest and oldest national forest, with numerous walking trails and tree species. It lies beside Lagarfljót, where legend says the Lagarfljót Worm dwells.

Hallormsstaður and Lagarfljót – Iceland's largest forest

Hallormsstaður is home to Iceland's largest and oldest national forest, a lush green oasis on the banks of the storied lake Lagarfljót in East Iceland. The woodland shelters dozens of tree species, a network of marked walking trails and an established arboretum where visitors can explore the diversity of Icelandic and imported forestry. According to old folklore, the Lagarfljót Worm, a serpent-like creature, dwells in the lake's deep waters, lending the place an air of mystery. Hallormsstaður is a rewarding stop for anyone wanting to experience the softer, greener side of East Iceland's nature.

Things to see and do at Hallormsstaður

Hallormsstaður offers a wide range of forest walks, from short family-friendly paths to longer trails climbing the hillsides. The arboretum showcases a rich variety of native and foreign tree species, and Atlavík is a popular campsite and lakeside beach on Lagarfljót. Nearby, Hengifoss, one of Iceland's tallest waterfalls, makes an excellent day hike with views of striking red rock layers. Visitors can also enjoy panoramas over Lagarfljót, cycle around the area, or keep a watchful eye out for the legendary Lagarfljót Worm across the water.

How to get to Hallormsstaður

Hallormsstaður sits in the Fljótsdalshérað district of East Iceland, about a half-hour drive south of Egilsstaðir along the western shore of Lagarfljót. The road is easy to drive and winds through beautiful woodland and countryside. From Egilsstaðir, the region's transport hub with an airport and a link to the Ring Road, it is simple to plan a day trip or an overnight stay in the area. Many travellers combine a visit with a loop through East Iceland, where Hallormsstaður and Lagarfljót form a green contrast to the surrounding fjords and highlands.

Best time to visit Hallormsstaður and practical tips

Hallormsstaður forest is at its finest from May to October, lush and green in summer and glowing with yellow and red autumn colours in September. Summer is ideal for hiking, outdoor recreation and camping at Atlavík, while autumn brings vivid foliage and quiet trails. In winter the area is snow-covered and peaceful, though access can be more limited. Bring sturdy walking shoes, insect repellent for still summer days, and plenty of time to enjoy the woods; it pairs perfectly with the hike to Hengifoss nearby.
