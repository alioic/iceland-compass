Stórurð

An adventurous valley of giant boulders and turquoise pools beneath Dyrfjöll.

Stórurð is one of the most distinctive hiking destinations in the country — a valley full of house-sized tuff boulders, soft grassy meadows and bright turquoise pools beneath the peaked Dyrfjöll. The route starts from Vatnsskarð or Borgarfjörður eystri.

Stórurð – a magical hiking destination beneath the Dyrfjöll

Stórurð is one of Iceland's most extraordinary and beautiful hiking destinations, a hidden valley beneath the jagged Dyrfjöll peaks in East Iceland. Here, house-sized boulders of tuff, soft green meadows and vivid turquoise pools form a fairy-tale landscape unlike anywhere else in the country. The giant rocks tumbled from the mountains at the end of the last Ice Age, creating this remarkable maze of stone and water. For hikers and nature lovers exploring the East Fjord mountains, Stórurð is an unforgettable gem and a highlight of outdoor life in East Iceland.

Things to see and do at Stórurð

At Stórurð the main experience is the hike itself, where the trail winds among enormous tuff boulders, green meadows and alongside vivid turquoise pools with views toward the Dyrfjöll. The rocks are so large you can walk between them as if through a natural labyrinth. Photography here is spectacular, thanks to the unique colours and forms. Many hikers bring a packed lunch and rest beside the pools. The area is ideal for a summer day hike, where tranquillity, vivid colours and dramatic mountain scenery come together in one unforgettable place.

How to get to Stórurð

Stórurð lies in East Iceland, between Egilsstaðir and Borgarfjörður eystri, and the valley is reached on foot from two main routes. Most hikers set off from Vatnsskarð pass on the road to Borgarfjörður eystri, though it is also possible to walk from Borgarfjörður eystri itself. From Egilsstaðir it is a short drive to the trailheads. The loop is roughly 11 to 20 kilometres depending on the route and typically takes six to eight hours. The hike is part of the wider Dyrfjöll and Víknaslóðir hiking paradise and calls for good preparation and proper equipment.

Best time to visit Stórurð and practical tips

The best time to hike Stórurð is high summer, from early July into September, when the snow has cleared from the trail and the paths are passable. Outside this window, snow can linger in the valley and make the hike dangerous. The route is moderate to demanding, so bring sturdy hiking boots, warm windproof clothing, food and plenty of water. Watch the forecast closely, as weather in the Dyrfjöll can change quickly. Set out early, respect the marked trails, and give yourself plenty of time to enjoy this truly unique landscape.
