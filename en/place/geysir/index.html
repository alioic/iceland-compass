Geysir og Strokkur

The original erupting hot spring — Strokkur erupts every few minutes.

Haukadalur is home to the original Geysir, which gave its name to every erupting hot spring in the world. Although it is now largely dormant, its neighbour Strokkur shoots 20–30 m into the air every 5–10 minutes. A colourful geothermal area of boiling pools surrounds the site.

Geysir and Strokkur – the erupting hot springs of the Golden Circle

In the Haukadalur valley of South Iceland stands the original Geysir, the geyser that gave all the world's geysers their name. Although Geysir itself is now largely dormant, its neighbour Strokkur keeps the show going, shooting a column of water twenty to thirty metres into the air every few minutes. The geothermal field is one of the most popular stops on the Golden Circle, captivating visitors with its hot springs, boiling pools and the raw power of Iceland's volcanic underground. Geysir is a textbook example of the country's high-temperature geothermal activity.

What to see at the Geysir geothermal area

Besides Strokkur, which erupts reliably every five to ten minutes, visitors can explore colourful springs such as Blesi and Konungshver, where clear boiling water and sulphur deposits create an otherworldly landscape. Marked paths wind across the field and viewpoints let guests watch the eruptions from a safe distance. Across the road, the Geysir Centre offers exhibits about the area's geothermal forces, along with dining and souvenirs. The site is family-friendly and easy to explore for visitors of all ages, making it a rewarding stop for everyone.

Location and how to reach Geysir

Geysir sits in the Haukadalur valley of South Iceland, about an hour and a half's drive from Reykjavík along Road 35. The geothermal field is the second major stop on the Golden Circle, located between Þingvellir and Gullfoss, making it easy to combine all three in a single day trip. The road is passable year-round and there is a large free car park at the Geysir Centre. Numerous coach and group tours from the capital include Geysir, so the area is accessible whether you travel independently or with a guide.

Best time to visit Geysir and helpful tips

The Geysir area is open year-round and Strokkur erupts in any weather, so a visit rewards you in every season. In winter the steam can look even more dramatic against the cold and the field is beautiful under snow, while summer brings mild weather and long daylight hours. Always stay on the marked paths, as the surrounding ground is scalding hot and dangerous. Stand with the wind at your back to avoid the hot spray, and keep your camera ready, because the eruptions happen fast and without warning.
