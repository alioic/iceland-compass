Skógafoss – a 60-metre waterfall on Iceland's South Coast

Skógafoss is one of Iceland's best-known and most impressive waterfalls, standing sixty metres high and roughly twenty-five metres wide, where the Skógá river spills over an ancient sea cliff beside the village of Skógar beneath Eyjafjöll. The falls lie just off the ring road and make an essential stop on any journey along the South Coast. The power of the water, the drifting spray and the lush green surroundings make Skógafoss one of the most photographed destinations in the country. On sunny days rainbows regularly form in the mist rising from the base, lending the site a magical atmosphere year-round.

The staircase, viewing platform and Fimmvörðuháls

At Skógafoss you can walk right up to the base and feel the spray swirl around you, or climb the staircase beside the falls to a viewing platform on the rim. From there the view opens across the South Coast, and this is where the famous Fimmvörðuháls hiking trail begins, running between the Eyjafjallajökull and Mýrdalsjökull glaciers over to Þórsmörk. The route is one of Iceland's most popular summer day hikes but demands good preparation and gear. Visitors who simply want to enjoy the waterfall can let the staircase suffice and take in the view over the Skógá valley below.

The legend of Þrasi's treasure chest

Skógafoss is wrapped in folklore, the most famous tale telling of the settler Þrasi Þórólfsson, who is said to have hidden a chest full of gold and treasures behind the cascade. According to the story, one side of the chest could once be glimpsed through the spray, but when people tried to retrieve it, only the chest's ring came loose. The ring is said to be preserved and linked to the Skógar folk museum, which stands a short distance from the waterfall. The legend gives Skógafoss an air of mystery that adds to the appeal of this majestic South Coast landmark.

Location, access and practical tips for Skógafoss

Skógafoss sits by the village of Skógar beneath Eyjafjöll, about a two-hour drive from Reykjavík along the ring road. The waterfall is only a few minutes' walk from the car park and access is good all year in an ordinary car. Be prepared to get wet from the spray as you approach the base, and dress in waterproof layers with sturdy hiking shoes, especially if you plan to climb the steps or set off on Fimmvörðuháls. Take care on the staircase in winter as it can become icy, and arrive early in the day to enjoy the calm before the crowds.
