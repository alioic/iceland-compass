Heiðmörk

The capital's largest recreation area, with woodland and lava formations.

Heiðmörk is a vast nature reserve and forestry area on the edge of Reykjavík, popular for walking, cycling and outdoor recreation. Rauðhólar, a cluster of red scoria craters, is among the area's finest natural gems.

Heidmork – the Capital's Largest Outdoor Recreation Area

Heidmork is an expansive nature and forestry reserve on the edge of Reykjavik and the greater capital area's largest outdoor paradise. Here larch and pine woods alternate with open heather slopes, lava formations and a wealth of walking trails that make Heidmork a popular destination for city dwellers all year. The area is a welcome refuge for reconnecting with nature without travelling far from the city. Raudholar, a cluster of red scoria craters, is among Heidmork's finest natural gems and gives the landscape a striking character.

Top Things to See and Do in Heidmork

Heidmork offers activities for the whole family. A dense network of walking and cycling paths runs through the woods and lava, and Raudholar features distinctive reddish scoria craters that are fun to explore. Lake Elliðavatn lies at the edge of the area and makes a beautiful viewpoint and resting spot. In autumn Heidmork fills with berries and colour, while year-round the area is ideal for walking, cycling, picnics and time outdoors. Its calm atmosphere and varied nature make every visit memorable.

Getting to Heidmork – Location and Access

Heidmork lies on the southeastern edge of Reykjavik, above Lake Elliðavatn, only about a 15–20 minute drive from downtown. Several car parks serve the main trailheads, including near Raudholar and Elliðavatn. Many people cycle from the city along the paths beside the Elliðaar river. Some roads within the area are partly gravel, so drive carefully. As Heidmork is large, it pays to study a trail map in advance and choose a route to match your time and ability.

Best Time to Visit Heidmork and Practical Tips

Heidmork is open and appealing all year. Summer brings lush woodland, long days and countless trails, while autumn dazzles with colour and berry-picking. In winter you can walk or ski through the snow-covered forest in peace. Dress for the weather and bring water and snacks, as there is little service within the area. Respect the reserve and its vegetation, keep dogs on a leash, and carry your rubbish back out. A trail map makes it easy to pick a route that suits you.
