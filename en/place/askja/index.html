Askja — a colossal volcano in the heart of the highlands

Askja is a spectacular caldera in the Dyngjufjöll mountains at the heart of the Icelandic highlands, within Northeast Iceland and surrounded by the vast Ódáðahraun lava desert. This immense volcano is one of the great gems of the central highlands, with a lunar, awe-inspiring landscape that has few equals. Within the caldera lies Öskjuvatn, the deepest lake in Iceland, and the warm crater lake of Víti, where you can bathe. The terrain is so otherworldly that Apollo astronauts trained here for their missions to the Moon. Askja is an unforgettable destination for adventurous travellers.

What to see and do at Askja

The most popular experience at Askja is the hike to the crater lake of Víti, where you can bathe in the warm, milky-blue water amid a sulphurous setting, with the depths of Öskjuvatn close by. Nearby lies Drekagil, a rugged gorge with a waterfall, where a mountain hut and campsite offer accommodation for travellers. In the distance rises the queen of Icelandic mountains, Herðubreið, a majestic table mountain that dominates the whole area. Highland jeep tours, hiking and photography are among the main activities in this stunning and remote wilderness of the central highlands.

Getting to Askja in the highlands

Askja lies deep in the central highlands and is reachable only by highland roads that require well-equipped four-wheel-drive vehicles, as the route crosses unbridged rivers. The usual approach follows the Askja route to Dreki by Drekagil, either from the Mývatn area or via other highland tracks. From there it is a short drive or walk to the car park, and then a trail to Víti and Öskjuvatn. Because of the conditions, many travellers choose a guided jeep tour from Akureyri or Mývatn. Preparation and experience with highland driving are essential for such a journey.

Best time to visit Askja and safety tips

Askja is accessible only during high summer, typically from July to September, once the highland roads have opened and the rivers have become passable. Outside this window the area is closed due to snow and impassable tracks. Always check road information and the weather forecast, as conditions can change quickly in the highlands. Bring ample supplies, fuel, warm clothing and good equipment, and let someone know your travel plans. Bathing in Víti is at your own risk and care is needed on the steep crater slope. A guided tour is a safe and comfortable way to experience this unique volcano.
