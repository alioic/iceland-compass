Hornvík og Hornbjarg

A multi-day trek through the untouched Hornstrandir to the highest bird cliffs in the country.

The hike across Hornstrandir to Hornvík is among the most adventurous treks in the country - no roads, no services, just a tent, the tame fox and the sheer cliff edges of Hornbjarg and Hælavíkurbjarg. You arrive by boat from Ísafjörður at the starting point.

Hornvík and Hornbjarg – A Wilderness Trek Across Untouched Hornstrandir

Hornvík, at the foot of the towering Hornbjarg cliff, is among the most famous destinations in Hornstrandir, the untouched nature reserve at the northernmost tip of the Westfjords. No roads reach the area and there are no permanent services, only wilderness, tent sites and sheer clifftops. Long abandoned by settlers, Hornstrandir has been left to evolve on nature's own terms, making a hike here a truly rare experience. For seasoned trekkers, a multi-day trek to Hornvík ranks among Iceland's most spectacular routes, defined by silence, seabird cliffs and famously tame Arctic foxes.

Things to See and Do in Hornvík

The highlight of the trek is Hornbjarg, one of Iceland's tallest and most majestic bird cliffs, rising vertically from the sea and teeming with seabirds. Nearby stands Hælavíkurbjarg, another dramatic cliff, while trails continue on to Hlöðuvík and other bays. The Arctic fox of Hornstrandir is unusually unafraid of people and can often be watched at close range. Hikers camp within the reserve, carry all their gear on their backs, and enjoy a sense of solitude and open space with few parallels anywhere in Europe.

How to Get to Hornvík

Hornvík can be reached only by boat, as no roads lead into the Hornstrandir reserve. Scheduled boats depart from Ísafjörður, and sometimes from Bolungarvík or Norðurfjörður on the Strandir coast, to trailheads within the reserve. From there hikers must rely entirely on their own feet and carry everything with them, since there are no roads, shops or emergency services on site. It is essential to book the boat in advance and coordinate both drop-off and pick-up, as the whole journey depends on weather, sea conditions and the boat's schedule.

Best Time to Visit and How to Prepare for the Hornstrandir Trek

The route to Hornvík is only passable in high summer, mainly from late June through August, once the snow has melted and boat services are running. This is a demanding multi-day trek for experienced hikers, and preparation is everything. Bring a sturdy tent, a warm sleeping bag, quality waterproof clothing and enough food for the entire trip, as no services exist in the area. Watch the weather forecast closely, respect the reserve's rules and leave no trace. Careful planning and respect for nature are the keys to a safe and memorable journey.
