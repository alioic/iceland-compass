Snæfellsjökull – The Mystical Glacier at the Tip of Snæfellsnes

Snæfellsjökull is an ancient volcanic cone crowned with a glacier, rising over the westernmost point of the Snæfellsnes Peninsula and forming the centrepiece of its national park. Shrouded in mystique, the mountain became world-famous as the gateway to the centre of the Earth in Jules Verne's novel "Journey to the Center of the Earth." Around the glacier lies a dramatic landscape of lava fields, lava caves, black beaches and old fishing villages such as Arnarstapi and Hellnar. A defining landmark of West Iceland, Snæfellsjökull is visible from Reykjavík across the bay on clear days.

What to See and Do at Snæfellsjökull

At Snæfellsjökull you can join a guided glacier hike to the summit, explore the Vatnshellir lava cave on a guided tour, and walk the beautiful coastal path between Arnarstapi and Hellnar. Djúpalónssandur offers black pebbles, sea-carved rock stacks and the remains of a shipwreck. The national park shelters diverse birdlife, seals and sweeping ocean views. This corner of Snæfellsnes is perfect for hiking, caving, photography, and for experiencing the energy and mystery long associated with Snæfellsjökull glacier.

How to Get to Snæfellsjökull on the Snæfellsnes Peninsula

Snæfellsjökull sits at the western tip of the Snæfellsnes Peninsula in West Iceland, about two to two-and-a-half hours' drive from Reykjavík. Head through Borgarnes and continue along the Snæfellsnes road (Route 54) to the national park, where a loop road (Route 574) circles the glacier's base past the main highlights. Service centres are found at Hellnar and elsewhere. Snæfellsjökull works both as a long day trip from the capital and as the highlight of a full loop around Snæfellsnes, often called "Iceland in Miniature" for its variety.

Best Time to Visit Snæfellsjökull and Practical Tips

The national park is open all year, but glacier hikes on Snæfellsjökull are mainly offered from May to September when conditions are safest. In summer everything is accessible and daylight lingers late into the evening, while winter brings a snow-covered glacier and chances of northern lights. Never venture onto the glacier alone – always go with a certified guide and proper equipment. Dress in layers of warm, windproof and waterproof clothing. Combine your visit with Arnarstapi, Hellnar and Djúpalónssandur on the Snæfellsnes Peninsula.
