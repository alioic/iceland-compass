Grímsey

Iceland's only inhabited settlement on the Arctic Circle.

Grímsey is a small island 40 km off the coast and the only place in Iceland crossed by the Arctic Circle. The spherical monument Orbis et Globus marks the circle. The island teems with seabirds, not least puffins.

Grímsey – Iceland's Only Inhabited Spot on the Arctic Circle

Grímsey is a small, captivating island about 40 kilometres off Iceland's north coast and the only place in the country that the Arctic Circle actually crosses. That makes it a bucket-list destination for travellers who want to set foot inside the Arctic Circle. The island is home to a small but resilient fishing community that welcomes visitors warmly. The spherical monument Orbis et Globus marks the line and is moved slightly each year to track the circle's natural drift. Grímsey combines a unique geographic position, rich birdlife and the genuine tranquillity of a remote ocean island.

Things to See and Do in Grímsey

The highlight of any visit to Grímsey is walking to the Arctic Circle and the Orbis et Globus stone sphere, where many visitors receive a certificate confirming they have crossed the line. The island is a paradise for birdwatchers, with bustling seabird colonies where the puffin steals the show in summer, alongside kittiwakes, guillemots and more. The cliffs around Básavík and the rugged shoreline offer spectacular views. Gentle walking paths cross the island, giving visitors the chance to take in the wide horizons, the sea breeze and the extraordinary calm of the Arctic Ocean.

How to Get to Grímsey

Grímsey is reached by ferry or plane from North Iceland. The Sæfari ferry sails from Dalvík on Eyjafjörður, a crossing of about three hours over open sea. Alternatively you can fly from Akureyri in roughly half an hour, enjoying views of islands and coastline along the way. Both options are part of the adventure of visiting this remote ocean island. Because schedules depend on weather and sea conditions, it is important to check timetables carefully and allow flexibility in your plans, as the journey itself is an essential part of the experience.

Best Time to Visit Grímsey and Practical Tips

The best time to visit Grímsey is from May to August, when puffins and other seabirds have settled on the cliffs and the midnight sun bathes the island in light around the clock. During these months ferry and flight connections are most frequent and conditions most favourable. Dress in warm, windproof and waterproof layers, as it can be cold and blustery out at sea. Book transport and accommodation in advance, bring supplies with you and allow plenty of time, because Grímsey is all about slowing down and savouring its unique position on the Arctic Circle.
