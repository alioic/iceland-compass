Drangey – The Saga Island of Grettir in Skagafjörður

Drangey is an isolated, sheer-sided tuff island rising majestically from the middle of Skagafjörður in North Iceland. The island is famous from Grettir's Saga as the hideout of the outlaw Grettir Ásmundarson, who spent his final years there with his brother. The cliffs surrounding the island teem with birdlife and have been used for centuries to gather eggs and seabirds. Drangey combines dramatic scenery, rich birdlife and one of the best-known tales of the Icelandic sagas, making it a unique and memorable destination for anyone drawn to the wild beauty of Skagafjörður.

Things to See and Do on Drangey

A guided boat tour out to Drangey is the highlight of any visit, as guests sail across the fjord and climb to the island's rim along steep, thrilling paths with the help of a guide. The bird cliffs are spectacular, home to puffins, guillemots and other species nesting in the sheer rock. On the mainland nearby lies Grettislaug, a natural hot pool by the shore where, legend says, Grettir bathed after his famous swim. Together, the boat tour, the birdwatching and the hot pool create an unforgettable experience.

How to Get to Drangey

Drangey can be reached only by boat, and guided boat tours run in summer from Skagafjörður, including from Sauðárkrókur and Reykir on the Reykjaströnd coast. The crossing over the fjord is part of the experience, offering beautiful views of the island and the surrounding mountains. The climb up the island is steep and requires a guide, as the paths lead through cliff bands. Booking the boat tour in advance is essential. The nearest town with accommodation and services is Sauðárkrókur, which makes an ideal base for the visit.

Best Time to Visit Drangey and Practical Tips

The best time to visit Drangey is high summer, mainly from June through August, when boat tours run and birdlife is at its peak. This is the seabird nesting season, so the cliffs are alive with activity and the experience is at its most striking. The climb up the island is steep and can be demanding, so good hiking shoes and warm, waterproof clothing are essential. Bring swimwear too if you want to bathe in Grettislaug after the trip. Book early, as seats on the boat tours are limited during peak season.
