Laufás

A beautiful old parsonage turf house by Eyjafjörður.

Laufás on the eastern side of Eyjafjörður is a splendid gabled turf house from the 19th century, a former parsonage and now a museum. The house stands in green surroundings by the river and gives a good picture of the life of wealthier farmers in earlier times. A turf church stands nearby.

Laufás by Eyjafjörður — a beautiful turf parsonage and museum

Laufás, on the eastern shore of Eyjafjörður, is one of Iceland's finest gabled turf farmhouses, a former parsonage now preserved as a living heritage museum in North Iceland. Dating from the 19th century, the turf house offers a rare glimpse into the life of prosperous farmers and clergy of earlier times, with its grassy roofs and row of gables facing the yard. Laufás is among the country's best-known turf farms and a symbol of Icelandic building heritage. It stands in green surroundings by the river, close to Akureyri, and is a popular stop for anyone keen to explore the history and culture of Eyjafjörður.

What to see at Laufás — turf house, church and interiors

A visit to Laufás leads guests through the old turf farmhouse, where the living quarters, kitchen and pantry are preserved with original furnishings that tell the story of everyday life. Nearby stands Laufáskirkja, a lovely old church with notable artefacts, and the setting invites a quiet stroll across the grassy meadow by the river. This museum in Grýtubakkahreppur is ideal for families and anyone interested in history, folk traditions and architecture. Here you can sense the atmosphere of bygone centuries and understand how turf houses kept warmth and shelter in Iceland's harsh climate.

Getting to Laufás — location near Akureyri

Laufás lies on the eastern shore of Eyjafjörður, only about 30 kilometres north of Akureyri, making it an easy day trip from the capital of North Iceland. You drive north along Eyjafjörður through Grýtubakkahreppur, and the route is comfortable for a standard car in summer. Laufás makes an excellent stop on the way to Grenivík or as part of a wider tour of Eyjafjörður. There is parking by the museum, and the location makes Laufás an easily reached destination for travellers exploring the Eyjafjörður area who want to add a historic site to their itinerary.

Best time to visit Laufás and practical tips

Laufás is mainly open during the summer season, when the turf house and museum welcome visitors, so it is best to plan your trip from spring through autumn. Check the museum's opening hours in advance, as they can vary from year to year. Allow at least one to two hours to fully enjoy the farmhouse, the church and the surroundings. Dress for the weather, as North Iceland summers can be cool, and bear in mind that this is a fragile piece of cultural heritage to be treated with respect and care.
