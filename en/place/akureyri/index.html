Akureyri — the capital of the north on Eyjafjörður

Akureyri is the capital of the north and the largest town in Iceland outside the capital region, beautifully set at the head of Eyjafjörður, the country's longest fjord, beneath tall and majestic mountains. The town is known for its warm and lively cultural scene, a cosy town centre, a lush botanical garden and varied activities year-round. As the hub of services, shopping and tourism in North Iceland, Akureyri is an ideal base for exploring natural wonders such as Goðafoss, Mývatn and Húsavík. A blend of town atmosphere and dramatic surroundings makes Akureyri an essential destination.

What to see and do in Akureyri

In Akureyri, visitors can admire the striking Akureyri Church towering above town, stroll through the Botanical Garden, one of the world's most northerly, and enjoy a lively centre full of restaurants, cafés, museums and shops. In winter, the ski area at Hlíðarfjall is among the best in the country, and the town's swimming pools are popular all year. Families enjoy attractions such as museums and gardens, while food lovers find a diverse choice of restaurants. From the harbour you can also take whale-watching and boat trips on Eyjafjörður. Akureyri combines culture, cuisine and the outdoors in one town.

Getting to Akureyri in North Iceland

Akureyri lies on Eyjafjörður in Northeast Iceland and is well connected via the Ring Road. The drive from Reykjavík takes about five hours, but you can also fly between Reykjavík and Akureyri in under an hour, making the town highly accessible. It has a domestic airport and good roads leading in every direction. As the capital of the north, Akureyri is a natural hub for travel around North and Northeast Iceland, with many famous natural wonders and stops on the round-the-island route within easy reach.

Best time to visit Akureyri and practical tips

Akureyri is charming year-round. Summer brings long days, a lush botanical garden, town festivals and prime conditions for touring North Iceland, while winter draws skiers to Hlíðarfjall and offers fine chances to see the northern lights in the darkness. Spring and autumn are quieter and colourful. Book accommodation early during the busy summer season. Dress for changeable weather, and allow at least one to two days to enjoy the town and its surroundings. Akureyri works both as a destination in itself and as a base for adventures across northern Iceland.
