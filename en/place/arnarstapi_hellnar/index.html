Arnarstapi–Hellnar

An easy coastal walk along dramatic basalt columns beneath Snæfellsjökull.

This comfortable coastal route links the fishing villages of Arnarstapi and Hellnar beneath Snæfellsjökull. The path follows a protected shoreline with basalt column ravines, arches and teeming birdlife — with the glacier as a backdrop.

Arnarstapi–Hellnar – a spectacular coastal walk beneath Snæfellsjökull

The walking trail between the fishing hamlets of Arnarstapi and Hellnar is one of the most beautiful and accessible coastal paths on the Snæfellsnes peninsula, at the heart of Snæfellsjökull National Park in West Iceland. The route follows a protected shoreline where the sea has carved dramatic basalt columns, ravines and cliffs, with the Snæfellsjökull glacier rising in the background. This easy, photogenic walk is perfect for the whole family, combining geology, birdlife and folklore in just a few kilometres. Arnarstapi and Hellnar are among the highlights of southern Snæfellsnes and an essential stop on any tour of the peninsula.

Things to see and do on the Arnarstapi–Hellnar trail

Along the way you pass natural wonders such as Gatklettur, a gracefully arched sea rock, deep basalt-column ravines and lively cliffs where seabirds nest. At Arnarstapi stands an imposing stone statue of Bárður Snæfellsás, a half-troll from Icelandic folklore said to be the guardian spirit of the peninsula. Visitors can enjoy cafés and fresh seafood in the villages, explore old jetties and take countless photographs of the interplay between the ocean, the rock formations and the glacier. Birdwatching and the peaceful surroundings make this a truly memorable coastal experience.

Getting to Arnarstapi and Hellnar – location on Snæfellsnes

Arnarstapi and Hellnar sit on the southern side of the Snæfellsnes peninsula, beneath Snæfellsjökull, about two and a half to three hours' drive from Reykjavík. The route runs through Borgarnes and then out onto the peninsula along the Snæfellsnes road. Car parks are available at both Arnarstapi and Hellnar, so walkers can start at one end and continue one way. The trail is roughly five kilometres round trip and takes about an hour and a half at an easy pace. Its location makes the walk a perfect addition to a road trip around Snæfellsnes.

Best time to walk the Arnarstapi–Hellnar trail and practical tips

The coastal path between Arnarstapi and Hellnar is best in spring, summer and autumn, when the trails are dry and birdlife is at its peak, especially from May through August. The route is passable year-round with care, but in winter ice and wet conditions can make the cliff edges slippery. Always keep well back from the edges of the cliffs, which are both high and unstable. Wear sturdy walking shoes and windproof clothing, as the sea air beneath Snæfellsjökull can be cool, and allow plenty of time to enjoy the views.
