Látrabjarg

The westernmost tip of Europe and one of the largest bird cliffs in the world.

Látrabjarg is a 14 km long bird cliff up to 440 m high - the westernmost point of Iceland and Europe. Millions of seabirds nest here, and the puffins are so tame you can get right up close to them. Nearby lies the golden beach of Rauðisandur.

Látrabjarg – the westernmost point of Europe and a spectacular bird cliff

Látrabjarg is one of the greatest bird cliffs in the world and also marks the westernmost point of Iceland and of Europe. The cliff stretches about 14 kilometres and rises to some 440 metres, plunging sheer into the sea on the southern side of the Westfjords. In summer Látrabjarg teems with seabirds, as millions of birds nest on its ledges and grassy edges. The site is a true paradise for birdwatchers and nature lovers and one of the most memorable landmarks in the Westfjords. Few places in Iceland combine such dramatic views, abundant birdlife and the sense of standing at the very edge of the continent.

Things to see and do at Látrabjarg

The main draw at Látrabjarg is the puffin, which is so tame along the cliff edge that visitors can often get remarkably close and capture unforgettable photographs. Alongside puffins, razorbills, common and Brünnich's guillemots, kittiwakes and other seabirds nest here in dense colonies. Bjargtangar lighthouse stands at the westernmost point, marking the edge of the continent. Nearby lies the golden beach of Rauðisandur, one of the most beautiful and unusual beaches in Iceland, with pale sand in a reddish-gold hue. Together, Látrabjarg and Rauðisandur make an irresistible destination in the southern Westfjords.

Getting to Látrabjarg – location in the southern Westfjords

Látrabjarg sits at the far southwestern tip of the Westfjords, and the drive there is part of the experience, as the final stretch follows gravel roads through remote country. From Reykjavík the journey takes considerable time, often via the Baldur ferry across Breiðafjörður or by driving around the fjords. From Patreksfjörður the cliff is a short distance away, and many travellers use the town as a base. The road is usually passable in summer but can be demanding, so careful driving is recommended. The natural gem of Rauðisandur lies in the same area, making the trip even more rewarding.

Best time to visit Látrabjarg and practical tips

The best time to experience Látrabjarg is from May to August, when puffins and other seabirds occupy the cliff and birdlife is at its peak. Summer also brings the longest daylight and the most accessible roads. In autumn the birds leave the cliff, and in winter the roads may be impassable. Take extreme care near the cliff edge, which can be unstable and give way without warning; always keep a safe distance from the edge. Wear warm, windproof clothing and bring supplies, as services are limited in this remote area.
