Súlur — Akureyri's home mountain and a popular day hike

Súlur is the signature mountain of Akureyri, a distinctive twin-peaked summit south of the town and one of the most popular day hikes in North Iceland. Rising above Eyjafjörður, it has long been a favourite outdoor destination for locals — the true home mountain of the capital of the north. Climbing Súlur is both demanding and rewarding, with sweeping views as the payoff for the effort. For anyone wanting to experience a mountain hike near Akureyri without straying far from town, Súlur is a perfect choice and a classic route in Icelandic mountaineering.

The Súlur hike — Ytri-Súla and views over Eyjafjörður

The trail up Súlur climbs through the Glerárdalur valley to Ytri-Súla at 1,144 metres, about 12 kilometres there and back, typically taking five to seven hours. The route is moderate to challenging and best suited to experienced hikers in good condition. From the summit a magnificent panorama opens over Eyjafjörður, Akureyri and the surrounding mountains, all the way to distant Kaldbakur. Lower in Glerárdalur there is also scope for mountain biking. Súlur offers a genuine Icelandic mountain experience with an unforgettable view over the Eyjafjörður area on a clear day.

Getting to Súlur — trailhead from Akureyri

Súlur rises just south of Akureyri and the hike begins close to town, at Glerárdalur, so access is exceptionally good compared with highland treks. From central Akureyri it is only a short drive to the trailhead, making it easy to set off early in the day and be back by mid-afternoon. Its closeness to the capital of the north makes Súlur a convenient mountain hike for anyone staying in Akureyri. Park at the start of the route and follow the marked paths up Glerárdalur toward the summit.

Best time to hike Súlur and practical tips

Summer, from June through September, is the best time to climb Súlur, once the snow has melted and the trail is clear. In winter the hike is only for experienced mountaineers with proper equipment because of snow and ice. Bring food, water, warm and waterproof clothing and good hiking boots, as the weather can change quickly in the mountains. Check the forecast before you set out and allow five to seven hours for the walk. Start early, tell someone your plans, and respect the fragile nature of the Glerárdalur valley.
