Þórsmörk – a lush mountain valley between glaciers

Þórsmörk is one of Iceland's most beautiful and sheltered mountain valleys, set in the southern highlands between the Eyjafjallajökull, Mýrdalsjökull and Tindfjallajökull glaciers. The valley is known for its unusually rich vegetation, birch woodlands, clear streams and stunning views of the glaciers that surround it. Þórsmörk is the famous end point of the Laugavegur trail and the starting point of the Fimmvörðuháls hike, making it a true hikers' paradise. The shelter between the mountains creates a mild climate, turning the valley into a remarkable oasis in the otherwise rugged highlands and an essential destination for nature lovers.

Things to see and do in Þórsmörk

Þórsmörk offers a wealth of varied hiking trails for all abilities. A popular day hike climbs Valahnúkur, with sweeping views over the valley and the glaciers, while the narrow Stakkholtsgjá canyon and the Krossá river strongly shape the landscape. For experienced hikers, Fimmvörðuháls, the route between Eyjafjallajökull and Mýrdalsjökull down to Skógar, is one of the most spectacular trails in the country. The birch woods, moss-covered slopes and glacier views make Þórsmörk a dream location for photographers and outdoor enthusiasts. The huts in Húsadalur and Langadalur provide accommodation and facilities within the valley.

Location and how to reach Þórsmörk

Þórsmörk lies in the highlands north of Eyjafjallajökull and access is challenging. The road into the valley crosses unbridged glacial rivers, including the treacherous Krossá, so only large, powerful four-wheel-drive vehicles with high clearance can make the journey. Attempting the drive in an ordinary rental car is strongly discouraged. The safest and most convenient option for most visitors is to take a specially equipped highland bus from Reykjavík or Hvolsvöllur during the summer. Always check river levels and road conditions before setting off into this remote mountain valley.

Best time to visit Þórsmörk and helpful tips

Þórsmörk is accessible from June to September, when the highland roads and rivers are passable during the summer. The shelter between the glaciers often makes the weather milder than elsewhere in the highlands, but it can still change quickly and the rivers can swell in rain and warm spells. Dress in warm, waterproof layers, bring good hiking boots and your own food, as there is no shop in the valley. Book huts and buses well ahead during the peak season. Allow at least a full day and check the weather and river conditions before you set out.
