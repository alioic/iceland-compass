Jökulsárlón og Breiðamerkursandur

A magical glacier lagoon full of icebergs, with the Diamond Beach alongside.

Jökulsárlón is the deepest lake in the country, filled with blue icebergs that break off Breiðamerkurjökull and drift out to sea. On the Diamond Beach, chunks of ice glitter like diamonds on the black sand. Seals are often seen in the lagoon.

Jökulsárlón in South Iceland: the glacier lagoon of blue icebergs

Jökulsárlón is a spectacular glacier lagoon in South Iceland, at the foot of Breiðamerkurjökull, an outlet glacier of Vatnajökull, Europe's largest ice cap. The lagoon fills with majestic blue and white icebergs that break off the glacier and drift slowly toward the sea, where they melt or float out into the Atlantic. It is one of the country's most popular and beautiful natural wonders, often called the crown jewel of South Iceland (Suðurland), and it sits on the edge of Vatnajökull National Park. The deep blue hues of the ice, the mirror-still water and seals swimming among the bergs make Jökulsárlón unforgettable.

What to see and do at Jökulsárlón

At Jökulsárlón, visitors can stroll along the shore and watch the icebergs shift in colour and shape with the changing light, while seals often haul out on the ice or bob up between the floes. A popular highlight is a boat tour among the icebergs, either aboard large amphibious boats or faster zodiac craft, bringing you right up to the towering ice. Just across the Ring Road lies Breiðamerkursandur, better known as Diamond Beach, where glittering chunks of ice rest like scattered diamonds on jet-black sand. Birdlife, photography and the sheer stillness make this a truly unique South Iceland stop.

How to get to Jökulsárlón

Jökulsárlón lies in the southeastern part of South Iceland, right on the Ring Road (Route 1) between Skaftafell and the town of Höfn í Hornafirði. From Reykjavík it is around a five-hour drive east along the South Coast, and many travellers make it a two-day trip with an overnight stay near Vík, Kirkjubæjarklaustur or Höfn. Large car parks sit beside the lagoon on both sides of the bridge, giving easy access to the lagoon itself and to Diamond Beach opposite. Organised day tours and coach trips from Reykjavík are also available for those who prefer not to drive.

Best time to visit, boat tours and Diamond Beach at Jökulsárlón

Jökulsárlón is open year-round, but boat tours among the icebergs generally run from spring through autumn when the lagoon is free of ice. Summer offers mild weather and bright nights, while winter reveals a frozen lagoon, fewer tourists and the chance of northern lights glowing above the bergs. Diamond Beach is captivating in every season and especially photogenic in low, golden light. Dress warmly against the wind and cold, take care near the water's edge and the ocean, and allow plenty of time to enjoy both the lagoon and the Diamond Beach shore in South Iceland.
