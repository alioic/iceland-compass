Hvítserkur – The Sea Dragon of the Vatnsnes Peninsula

Hvítserkur is a 15-metre-high basalt sea stack rising majestically from Húnaflói bay off the eastern side of the Vatnsnes peninsula in Northwest Iceland. The rock is often likened to a dragon or a giant beast drinking from the sea, its arches and holes carved out by centuries of waves and wind. Hvítserkur is one of the region's most iconic landmarks and a true paradise for photographers. The white colour that gives the stack its name comes from bird droppings, as seabirds nest and rest on the rock. Few places capture the wild beauty of Iceland's northern coast so vividly.

Things to See and Do at Hvítserkur

The sea stack itself is the main attraction, with a clifftop viewing platform offering a fine perspective and a path leading down to the beach for a closer look. The Vatnsnes peninsula around Hvítserkur is home to one of Iceland's largest seal colonies, and there is a good chance of watching seals up close on the skerries and along the shore. A short drive away lies Borgarvirki, an ancient circular fortress on a rocky hill with sweeping views. Together, Hvítserkur, seal watching and Borgarvirki make a memorable day trip around Vatnsnes.

Where Is Hvítserkur and How to Get There

Hvítserkur lies on the eastern side of the Vatnsnes peninsula in Northwest Iceland, just off the ring road. From Route 1 you turn onto the Vatnsnes road, Route 711, which loops around the peninsula, and follow signs to a car park above the beach. From there a short trail leads to the viewing platform and down to the shore. The nearest town is Hvammstangi, which offers accommodation and services. Parts of the Vatnsnes road are gravel, so drive carefully, but the scenery makes the route well worth it.

Best Time to Visit Hvítserkur and Practical Tips

Hvítserkur is accessible year-round, but summer from May to September is the most comfortable, with clear roads and the seal colony at its most active. The beach at Hvítserkur is governed by the tides, so check high and low water in advance; at low tide you can walk closer to the stack. Photographers seek out the soft light of sunrise and sunset, when the stack reflects in the wet sand. Bring waterproof shoes and warm clothing, as the wind off Húnaflói can be strong, and take care on slippery beach rocks.
