Skriðuklaustur in Fljótsdalur

Skriðuklaustur is a culture centre and historic site in Fljótsdalur in East Iceland, housed in the stately building that author Gunnar Gunnarsson had built in 1939. It holds permanent exhibitions on the author's life and work, along with the history of a medieval monastery excavated on the site. The centre runs a varied programme of cultural events, art exhibitions and activities throughout the year. The grounds are family-friendly, with walking paths and a play area, and the ground floor houses the restaurant Klausturkaffi, which focuses on local ingredients. Skriðuklaustur is a welcome stop while touring Fljótsdalur and the area around Snæfellsstofa.

Opening hours and visiting

Skriðuklaustur is open daily through the summer, with longer hours at the height of the season and shorter hours outside peak times. It lies about half an hour's drive from Egilsstaðir, and the visit is easily combined with other destinations in Fljótsdalur. Guided tours of the house and exhibitions are offered, and visitors can walk around the monastery ruins outside. Klausturkaffi serves soup, cakes and meals made from local ingredients. Children under sixteen enter free when accompanied by an adult, making the visit a good choice for families.

History and culture in one place

Skriðuklaustur brings together literature, archaeology and culture in a unique way. The house itself is a notable building and a monument to one of Iceland's best-known authors, while beneath the surface lies the history of a medieval monastery that operated here centuries ago. For families the setting is ideal, with paths, a tree walk and a play area for the youngest visitors. Its proximity to Snæfellsstofa and the beautiful nature of Fljótsdalur makes Skriðuklaustur a good base for further experiences. A visit here combines learning, beauty and pleasant refreshments.
