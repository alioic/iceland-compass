Hverir við Námaskarð

A colourful high-temperature geothermal field with mud pots and fumaroles east of Mývatn.

Hverir at Námaskarð below Mount Námafjall, east of Lake Mývatn, is a powerful high-temperature geothermal field with boiling mud pots, steaming fumaroles and sulphur deposits. The barren ground glows in shades of red, yellow and ochre, and boardwalks lead across the area.

Hverir at Námaskarð – Geothermal Wonder of the Diamond Circle

Hverir at Námaskarð, beneath Mount Námafjall just east of Lake Mývatn, is one of Iceland's most striking high-temperature geothermal fields and a highlight of the Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn) in North Iceland. Here mud pots bubble and gurgle, steaming fumaroles hiss from the ground, and the barren earth glows in shades of red, yellow and ochre without a trace of vegetation. A strong sulphur smell hangs over the site, a reminder of the forces at work below. The colours, steam and lunar-like terrain make Hverir unforgettable.

What to See and Experience at Hverir

At Hverir, visitors follow boardwalks and marked paths past boiling mud pots that churn and spit, fumaroles venting steam, and delicate sulphur crystals glinting around the vents. The ground shifts in colour from ochre yellow to rust red and grey, while steam drifts continuously into the air. From the field a short climb up Námafjall opens a sweeping view over the geothermal area, Lake Mývatn and the surrounding mountains. Hverir is a vivid demonstration of the geothermal power that defines this part of North Iceland (Norðurland).

Getting There – Its Place on the Diamond Circle

Hverir sits just east of Lake Mývatn along Route 1 (the Ring Road), near Reykjahlíð, making it a very convenient stop on the Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn) and the Ring Road through North Iceland. The field lies at the foot of Námafjall, only about a ten-minute drive from Mývatn toward Egilsstaðir. A spacious car park sits beside the road and entry is free. Hverir links easily to other Diamond Circle sights such as Mývatn, Dettifoss and Goðafoss, and works well as a short but powerful stop on the journey.

Best Time to Visit and Practical Tips

Hverir is open year-round and accessible at any time, though summer and spring offer the most comfortable weather for walking the field. Most important, always stay on the marked paths and boardwalks: the ground is scalding hot in places and the crust can be dangerously thin off-trail. The strong sulphur smell is normal and soon fades; those sensitive in the airways should keep it in mind. Never touch the vents or mud, and respect every warning sign across this active geothermal area.
