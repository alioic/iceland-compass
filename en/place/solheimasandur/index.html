Sólheimasandur

A black-sand plain on the South Coast with the wreck of a US DC-3 plane.

On Sólheimasandur on the South Coast lies the wreck of a United States DC-3 plane that crash-landed in 1973, with the crew surviving. The wreck sits on a vast black-sand plain and is a popular, eerie photography spot. It is reached by a roughly 4 km walk each way from a car park by the Ring Road, or by a shuttle bus.

Sólheimasandur in South Iceland: the DC-3 plane wreck

Sólheimasandur is a vast black sand plain on the South Coast of Iceland, between Skógafoss and Vík, where the skeletal wreck of a United States Navy DC-3 (Douglas) plane rests on the barren sands. The aircraft crash-landed here in 1973 and the entire crew survived, but the fuselage has lain on the plain ever since. Today it is one of the most famous and atmospheric photography spots in South Iceland (Suðurland), its rusting aluminium body forming a striking contrast against the jet-black sand, distant glaciers and the moody Atlantic sky overhead.

What to see and do at Sólheimasandur

The main attraction is the plane wreck itself, where visitors can walk around the fuselage, step inside and capture unforgettable photographs in a raw, almost lunar landscape. The empty sand, with no vegetation and mountains and glaciers on the horizon, gives Sólheimasandur an otherworldly, cinematic feel. Many people arrive at sunrise or sunset when the light is softest and the crowds are thinnest, and on clear winter nights the northern lights sometimes dance above the wreck. Please tread lightly, respect the fragile site and leave nothing behind so this special place in South Iceland stays pristine.

How to get to Sólheimasandur

Sólheimasandur lies in South Iceland just east of Skógafoss, roughly a two-hour drive from Reykjavík along the Ring Road (Route 1). A signposted car park sits right beside the road, and the wreck is reached on foot from there; driving out onto the sand is no longer permitted. The walk is flat and easy but long, about four kilometres each way, so allow two to three hours there and back. For those who would rather skip the hike, a shuttle bus runs regularly between the car park and the plane wreck for a small fee.

Best time to visit and practical tips for the Sólheimasandur walk

Sólheimasandur can be visited year-round, but sturdy footwear, windproof clothing and water are essential because the route is long, open and completely exposed to the elements. Summer brings long, bright evenings ideal for photography, while winter offers snow, fewer tourists and the chance of northern lights. Allow at least two to three hours for the round-trip walk, or take the shuttle bus if time is short. Note that there are no services, toilets or shelter at the wreck itself, so come well prepared before setting out across this open plain in South Iceland.
