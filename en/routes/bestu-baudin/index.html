Best pools & geothermal baths

From luxury lagoons to hidden hot springs

Iceland is a land of hot water, and nothing is more Icelandic than soaking in a pool under the open sky. Here are the best: sleek geothermal spas like the Blue Lagoon, Sky Lagoon and the Mývatn Nature Baths, the sea baths of GeoSea and Forest Lagoon, and the unique natural pools and hot pots — Seljavallalaug beneath a mountain, Reykjafjarðarlaug in the Westfjords, the Drangsnes hot pots and Hrunalaug. One tip: shower naked before bathing — that's Icelandic pool etiquette.

The Best Hot Springs and Baths in Iceland – Geothermal Bathing at Its Finest

Iceland is a land of geothermal energy, and nowhere is that more enjoyable than in its hot springs and baths. A centuries-old bathing culture meets sleek modern spas where architecture, nature and naturally heated water come together in one experience. Travellers can choose between world-famous lagoons with every comfort and tiny natural pools where the only sounds are the wind and a running stream. Soaking outdoors in warm water – under the midnight sun in summer or the northern lights in winter – is a highlight of any Iceland trip. This collection brings together the country's best-known geothermal baths and its most beloved natural and historic pools, so you can find the right soak for your journey, whether you are after luxury or solitude.

From Bláa lónið to Seljavallalaug – Iceland's Must-Visit Lagoons and Pools

The famous lagoons come first: Bláa lónið (the Blue Lagoon) on the Reykjanes peninsula, Sky Lagoon just outside Reykjavík with its infinity edge over the Atlantic, Jarðböðin við Mývatn (the Mývatn Nature Baths), GeoSea in Húsavík, Forest Lagoon (Skógarböðin) across the fjord from Akureyri, Vök Baths near Egilsstaðir, Hvammsvík in Hvalfjörður, Krauma beside the powerful Deildartunguhver hot spring, and Laugarvatn Fontana in the Golden Circle area. For a simpler, wilder experience, seek out the beloved natural and historic pools: Seljavallalaug tucked into a valley below Eyjafjöll, Hrunalaug near Flúðir, Reykjafjarðarlaug and Krossneslaug in the Westfjords, and the famous infinity pool at Hofsós overlooking Skagafjörður.

Practical Tips for Iceland's Baths – Booking, Pool Etiquette and Seasons

The most popular baths – above all Bláa lónið and Sky Lagoon – often sell out, so book tickets in advance, especially in summer and at weekends. Icelandic bathing culture has one firm rule: everyone must shower thoroughly, without a swimsuit, before entering the water. At the natural pools, unwritten rules apply as well – take your rubbish with you, never use soap in the pool itself and leave the site exactly as you found it. The more remote pools, such as Krossneslaug and Reykjafjarðarlaug, lie along gravel roads that are easiest to drive in summer. Most of the lagoons welcome guests year-round, but winter visits call for a close eye on the weather and road conditions.

Places on this route

  • Bláa lónið World-renowned geothermal baths with milky-blue, mineral-rich water set in a lava field.
  • Sky Lagoon An upscale oceanfront geothermal lagoon in Kópavogur with an infinity edge dissolving into the North Atlantic.
  • Jarðböðin við Mývatn Milky-blue geothermal baths set in lava fields overlooking Lake Mývatn.
  • GeoSea Geothermal sea baths on the clifftop overlooking Skjálfandi bay.
  • Skógarböðin (Forest Lagoon) Geothermal baths tucked into a birch forest above Eyjafjörður.
  • Vök Baths Iceland's only floating geothermal pools set out in Lake Urriðavatn near Egilsstaðir.
  • Krauma Geothermal baths fed by Deildartunguhver, Europe's most powerful hot spring, in Borgarfjörður.
  • Hvammsvík Natural hot pools set right at the tideline of Hvalfjörður, framed by fjord and mountains.
  • Laugarvatn Fontana Geothermal baths and natural steam rooms on the shore of Lake Laugarvatn.
  • Gamla laugin (Flúðir) The oldest swimming pool in the country, a natural geothermal pool in Flúðir.
  • Seljavallalaug One of Iceland's oldest pools, hidden in a narrow valley under Eyjafjöll.
  • Reykjafjarðarlaug A peaceful geothermal pool at the head of Reykjafjörður in Arnarfjörður.
  • Grettislaug og Reykjaströnd Natural hot pools by the sea with views towards Drangey.
  • Drangsnes Hot Pots Three seaside hot tubs right in the middle of the village of Drangsnes.
  • Hrunalaug Small stone-built natural pools on private farmland near Flúðir.
