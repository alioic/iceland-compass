Vestmannaeyjar

Vestmannaeyjar – the volcanic islands off the South Coast

The Vestmannaeyjar, or Westman Islands, are a captivating volcanic archipelago off the South Coast of Iceland and one of the country's most distinctive destinations. The largest island, Heimaey, is the only inhabited one and still bears clear scars from the 1973 eruption, when the Eldfell volcano was born and part of the town was buried under lava. The islands are home to one of the world's largest puffin colonies, a lively island community and dramatic scenery where steep bird cliffs meet volcanic craters. Vestmannaeyjar offers a unique blend of volcanic history, birdlife and seafaring culture.

Things to see and do in Vestmannaeyjar

On Heimaey you can hike the Eldfell volcano and visit the Eldheimar museum, which tells the moving story of the 1973 eruption. The Sæheimar natural history museum showcases seabirds and marine life, and in summer you can watch puffins in vast colonies across the island. Boat tours around the archipelago bring visitors to bird cliffs, caves and the dramatic coastline. Sprangan, a traditional cliff-swinging practice, is part of the islands' distinctive culture. The town, the harbour and the trails up the surrounding peaks make Heimaey a varied destination for the whole family.

Location and how to reach Vestmannaeyjar

Vestmannaeyjar lies off the South Coast and is most easily reached by the Herjólfur ferry from Landeyjahöfn, about an hour and a half's drive from Reykjavík. The crossing to Heimaey takes only around forty minutes and the ferry runs several times a day. You can also fly to the islands from Reykjavík in a short time. Booking the ferry in advance is recommended, especially during the peak summer season, and it is wise to check schedules carefully, as weather and sea conditions can affect sailings to and from the islands.

Best time to visit Vestmannaeyjar and helpful tips

Vestmannaeyjar welcomes visitors all year, but summer, from May to August, is the best time to see the puffins that nest in the colonies then. In August the famous Þjóðhátíð festival is held, one of the country's largest outdoor gatherings, so it is lively but accommodation fills up fast. Dress in windproof and waterproof layers, as the weather on the islands can be gusty and changeable. Book your ferry and accommodation well ahead, allow at least a full day, and check the ferry schedule against the weather before you set off.
