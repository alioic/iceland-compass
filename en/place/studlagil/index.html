Stuðlagil – a dramatic basalt column canyon in East Iceland

Stuðlagil is a breathtaking canyon in the Jökuldalur valley in East Iceland (Austurland), famed for its dramatic basalt columns and the blue-green glacial river winding along its floor. The canyon became widely known after around 2016, when the river level dropped following the Kárahnjúkar hydropower dam and revealed the previously hidden basalt columns in their full glory. The hexagonal basalt formations, paired with the turquoise water, create one of Iceland's most distinctive and photographed natural wonders. Stuðlagil is not part of the Diamond Circle but a standalone gem of the East Iceland landscape drawing ever more visitors.

What to see and do at Stuðlagil

At Stuðlagil you can admire the tall, remarkably regular basalt columns that wall the canyon and contrast with the blue-green glacial water flowing between them. On the east side, a walking trail leads down toward the canyon, letting you get closer to the columns and passing the waterfall Stuðlafoss along the way. The west side offers a viewing platform with a sweeping overview of the canyon without a long hike. The site is a true paradise for photographers, nature lovers and hikers wanting to experience one of the leading natural attractions of East Iceland.

How to get to Stuðlagil

Stuðlagil lies in the Jökuldalur valley in East Iceland, not far from the Ring Road (Route 1) between Egilsstaðir and the highland route north of Vatnajökull. You drive along the Jökuldalsvegur road (923) beside the glacial river Jökulsá á Dal, and your destination depends on which side of the river you choose. The west side with its viewing platform is quicker to reach, while the east side requires driving farther up the valley to the farm Grund, followed by a fairly long walk down to the canyon. Parts of the route are gravel roads, so allow time and drive carefully through this East Iceland countryside.

Best time to visit and practical tips – choose the right side and check water levels

The best time to visit Stuðlagil is generally summer and early autumn, when the roads are clear and the river runs low, so the basalt columns and the blue-green color show at their finest. Note that water levels can change and affect both the river's color and how well the columns are exposed. Decide in advance which side you want: the west side suits those wanting a short walk to a viewing platform, while the east side rewards those up for a longer hike down into the canyon. Take care near the edges, dress for the weather and respect the nature of this East Iceland gem.
