Glymur – One of Iceland's Tallest Waterfalls Hidden in Hvalfjörður

Glymur plunges 198 metres and was long considered Iceland's tallest waterfall, tucked away in a deep, green gorge at the head of Hvalfjörður in West Iceland. The falls tumble through a narrow ravine where the Botnsá river drops from the highland edge, surrounded by birdlife and verdant slopes. The hike to Glymur is an adventure in itself and a favourite among outdoor enthusiasts. Just about an hour's drive from Reykjavík, Glymur offers a genuine nature experience and one of the country's most captivating gorges, away from the busiest tourist sites.

What to See and Do at Glymur

The hike to Glymur is the highlight of any visit – the route passes through a lava cave, crosses the Botnsá river on a log, and climbs a steep slope with spectacular views over the waterfall and gorge. The loop takes about three to four hours and is demanding but rewarding. At the top, Glymur reveals itself in full glory, ringed by bird cliffs and moss-covered rock. Hvalfjörður also invites quiet walks along the fjord and glimpses of history. Glymur is ideal for experienced hikers and adventure-seeking travellers in West Iceland.

How to Get to Glymur in Hvalfjörður

Glymur lies at the head of Hvalfjörður in West Iceland, about an hour's drive from Reykjavík. Drive around the fjord on Route 47 (Hvalfjarðarvegur) to its innermost point, where a car park marks the start of the trail into Botnsdalur. Note that you bypass the Hvalfjörður tunnel and follow the fjord itself. From the car park it is a hike to the falls – no road leads all the way. Glymur makes a fine day trip from the capital and connects easily with other West Iceland nature such as Borgarfjörður and Akranes.

Best Time to Visit Glymur and Practical Tips

The best time to hike to Glymur is from June to September, once the snow has melted and the log bridge over the Botnsá has been set in place; it is removed for winter, when the route becomes dangerous and difficult. Wear good hiking boots and bring water, snacks and windproof, waterproof clothing. The trail is steep and muddy in places, so trekking poles are helpful. Start early in the day and watch the weather. Glymur is a challenging but unforgettable nature experience in Hvalfjörður, West Iceland.
