Blönduós — a town at the mouth of the Blanda in Northwest Iceland

Blönduós is a welcoming town in Northwest Iceland, set where the glacial river Blanda flows into the sea at Húnaflói bay. It is known as a centre of textile art in Iceland and boasts a lively cultural scene, a distinctive church and beautiful riverside surroundings. As one of the main service stops on the Ring Road between Reykjavík and Akureyri, Blönduós is a natural place to break the journey, spend the night and discover the region's crafts and history. A blend of seaside-town atmosphere, artistic life and nature makes Blönduós an enjoyable stop on a tour of Northwest Iceland.

What to see and do in Blönduós

In Blönduós, visitors can explore the Textile Museum and its guest workshops to discover Iceland's rich tradition of textile art and craft. Blönduós Church draws the eye with its unusual design, shaped like a volcano, and ranks among the country's most striking churches. In the river itself lies the island of Hrútey, a small and delightful nature reserve with marked walking paths, vegetation and birdlife, ideal for families. Nearby, you can also go seal watching along the shores of Húnaflói. Together these attractions make Blönduós a varied stop for lovers of both culture and nature.

Getting to Blönduós — location

Blönduós sits right on the Ring Road in Northwest Iceland, roughly halfway between Reykjavík and Akureyri, the capital of the north. The drive from Reykjavík takes about three hours, making the town an ideal place to pause on a longer journey north. Good access, services and accommodation make Blönduós a comfortable stop. From here it is also a short trip out onto the Skagi peninsula and into Skagafjörður, so the town serves as a fine base for exploring the natural and historical gems of Northwest Iceland such as Kálfshamarsvík, Glaumbær and Grettislaug.

Best time to visit Blönduós and practical tips

Blönduós welcomes visitors year-round, but summer and late summer offer the best conditions for walking around Hrútey, enjoying the riverbanks and going seal watching. Museums and workshops are open mainly during the summer, so check opening hours before you visit. In winter, the town makes a convenient stop on the Ring Road with accommodation and services. Dress for the weather, as northern Icelandic conditions can be varied. Give yourself time to stroll through town and enjoy the interplay of river, sea and culture in this friendly place.
