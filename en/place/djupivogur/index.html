Djúpivogur – a tranquil Cittaslow village in the East Fjords

Djúpivogur is a historic, peaceful fishing village on the southern East Fjords and Iceland's first Cittaslow community, a movement that celebrates calm, quality and closeness to nature. Along the harbour stands the artwork Eggin í Gleðivík by Sigurður Guðmundsson, a series of large granite eggs each representing a local bird species. The pyramid-shaped mountain Búlandstindur rises dramatically above the village. Djúpivogur is a perfect destination for travellers who want to slow down and enjoy art, history and birdlife on the route through East Iceland.

Things to see and do in Djúpivogur

The main landmark of Djúpivogur is Eggin í Gleðivík, an art walk along the harbour where 34 large granite eggs line the shore. The village itself is rich in well-preserved old houses and the relaxed atmosphere of a Cittaslow community. Nearby, the striking mountain Búlandstindur is popular for hiking and photography. From the harbour you can take a boat trip to the island of Papey, known for its puffins and abundant birdlife. Birdwatching, gentle walks and a cosy café atmosphere make a visit here genuinely memorable.

How to get to Djúpivogur

Djúpivogur sits directly on Route 1, the Ring Road, in southern East Iceland, roughly halfway between Höfn and the fjords further north. From Höfn it is a short, scenic drive north along the coast, and from Egilsstaðir it is an easy drive south. This location makes the village a natural stop for anyone travelling the Ring Road through East Iceland. The drive passes beaches, fjords and mountains, and Djúpivogur is an ideal place to pause, stretch your legs and soak up the calm on a long journey around the country.

Best time to visit Djúpivogur and practical tips

Summer, from May to September, is the best time to visit Djúpivogur, when boat trips to Papey run, birdlife is at its peak and the weather is mildest. It is also the ideal season to walk along the Eggin í Gleðivík and, for experienced hikers, up toward Búlandstindur. In winter the village is quiet and snow-covered, with more limited services. Bring warm clothing, sturdy shoes and binoculars for birdwatching. Above all, allow time to enjoy the slow pace that the Cittaslow village of Djúpivogur is known for.
