Lofthellir

A lava cave near Lake Mývatn known for spectacular ice formations.

Lofthellir – an adventurous lava cave of ice sculptures near Lake Mývatn

Lofthellir is a spectacular lava cave near Lake Mývatn in North Iceland, celebrated for its extraordinary natural ice sculptures and formations. Deep inside, ice builds up on the walls and floor year-round, and towering ice columns and glassy ice statues create a magical underworld that has few equals in Iceland. The cave lies within easy reach of the popular Diamond Circle route and the Mývatn geothermal region, making it one of the area's best-kept secrets for travelers seeking something unusual beyond the classic natural attractions of North Iceland.

What to see and do in Lofthellir

Inside Lofthellir, glittering ice formations, frozen waterfalls and clear ice columns take on shifting shapes depending on the season and temperature. The journey is part of the adventure: you hike across rugged lava to the cave entrance, then crawl and clamber through narrow passages to reach the main chambers. There, guides light up the ice with headlamps and explain the cave's geology and how it formed. It is a memorable, hands-on experience for anyone who enjoys exploration, raw nature and a bit of underground challenge near Lake Mývatn in North Iceland.

How to get to Lofthellir

Lofthellir sits in the lava fields east of Lake Mývatn in North Iceland, and access is by guided tour only. Operators typically collect guests near Mývatn or at a set meeting point, then drive to the trailhead in a 4x4 or super-jeep along a rough track. From there it is a short but rocky walk across the lava to the cave. Because the area is fragile and the cave demanding, you cannot visit Lofthellir on your own – a booked guide provides the equipment, safety and interpretation for the entire trip.

Best time to visit and practical tips – guided tour only

Lofthellir can only be visited on a guided tour, and trips often run in both summer and winter, though availability can be seasonal. The ice formations are present year-round but change from season to season. Tours provide special protective clothing, a helmet and a headlamp, but wear warm base layers because it is cold inside the cave. Be prepared to crawl, scramble and get muddy, and choose sturdy footwear. Book ahead, especially during the busy season around Mývatn, and tell your guide in advance if you are prone to claustrophobia.
