Hrísey

A tranquil island in Eyjafjörður, the 'pearl of Eyjafjörður', with rich birdlife.

Hrísey is the second largest island off Iceland and a peaceful paradise in Eyjafjörður, reached by a short ferry from Árskógssandur. Largely car-free, with marked trails, tame ptarmigan and one of the largest eider duck nesting grounds in the country.

Hrísey – The Pearl of Eyjafjörður with Rich Birdlife

Hrísey is the second-largest island off Iceland and a peaceful natural paradise in the middle of Eyjafjörður, often called the pearl of Eyjafjörður. The island is known for its remarkable tranquillity, rich birdlife and relaxed community life, where cars are few and the pace of life slows right down. It is home to one of Iceland's largest eider-duck nesting grounds, and tame ptarmigan add to the charm. Hrísey is an ideal destination for travellers who want to escape the bustle, enjoy nature and experience genuine Icelandic island culture in the heart of North Iceland, just a short ferry ride from the mainland.

Things to See and Do in Hrísey

Hrísey has marked walking trails that cross the island and suit the whole family, offering the chance to enjoy the birdlife, views across Eyjafjörður and the surrounding mountains. Birdwatching takes centre stage, as the island is famous for its tame ptarmigan and one of the country's largest eider colonies. The cultural centre known as The House tells the story of the island and life there. Quiet moments by the harbour, a visit to a small café and an evening stroll in the calm all make a stay memorable. Hrísey is about slowing down, breathing in the fresh sea air and enjoying an extraordinary sense of peace.

How to Get to Hrísey

Hrísey is reached by a small ferry, the Sævar, which sails regularly from Árskógssandur on the western shore of Eyjafjörður. The crossing takes only about fifteen minutes over the fjord and is quick and comfortable. Árskógssandur is roughly a 35-minute drive north of Akureyri, close to Dalvík, so Hrísey fits well into a journey around Eyjafjörður and the Tröllaskagi peninsula. The ferry runs several times a day year-round, though the timetable varies by season, so it is wise to check the schedule before you go. On the island itself, walking or cycling is the best way to get around.

Best Time to Visit Hrísey and Practical Tips

Summer, from May to August, is the best time to visit Hrísey, when the birdlife is at its peak, the daylight is long and the walking trails are most accessible. The midnight sun over the fjord is enchanting during this season. That said, the island is worth visiting year-round for those seeking complete calm. Bring warm, waterproof clothing and good walking shoes, as it can be cool and windy out on the fjord. Respect the birds' nesting areas and stay on the marked trails. Combine your visit with Dalvík, the Beer Spa at Árskógssandur and other spots along Eyjafjörður.
