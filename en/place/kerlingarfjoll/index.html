Kerlingarfjöll

Colourful rhyolite mountains with a steaming geothermal area in Hveradalir.

Kerlingarfjöll is a cluster of colourful rhyolite mountains by the Kjölur route in the Highlands. The Hveradalir geothermal area is stunning — steaming ground in shades of red, yellow and green with snowdrifts in between. A popular hiking area with well-developed facilities.

Kerlingarfjöll – the colourful mountains of the Icelandic highlands

Kerlingarfjöll is one of the most spectacular natural gems in the Icelandic highlands, a cluster of rhyolite mountains rising beside the Kjölur route between the Langjökull and Hofsjökull glaciers. The peaks glow in shades of red, pink, yellow and green, colours created by intense geothermal activity that has transformed the rock over millennia. Once a summer ski area, Kerlingarfjöll is now a favourite destination for hikers and photographers drawn to the striking contrast of steaming hot springs, glacial snow and vividly coloured slopes. Few places capture the drama of the highlands quite like it.

Hveradalir and hiking trails in Kerlingarfjöll

The heart of Kerlingarfjöll is the Hveradalir geothermal valley, where columns of steam rise from red and ochre earth among lingering snowfields. Marked trails wind through the area, ranging from short loops to longer day hikes up Snækollur, the highest peak, with sweeping views over the surrounding glaciers. Walkers can explore bubbling mud pots, multicoloured hillsides and vast highland panoramas. After a hike, soaking in the area's natural hot spring is the perfect reward. Kerlingarfjöll offers hikers of every ability an unforgettable encounter with Iceland's raw highland wilderness.

How to get to Kerlingarfjöll

Kerlingarfjöll lies along the Kjölur route (F35) in the central highlands, roughly a three-hour drive from Reykjavík via the Golden Circle and up the mountain road. The route follows gravel highland tracks and crosses some unbridged rivers, so a four-wheel-drive vehicle and experience with highland driving are essential. Travellers who prefer not to drive can use scheduled highland buses or join organised day tours from the capital area during summer. On site, the Highland Base offers accommodation, dining and a comfortable place to prepare for hikes into the mountains.

Best time to visit Kerlingarfjöll

Kerlingarfjöll is accessible from June through September, once the highland roads open after the winter snows. High summer, July and August, brings long bright days, milder weather and the best conditions for exploring Hveradalir on foot. Highland weather can change quickly, however, so dress in layers, pack windproof and waterproof clothing, and wear sturdy hiking boots. Always check the forecast and road conditions before setting out. Bring food, water and warm clothing, as services are limited once you head into this remote corner of the Icelandic interior.
