Höfn í Hornafirði – the langoustine town at the foot of Vatnajökull

Höfn í Hornafirði is a lively harbour town in southeast Iceland, celebrated as the country's langoustine capital. Set on a spit of land, it offers spectacular views over the glacier tongues of Vatnajökull, Europe's largest glacier, and the mountains surrounding Hornafjörður fjord. Höfn is an ideal base for glacier tours, trips to Jökulsárlón, and experiencing a rare landscape where ice, sea and mountains meet. For food lovers, photographers and adventurers travelling the Ring Road, Höfn is an essential stop in the southeast of the country.

Things to see and do in Höfn

Höfn is renowned for its langoustine restaurants, where visitors can savour fresh Icelandic langoustine caught straight from Hornafjörður. The town's glacier exhibition tells the story of Vatnajökull and Iceland's ice caps, and many guided glacier tours depart from here. Just nearby, Stokksnes with the majestic Vestrahorn mountain is one of Iceland's most popular photography spots. Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon and Breiðamerkursandur are within easy reach, and in winter you can join ice cave tours beneath Vatnajökull. Höfn combines great food, glacier adventures and unrivalled glacier views in one place.

How to get to Höfn í Hornafirði

Höfn lies on Route 1, the Ring Road, in southeast Iceland, roughly midway between Reykjavík and Egilsstaðir when driving east along the south coast. From Jökulsárlón it is about an hour's drive east to Höfn, and the town is a natural base for the Vatnajökull region. A domestic airport is also located just outside town. The route runs alongside glacier tongues and outwash plains with Vatnajökull in view the whole way. Höfn is a key stop for anyone travelling the Ring Road who wants to experience the glaciers, the langoustine and the mountains around Hornafjörður.

Best time to visit Höfn and practical tips

Höfn welcomes visitors year-round, and each season has its own charm. Summer, from June to August, brings mild weather, long daylight and the best conditions for glacier hiking and driving. In winter, from November to March, you can join ice cave tours beneath Vatnajökull and have a chance to see the northern lights. A langoustine festival is held in the town in autumn. Bring warm, windproof clothing and sturdy hiking shoes, and book glacier tours and accommodation in advance during peak season. Enjoy a langoustine meal and the Vatnajökull views before moving on.
