Reykjadalur

A hike up into a valley to a warm river you can bathe in.

From Hveragerði a popular trail leads up into Reykjadalur, the 'steam valley', past hot springs and mud pots. At the top runs a warm river where wooden decking lets people bathe in nature under the open sky.

Reykjadalur – the steam valley with a warm bathing river

Reykjadalur, the 'steam valley', is one of South Iceland's most popular spots for combining a hike with a natural soak. The valley lies above the town of Hveragerði and is dotted with hot springs, mud pots and columns of steam rising from the ground. At the top of the valley runs a warm river where you can bathe out in the open air. Reykjadalur brings together the joy of walking, geothermal heat and relaxation in a unique setting, making it an ideal destination for families and anyone wanting to experience Iceland's geothermal nature in an accessible and enjoyable way.

Hiking and bathing in Reykjadalur

The trail through Reykjadalur is about seven kilometres round trip and typically takes around three hours. From the car park above Hveragerði, the path climbs uphill past bubbling hot springs, colourful mud pots and steam vents, with lovely views over the valley and countryside below. At the top comes the reward: a warm bathing river where wooden platforms make it easy to change and slip into the naturally heated water. Remember to bring a towel and swimsuit. Reykjadalur is a perfect blend of gentle hiking and unique relaxation in the heart of Icelandic nature.

Location and getting to Reykjadalur

Reykjadalur is located just above Hveragerði in South Iceland, only about a 45-minute drive from Reykjavík along the Ring Road. From Hveragerði, a short drive leads up to the car park where the trail begins. Its closeness to the capital and good roads make Reykjadalur one of the most accessible hiking and bathing spots in the country, perfect for a day trip. The site is also an ideal stop on a South Coast journey or a relaxing end to the day. It pairs well with the region's other natural attractions, as there is plenty to see nearby.

Best time to visit and tips for Reykjadalur

Reykjadalur is most popular in summer, from late May through September, when the trail is dry and accessible and the weather is mild. The route can be walked at other times of year, but in winter the path may be snowy, wet and slippery, calling for good footwear and caution. Bring a swimsuit, towel and sturdy hiking shoes, as the trail can be muddy. Dress in layers and be prepared for wind and changeable weather. Arrive early in the day to enjoy the bathing river in greater peace, as it can get busy on fine summer days.
