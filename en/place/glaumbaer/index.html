Glaumbær

One of the best-preserved turf houses in the country, the Skagafjörður heritage museum.

Glaumbær is a splendid turf house from the 18th–19th century and one of the best-preserved examples of the traditional Icelandic gabled farmhouse. Today it houses a heritage museum showing the dwellings and way of life of past centuries. Guðríður Þorbjarnardóttir, a seafarer to Vínland, is connected to the site.

Glaumbær in Skagafjörður — Iceland's best-preserved turf farm

Glaumbær is one of Iceland's finest and best-preserved turf houses, set in the heart of Skagafjörður in Northwest Iceland. This cluster of gabled turf-and-stone buildings dates from the 18th and 19th centuries and offers a rare, authentic glimpse of how Icelanders lived for centuries. Today the farm houses the Skagafjörður Folk Museum and ranks among the country's most treasured cultural monuments. Glaumbær is also linked to Guðríður Þorbjarnardóttir, the far-travelled explorer who sailed to Vínland, making it a must-see for anyone interested in Icelandic history and building heritage.

What to see and do at Glaumbær turf house

At Glaumbær you can wander through the old turf buildings, exploring the communal living room, kitchen and pantries that reveal the daily life of Icelandic farm households. The folk museum displays tools, furnishings and everyday objects that bring the region's tough but colourful farming history to life. After the museum visit, settle into Áskaffi, a charming old timber house, for Icelandic pastries and home-baked cakes. Families, history lovers and photographers will find plenty here, as the turf farm is wonderfully picturesque beneath the mountains of Skagafjörður in northern Iceland.

Getting to Glaumbær — location in Skagafjörður

Glaumbær lies in Skagafjörður in Northwest Iceland, just off Route 75 and a short drive from the towns of Sauðárkrókur and Varmahlíð. It makes an easy stop for travellers on the Ring Road between Northwest Iceland and Akureyri, the capital of the north. From Reykjavík, the drive north to Skagafjörður takes roughly four and a half hours. There is ample parking at the farm and access is straightforward. Glaumbær fits naturally into a longer tour of Northwest Iceland alongside the region's other natural and historical highlights.

Best time to visit Glaumbær and practical tips

The folk museum at Glaumbær is open mainly during the summer months, from spring into autumn, when access is easiest and daylight is long. Summer and late summer bring mild weather and the lush landscapes of Skagafjörður, while autumn washes the turf houses in warm colours. Check the museum's opening hours before your visit and allow one to two hours to explore. Dress for changeable northern Icelandic weather. Combine your trip with nearby attractions such as Grettislaug, Tindastóll and other worthwhile stops around Skagafjörður for a rewarding day in the region.
