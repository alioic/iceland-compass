Northeast Iceland – capital of the north and natural wonders

Northeast Iceland (Norðurland eystra) is the largest and most varied part of North Iceland, where town life, volcanic country and thundering waterfalls come together. Here stands Akureyri, the capital of the north, snugly set at the head of Eyjafjörður beneath high mountains. Around the town unfold some of the country's most spectacular sights: the volcanic land around Lake Mývatn, the roaring Dettifoss, the horseshoe canyon of Ásbyrgi and the whale watching of Húsavík. This is a region of contrasts – green valleys and lunar highlands, boiling hot springs and icy glacial rivers, a lively town and untouched wilderness. For travellers wanting North Iceland in full, it is an essential year-round destination.

Top places and the Diamond Circle

Akureyri offers a warm cultural scene, a botanical garden and the Hlíðarfjall ski area. Just east begins the Diamond Circle, linking the region's headline sights: Goðafoss, the arcing 'waterfall of the gods' beside the Ring Road; Lake Mývatn with its pseudo-craters, the lava forms of Dimmuborgir, the Námafjall geothermal field and the relaxed Mývatn Nature Baths; the horseshoe canyon of Ásbyrgi; and Dettifoss, the most powerful waterfall in Europe. At Húsavík, Europe's whale-watching capital, humpbacks and minke whales appear regularly, and the GeoSea baths offer warm seawater overlooking Skjálfandi bay. Inland lies the Askja caldera with its Víti crater lake, and offshore sits Grímsey on the Arctic Circle itself.

Getting around Northeast Iceland and the Diamond Circle

The Ring Road threads through Northeast Iceland, connecting Akureyri, Goðafoss and Lake Mývatn in one easy run. From Mývatn it is ideal to drive the Diamond Circle, a loop of roughly 250 km that takes in Dettifoss, Ásbyrgi and Húsavík and can be done in one long day or spread over two. Most travellers give the region at least two to three days to enjoy both town life and nature. Roads to the main sights are paved and easy, but the route to Askja crosses mountain tracks and unbridged rivers passable only to sturdy 4x4s in summer.

Best time to visit and practical tips

Summer is the liveliest season: bright nights, whale watching at its peak from April into October, and the highland route to Askja usually opening in July. It is also the best time to see puffins on Grímsey and to hike the canyons at Ásbyrgi and Dettifoss. In winter Akureyri becomes a ski town, the northern lights dance over Eyjafjörður, and the Mývatn Nature Baths are a haven in the frost. Goðafoss, Mývatn and Akureyri are accessible all year. Dress for the weather, take care near hot springs and waterfall edges, fuel up in Akureyri, and book whale watching and accommodation well ahead in summer.

Places & nature

  • Akureyri The capital of the north, set at the head of Iceland's longest fjord.
  • Mývatn A volcanic landscape of pseudo-craters, hot springs and natural baths.
  • Goðafoss The waterfall of the gods, where the pagan gods were cast aside around the year 1000.
  • Húsavík Europe's whale-watching capital with sea baths on the cliff edge.
  • Ásbyrgi A horseshoe-shaped canyon with cliffs up to 100 metres, a still pond and birch woodland.
  • Askja A giant volcanic caldera in Ódáðahraun with a warm crater lake.
  • Siglufjörður Iceland's northernmost town, a former herring capital with a superb museum.
  • Grímsey Iceland's only inhabited settlement on the Arctic Circle.
  • Dalvík og Árskógssandur A quiet town on Eyjafjörður with whale watching and beer baths.
  • Aldeyjarfoss A powerful waterfall framed by black basalt columns in Skjálfandafljót.
  • Krafla og Leirhnjúkur An active volcanic system with the crater lake Víti and steaming lava fields.
  • Hrísey A tranquil island in Eyjafjörður, the 'pearl of Eyjafjörður', with rich birdlife.
  • Vaglaskógur One of the largest and most beautiful birch forests in the country, in Fnjóskadalur.
  • Laufás A beautiful old parsonage turf house by Eyjafjörður.
  • Ólafsfjörður og Héðinsfjörður A quiet fjord town on Tröllaskagi with the uninhabited Héðinsfjörður alongside.
  • Tjörnes A peninsula between Skjálfandi and Öxarfjörður, known for its fossils.
  • Heimskautsgerðið A large stone gateway monument inspired by Norse mythology at Iceland's northern edge.
  • Hljóðaklettar Striking basalt rock formations in Vesturdalur within the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon.
  • Lofthellir A lava cave near Lake Mývatn known for spectacular ice formations.
  • Hverir við Námaskarð A colourful high-temperature geothermal field with mud pots and fumaroles east of Mývatn.
  • Dettifoss Europe's most powerful waterfall, about 44 metres high and 100 metres wide.

Hiking trails

  • Askja og Dyngjufjöll Hikes through the lunar landscape of Askja and its surroundings at the heart of the highlands.
  • Súlur Akureyri's local mountain - a twin-peaked summit with sweeping views.
  • Vindbelgjarfjall A short but steep climb to the best viewpoint over Mývatn.
  • Ásbyrgi A rim loop around the horseshoe canyon of Ásbyrgi with views down into its forest.
  • Hverfjall A climb to the rim and a loop around the vast tephra crater of Hverfjall by Lake Mývatn.
  • Leirhnjúkur A loop through fresh lava and steaming geothermal ground in the Krafla caldera.
  • Dimmuborgir An easy loop through a maze of towering lava pillars and arches by Lake Mývatn.

Pools & baths

  • Jarðböðin við Mývatn Milky-blue geothermal baths set in lava fields overlooking Lake Mývatn.
  • GeoSea Geothermal sea baths on the clifftop overlooking Skjálfandi bay.
  • Skógarböðin (Forest Lagoon) Geothermal baths tucked into a birch forest above Eyjafjörður.
  • Sundlaug Akureyrar One of Iceland's most popular pools, home to the country's longest waterslide.
  • Sundlaug Dalvíkur An attractive pool with mountain views in the seaside town of Dalvík.
  • Ólafsfjörður Swimming Pool An outdoor pool ringed by the steep peaks of Tröllaskagi.
  • Hrísey Swimming Pool A little island pool with hot tub and cold plunge on Hrísey in Eyjafjörður.
  • Grímsey Swimming Pool Iceland's northernmost pool, on the Arctic Circle island of Grímsey.
  • Þelamörk Swimming Pool A countryside pool in Hörgárdalur valley near Akureyri.
  • Grenivík Swimming Pool A small outdoor pool overlooking Eyjafjörður beneath Mt. Kaldbakur.
  • Húsavík Swimming Pool Húsavík's town pool with hot tubs and a water slide.
  • Laugar Swimming Pool A historic school pool with hot tubs in Reykjadalur valley, between Goðafoss and Mývatn.
  • Raufarhöfn Swimming Pool A remote pool in mainland Iceland's northernmost village, near the Arctic Henge.
  • Þórshöfn Swimming Pool An indoor pool with hot tubs and steam bath in Þórshöfn by Langanes.
  • Kópasker Swimming Pool Small village pool in Kópasker on the Melrakkaslétta peninsula.
  • Bjórböðin Beer Spa Iceland's original beer spa paired with geothermal hot tubs on Eyjafjörður.
  • Glerárlaug A cosy indoor pool in the Glerá district of Akureyri with hot tubs outside.
  • Illugastaðir An outdoor pool in the Illugastaðir holiday village in Fnjóskadalur, open to all in summer.
  • Lundur í Öxarfirði A pleasant country pool in Öxarfjörður with a hot tub, for all ages.

Attractions

  • Husavik Whale Museum One of the world's few whale museums, in the whale-watching town of Husavik.
  • The Christmas Garden Year-round Christmas cheer in a gingerbread house just south of Akureyri.
  • Akureyri Botanical Garden A beautiful botanical garden in the heart of Akureyri with diverse plants.
  • The Motorcycle Museum of Iceland A museum devoted to the history of motorcycles in Iceland, at Krokeyri in Akureyri.
  • Daladyrd Petting Zoo A family-run petting zoo in Fnjoskadalur valley with diverse animals.
  • Kjarnaskogur Forest Iceland's most-visited forest, with playgrounds and trails by Akureyri.

Local shops & makers

  • Vogafjós A family farm by Lake Mývatn with a cowshed, food and a small shop of local goods.
