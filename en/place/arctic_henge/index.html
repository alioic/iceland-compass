Heimskautsgerðið

A large stone gateway monument inspired by Norse mythology at Iceland's northern edge.

Heimskautsgerðið (the Arctic Henge) is a large circular stone monument at Raufarhöfn on the Melrakkaslétta peninsula, inspired by the poem Völuspá and Norse mythology. Its arches and gateways frame the midnight sun, and the work is still being built — a striking detour north of the Diamond Circle.

Arctic Henge (Heimskautsgerðið) at Raufarhöfn – a mystical monument in North Iceland

The Arctic Henge, or Heimskautsgerðið, is a large circular stone monument set just above Raufarhöfn, one of the northernmost villages in mainland Iceland, at the tip of the Melrakkaslétta peninsula in North Iceland. Inspired by Norse mythology and the poem Völuspá, its gateways and arches reference the dwarves of the Edda. The structure is designed to frame the midnight sun and channel sunlight through its stone archways, functioning almost like a giant sundial. Still being built and expanded over the years, the Arctic Henge has already become one of North Iceland's most striking and photogenic landmarks.

What to see and do at the Arctic Henge

At the Arctic Henge you can walk into the circle itself, stand beneath the towering arches and watch how they draw your eye toward the horizon and sky. Stone gateways, columns and a central point form a kind of cosmic compass linking mythology, the sun and the seasons. It is a magnificent spot for photography, whether in the golden glow of the midnight sun or beneath winter's northern lights. The views over Raufarhöfn, the ocean and the vast Melrakkaslétta are sweeping and serene. Many visitors combine the henge with birdwatching and gentle walks across the wildlife-rich peninsula.

How to get to the Arctic Henge

The Arctic Henge stands on a low hill above the village of Raufarhöfn at the northern edge of the Melrakkaslétta peninsula in Northeast Iceland. From the Ring Road (Route 1) you head north via Kópasker or Þórshöfn and follow Route 85 out onto the peninsula. It is a notable detour north of the main Diamond Circle route, which links Goðafoss, Lake Mývatn, Dettifoss and Ásbyrgi, but many travelers add Raufarhöfn as an adventurous extension. The road is remote yet driveable, so plan fuel and conditions ahead. A short walk from the car park leads up to the monument.

Best time to visit – midnight sun, northern lights and practical tips

The Arctic Henge rewards visitors year-round, but timing shapes the experience. In high summer, around the solstice, the midnight sun hangs low over the horizon and the arches frame it unforgettably, which is when the site is most famous. In winter, under dark, clear skies far from light pollution, it becomes a superb spot for watching the northern lights. Dress warmly, as weather on Melrakkaslétta is changeable and often windy, and remember the site is remote with limited services. Check the forecast and local facilities in Raufarhöfn before setting out on this North Iceland adventure.
