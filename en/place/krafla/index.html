Krafla and Leirhnjúkur – An Active Volcanic System near Lake Mývatn

Krafla is one of Iceland's most active volcanic systems, located just north of Lake Mývatn and part of the popular Diamond Circle route in Northeast Iceland. The area is defined by steaming lava fields, colourful geothermal ground and the green crater lake Víti, formed in a powerful eruption. Here you can still see glowing geothermal activity and lava fields from the Krafla Fires, a prolonged series of eruptions in the late 20th century. The Krafla power station harnesses the geothermal heat for electricity, underlining the raw energy of the site. For travellers, Krafla is a unique chance to experience Iceland's living volcanism up close and accessibly.

Things to See and Do at Krafla

The crater lake Víti, with its deep green water ringed by steep crater walls, is one of the highlights of the Krafla area and easy to walk to. Nearby, trails wind through the colourful geothermal field at Leirhnjúkur, where steaming lava, sulphur scents and boiling ground are reminders that the area is very much alive. The lava fields from the Krafla Fires are still hot in places and give the landscape a dramatic character. The Krafla power station is interesting for seeing geothermal energy in action. This is an ideal stop for anyone fascinated by volcanoes, geothermal activity and the extraordinary geology of the Mývatn region.

How to Get to Krafla

Krafla is in Northeast Iceland, just about 7 kilometres north of Lake Mývatn and close to the Ring Road. From the Ring Road you take a short paved side road north to the car parks at Víti and Leirhnjúkur, making the area very accessible in summer. From Akureyri the drive takes about an hour, and from Mývatn only around fifteen minutes. Krafla fits neatly into the Diamond Circle along with Dettifoss, Ásbyrgi and Goðafoss. In winter the road to Leirhnjúkur can be closed due to snow, so it is wise to check road conditions before setting out.

Best Time to Visit Krafla and Practical Tips

The best time to visit Krafla is from June to September, when the trails at Leirhnjúkur are snow-free and accessible and the weather is most favourable for walking through the geothermal field. In winter you can still reach Víti, but access may be limited. Always stay on the marked paths, as the ground can be hot and dangerous off-trail. Bring good hiking boots, warm and waterproof clothing and drinking water. Combine your visit with Lake Mývatn, the Mývatn Nature Baths and other Diamond Circle sights for an unforgettable day in the volcanic landscapes of North Iceland.
