Grettislaug og Reykjaströnd

Natural hot pools by the sea with views towards Drangey.

On Reykjaströnd by Skagafjörður are Grettislaug and Jarlslaug, stone-built natural hot pools at the water's edge. According to Grettis saga, Grettir swam ashore here from Drangey and warmed himself in the pool. Boat trips out to Drangey also depart from here.

Grettislaug and Reykjaströnd — natural hot pools by Skagafjörður

Grettislaug on the Reykjaströnd shore of Skagafjörður is one of Iceland's most romantic natural hot pools, built of stone right at the water's edge with sweeping views out to the island of Drangey. Beside it lies Jarlslaug, a second geothermal pool, and together they form a peaceful bathing spot in the wild scenery of Northwest Iceland. The pool takes its name from Grettis saga, according to which the outlaw hero Grettir Ásmundarson swam ashore here from Drangey and warmed himself in the hot water. The site blends Icelandic bathing culture, saga history and rugged coastal nature.

Bathing and boat trips — what to do at Grettislaug

The main draw is soaking in the geothermal pools of Grettislaug and Jarlslaug while waves lap the shore and Drangey rises from the fjord. From Reykjaströnd, boat trips also run out to Drangey, the storied bird island where Grettir lived in exile, an unforgettable excursion for lovers of birdlife and the Icelandic sagas. The area is perfect for beach walks, photography and enjoying the low evening sun. Visitors can stay at the campsite and guesthouse accommodation on Reykjaströnd, extending their time at this tranquil corner of northern Iceland.

Getting to Grettislaug on Reykjaströnd

Grettislaug sits at the far end of Reykjaströnd on the western side of Skagafjörður in Northwest Iceland, about a half-hour drive from the town of Sauðárkrókur. The final stretch follows a gravel road out along the coast, so drive carefully and allow extra time. From the Ring Road it is a short turn into Skagafjörður, and the route from there is well signposted. The spot is an ideal stop for travellers touring Northwest Iceland who want to venture a little off the beaten path to natural seaside hot pools with Drangey on the horizon.

Best time to visit Grettislaug and practical tips

Grettislaug is enchanting year-round, but summer and late summer offer the mildest weather, the longest daylight and the safest access along the gravel road. Boat trips out to Drangey run mainly during the summer, so plan your visit accordingly if you want to sail. Bring a towel and swimwear, and remember this is a natural pool with simple facilities; be considerate and leave no trace. The evening and midnight sun make a soak here especially memorable. Combine your visit with other gems of Skagafjörður such as Glaumbær and Tindastóll.
