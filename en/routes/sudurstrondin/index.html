The South Coast

Waterfalls, black sand and glaciers

The South Coast is one of Iceland's most varied and accessible routes, all along the Ring Road east of Reykjavík. Along the way are the Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss waterfalls, the black beach of Reynisfjara near Vík, the plane wreck on Sólheimasandur, the Sólheimajökull glacier tongue, the dramatic Fjaðrárgljúfur canyon, the green oasis of Skaftafell, and finally the crown jewel: the Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon and Diamond Beach. Easily done over 2–3 days.

Iceland's South Coast – Waterfalls, Glaciers and Black Sand Beaches

The South Coast (Suðurströndin) is one of Iceland's most beloved travel routes, following the Ring Road (Route 1) east from Reykjavík to Jökulsárlón. Few drives anywhere pack in such variety: the waterfalls Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss, the Sólheimajökull glacier tongue, the black sand beach Reynisfjara with its basalt columns and Reynisdrangar sea stacks, the village of Vík, the winding Fjaðrárgljúfur canyon, Skaftafell in Vatnajökull National Park, and finally the iceberg-filled Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon and Diamond Beach, where ice chunks sparkle on black sand. You can race the route in one very long day, but one to three days with overnights near Vík or Skaftafell lets you truly experience it. The South Coast is accessible year-round and magical in every season.

South Coast Stops in Driving Order from Reykjavík

From Reykjavík, follow the Ring Road east past Hveragerði and Selfoss. The first major stop is Seljalandsfoss, the waterfall you can walk behind, with the hidden hillside pool Seljavallalaug tucked beneath the Eyjafjöll mountains nearby. Next comes mighty Skógafoss, followed by Sólheimajökull, where guided glacier walks depart onto the ice. On the black expanse of Sólheimasandur lies the famous DC-3 plane wreck, reached on foot. Near Vík, visit Reynisfjara black sand beach with its basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore. East of Vík, the mossy Fjaðrárgljúfur canyon waits near Kirkjubæjarklaustur, then Skaftafell with the Svartifoss waterfall, before the journey culminates at Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon and the adjacent Diamond Beach.

South Coast Practical Tips – Safety, Weather and Avoiding Crowds

The South Coast is drivable year-round on the paved Ring Road, but weather shifts quickly, and winter storms and strong winds are common – check road.is and safetravel.is before and during your trip. The single most important safety note concerns Reynisfjara: the beach is notorious for sneaker waves that surge far up the sand without warning. Keep a generous distance from the water, never turn your back on the ocean, and obey the warning lights and signs. The walk to the Sólheimasandur plane wreck is several kilometres each way across exposed, shelterless sand, so dress accordingly. Only step onto a glacier with a certified guide. Start early to beat the tour buses at Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss; sites further east stay quieter later in the day.

Places on this route

  • Skógafoss og Seljalandsfoss Two magnificent waterfalls at the foot of Eyjafjallajökull.
  • Reynisfjara og Vík Famous black-sand beach with basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks.
  • Sólheimajökull An accessible glacier tongue popular for guided glacier tours.
  • Sólheimasandur A black-sand plain on the South Coast with the wreck of a US DC-3 plane.
  • Fjaðrárgljúfur A dramatic, winding canyon near Kirkjubæjarklaustur.
  • Skaftafell A green oasis in Vatnajökull National Park with Svartifoss and glacier views.
  • Jökulsárlón og Breiðamerkursandur A magical glacier lagoon full of icebergs, with the Diamond Beach alongside.
