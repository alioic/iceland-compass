Ásbyrgi og Dettifoss

Sleipnir's horseshoe-shaped canyon and Europe's most powerful waterfall.

Ásbyrgi is a magnificent horseshoe-shaped canyon, said in folklore to have been formed by the hoof of Sleipnir, Odin's horse. Further south in Jökulsárgljúfur thunders Dettifoss, Europe's most powerful waterfall, along with Selfoss and Hafragilsfoss.

Ásbyrgi and Dettifoss – Jewels of the Diamond Circle in North Iceland

Ásbyrgi is a spectacular horseshoe-shaped canyon in Vatnajökull National Park and one of the signature stops on North Iceland's Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn). Folklore says it formed when Sleipnir, Odin's eight-legged horse, set down a mighty hoof, though geologists credit catastrophic glacial floods in the Jökulsá á Fjöllum river. Sheer cliffs rising as high as a hundred metres enclose a green floor of birch woodland and the tranquil pond Botnstjörn. Just beyond, in Jökulsárgljúfur canyon, thunders Dettifoss, one of Europe's most powerful waterfalls.

What to See and Do in Ásbyrgi and at Dettifoss

Ásbyrgi offers gentle walking trails winding through birch forest to Botnstjörn, where the cliff walls mirror in still water and birdlife flourishes. The viewpoint atop Eyjan, the rock island splitting the canyon, delivers a sweeping panorama. In Jökulsárgljúfur, paths trace the glacial river to Dettifoss, where you feel the ground tremble as the water plunges over the edge. Upstream lies the broad, elegant Selfoss, and downstream the lesser-known Hafragilsfoss. The national park visitor centre shares insight into the area's geology, folklore and hiking routes.

Getting There – Its Place on the Diamond Circle

Ásbyrgi sits in Iceland's northeast, near Route 85 by Kelduhverfi, and together with Dettifoss, Lake Mývatn and Húsavík forms the popular Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn). From Akureyri or Mývatn the route runs smoothly north. Two roads reach Dettifoss: Route 864 on the east side (gravel) and Route 862 on the west side, which is smoother and partly paved. The western approach is generally more accessible, while the eastern bank offers a closer, more intimate view of the falls. Both roads are chiefly passable in summer.

Best Time to Visit and Practical Tips

Summer, from June through September, is the ideal time to explore Ásbyrgi and Dettifoss, when roads are open, the birch is in leaf and daylight lasts around the clock. The roads to Dettifoss, especially gravel Route 864 on the east side, can be closed or difficult outside summer due to snow. Dress for changeable weather, keep to marked trails, and take care near the waterfall edges where spray leaves the rock slick. Sturdy shoes and windproof layers are essential in open, weather-exposed North Iceland (Norðurland).
