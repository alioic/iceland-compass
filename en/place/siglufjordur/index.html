Siglufjörður – Iceland's Former Herring Capital on the Tröllaskagi Peninsula

Siglufjörður is Iceland's northernmost town, a picturesque fishing port tucked beneath steep mountains on the Tröllaskagi peninsula. During the herring boom it was one of the busiest herring capitals in the world, drawing thousands of seasonal workers and boats as the so-called silver of the sea poured ashore. Today that history lives on in the colourful harbour houses, the dramatic mountain backdrop and a quiet but vibrant cultural scene. For travellers exploring North Iceland, Siglufjörður offers an authentic small-town atmosphere, rich maritime heritage and spectacular scenery well away from the busiest tourist routes.

Things to See and Do in Siglufjörður

The Herring Era Museum is the heart of Siglufjörður, one of the largest maritime museums in the Nordic countries, bringing the salting stations and old factory life vividly back to life. The harbour itself, lined with colourful boats and restaurants, is perfect for a relaxed stroll. In winter the Tröllaskagi ski area draws downhill and backcountry skiers, while in summer numerous hiking trails climb into the surrounding mountains and over to neighbouring fjords. With cosy cafés, craft-beer culture and historic streets, Siglufjörður is an inviting place to slow down and soak up genuine Icelandic town life.

How to Get to Siglufjörður

Siglufjörður sits on the northern edge of the Tröllaskagi peninsula, about an hour's drive from Akureyri through the impressive Héðinsfjörður tunnels that link it to Ólafsfjörður and Dalvík. The route forms part of the scenic Tröllaskagi coastal road, a rewarding alternative to the Ring Road when travelling across North Iceland. From Reykjavík the drive takes roughly five to six hours. The road is open year-round, but heavy snowfall is common in winter, so it is wise to check road and weather conditions on the Icelandic Road Administration website before setting out.

Best Time to Visit Siglufjörður and Practical Tips

Siglufjörður welcomes visitors all year round. Summer, from June to August, brings long daylight hours, open museums and the best conditions for hiking across Tröllaskagi. In winter the town becomes a snowy paradise with skiing, mountain scenery and good chances of catching the northern lights. Book accommodation well in advance during peak season and local events. Pack warm, waterproof clothing, as the weather can change quickly, and allow yourself time to enjoy the slower pace of Iceland's northernmost town.
