Jökulsárlón

Iceland's deepest lake, filled with blue icebergs calving from Breiðamerkurjökull.

Jökulsárlón is Iceland's deepest lake and one of the country's most spectacular natural wonders. The lagoon fills with blue and white icebergs that break off Breiðamerkurjökull, an outlet glacier of Vatnajökull, and drift slowly toward the sea. Seals are often seen swimming between the floes or resting on the ice year-round. In summer you can take a boat tour among the icebergs, either on large amphibian boats or faster zodiac craft. The lagoon sits on the Ring Road at the edge of Vatnajökull National Park and is open all year.

Jökulsárlón: Iceland's deepest lake and the glacier lagoon of blue icebergs

Jökulsárlón is a spectacular glacier lagoon in the southeast of South Iceland and the deepest lake in the country. It lies at the foot of Breiðamerkurjökull, an outlet glacier of Vatnajökull, Europe's largest ice cap. The lagoon fills with majestic blue and white icebergs that break off the glacier and drift slowly toward the sea, where they melt or float out into the Atlantic. Often called the crown jewel of South Iceland, it sits on the edge of Vatnajökull National Park. The deep blue ice, mirror-still water and swimming seals make Jökulsárlón unforgettable all year round.

Boat tours on Jökulsárlón: amphibian and zodiac trips among the icebergs

The most popular experience at Jökulsárlón is a boat tour among the icebergs, generally available from spring through autumn when the lagoon is free of ice. Visitors can choose between large amphibian boats, suitable for all ages, or faster zodiac craft that reach deeper into the lagoon and right up to the biggest bergs. On the water you see the ice in every shade of blue and white, and seals often swim close to the boats. Between tours it is rewarding to walk along the shore, watch the icebergs shift with the light and spot the abundant birdlife.

How to get to Jökulsárlón

Jökulsárlón lies in the southeastern part of South Iceland, right on the Ring Road (Route 1) between Skaftafell and the town of Höfn í Hornafirði. From Reykjavík it is around a five-hour drive east along the South Coast, and many travellers make it a two-day trip with an overnight stay near Vík, Kirkjubæjarklaustur or Höfn. Large car parks sit beside the lagoon on both sides of the bridge, giving easy access to the lagoon itself and to Diamond Beach opposite. Organised day tours and coach trips from Reykjavík are also available for those who prefer not to drive.

Best time to visit Jökulsárlón

Jökulsárlón is open year-round and captivating in every season. Boat tours among the icebergs usually run from spring through autumn when the lagoon is ice-free, and summer brings mild weather and bright nights. Winter reveals a frozen lagoon, fewer tourists and the chance of northern lights glowing above the bergs, while seals can be seen in the lagoon throughout the year. Dress warmly against the wind and cold, take care near the water's edge, and allow plenty of time to enjoy both the lagoon and its surroundings, including Diamond Beach just across the Ring Road.
