Gullfoss

The golden waterfall plunges in two tiers into a vast canyon.

Gullfoss is the crown of the Golden Circle — the river Hvítá plunges in two tiers, some 32 m in total, into a narrow canyon. On sunny days a rainbow often forms in the spray. The waterfall was saved from being harnessed for power thanks to the campaign of Sigríður of Brattholt.

Gullfoss in South Iceland: the jewel of the Golden Circle

Gullfoss, or the Golden Falls, is one of Iceland's most majestic and famous waterfalls, located in South Iceland where the glacial river Hvítá plunges in two dramatic tiers into a deep, rugged canyon. It is the crown jewel of the Golden Circle (Icelandic: Gullni hringurinn), the country's most popular sightseeing route, alongside Þingvellir and Geysir. The name, meaning the golden waterfall, is said to refer to the golden hue of the spray in sunshine, where rainbows often arch above the tumbling water. Gullfoss is a powerful symbol of Icelandic nature and one of the most visited attractions in South Iceland (Suðurland) all year round.

What to see and do at Gullfoss

At Gullfoss, visitors can follow pathways and viewing platforms that reveal the waterfall from several angles, both from above and down near the canyon's edge where the roar and spray are most powerful. On sunny days, glorious rainbows frequently form in the mist. Nearby stands a memorial to Sigríður of Brattholt, who campaigned tirelessly against damming the falls in the early twentieth century and helped save them. A visitor centre with a café and shop sits on site, and many travellers combine Gullfoss with the other stops along the Golden Circle for a full day of South Iceland sightseeing.

How to get to Gullfoss

Gullfoss lies in South Iceland, about a one-and-a-half to two-hour drive from Reykjavík along good, well-maintained roads. The most popular way to visit is by driving the Golden Circle, which combines Gullfoss with Þingvellir and the Geysir geothermal area with Strokkur in Haukadalur. A large car park with a visitor centre sits beside the falls, and access is good year-round, though care is needed in winter when paths can be icy. Countless organised day tours and coach trips from Reykjavík drive the Golden Circle, making it easy to visit Gullfoss without driving yourself.

Best time to visit and practical tips for the Golden Circle and Gullfoss

Gullfoss can be visited all year and works perfectly as a Golden Circle day trip from Reykjavík. In summer the flow is powerful, the landscape lush green and the nights bright, while winter brings ice-clad cliffs and snow-covered scenery that make the falls feel magical. Bring windproof and waterproof clothing, as the spray from the waterfall can leave you damp, and wear sturdy shoes for the paths and viewing platforms. Arrive early in the day to avoid the biggest crowds, and allow time to enjoy both the upper and lower viewpoints of this magnificent waterfall in South Iceland.
