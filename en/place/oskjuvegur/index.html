Askja og Dyngjufjöll

Hikes through the lunar landscape of Askja and its surroundings at the heart of the highlands.

Askja and Dyngjufjöll – Hiking Trails Through a Lunar Highland Landscape

Askja and the Dyngjufjöll mountains, deep in the highlands of Northeast Iceland, offer some of the most dramatic and remote hiking in the country. The landscape is so otherworldly and lunar that astronauts once trained nearby for their missions to the Moon. From the hut at Drekagil, both short day walks and challenging multi-day routes lead across Dyngjufjöll and Ódáðahraun, Iceland's vastest desert. Hikers are surrounded by black lava, volcanoes and seemingly endless wilderness. This is a region for those seeking a true wilderness experience, silence and breathtaking geology far from any settlement.

Things to See and Do on the Askja Trails

From the hut at Drekagil, a short but striking walk leads into Askja to the crater lake Víti, where you can bathe in its warm, opal-green water, and on to the deep, majestic Öskjuvatn lake. Longer routes cross Dyngjufjöll and traverse Ódáðahraun, where black lava, sand dunes and volcanoes define the terrain. In the distance rises Herðubreið, the queen of Icelandic mountains. Drekagil itself, with its waterfalls and cliffs, is a beautiful outdoor haven. This is a hike for well-equipped, experienced walkers who want to experience the very heart of the highlands.

How to Get to Askja and Dyngjufjöll

Askja and Dyngjufjöll lie deep in the highlands and are accessible only in summer via rough mountain roads (F-roads) that require a well-equipped, high-clearance four-wheel-drive vehicle. The most common approach is from the area north of Vatnajökull, often along the route that branches off the Ring Road east of Mývatn towards Drekagil. The journey involves crossing unbridged rivers, which can be hazardous. Many travellers prefer to join organised super-jeep tours from Mývatn or Akureyri. Always check road conditions, weather and river levels before setting out.

Best Time to Hike Askja and Practical Tips

The Askja area is accessible only during high summer, typically from July to September, once the mountain roads have opened and the snow has melted. Outside this window the roads are closed and the area is impassable. Bring warm, windproof and waterproof clothing, sturdy hiking boots, food and plenty of water, as there are no services along the way. Stay at the hut in Drekagil or the nearby campsite and plan your trip carefully. Given the remote location and changeable weather, it is wise to travel with experience or on an organised tour, and to share your itinerary with someone.
