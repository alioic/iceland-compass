Goðafoss

The waterfall of the gods, where the pagan gods were cast aside around the year 1000.

Goðafoss is one of the country's most beautiful waterfalls, crescent-shaped and about 12 m high. Its name traces back to the conversion to Christianity around the year 1000, when statues of the pagan gods are said to have been thrown into the falls. Easily accessible right off the Ring Road.

Goðafoss – Waterfall of the Gods on the Diamond Circle

Goðafoss is one of North Iceland's most beautiful and celebrated waterfalls, where the Skjálfandafljót river tumbles over a broad, curving ledge in a graceful crescent shape. Its name means "waterfall of the gods," a reference to Iceland's conversion to Christianity around the year 1000, when the lawspeaker Þorgeir is said to have cast his pagan idols into the falls. A key stop on the famous Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn), Goðafoss draws visitors year-round with its elegant form and rich history, blending natural beauty with cultural heritage in a way few Icelandic waterfalls can match.

Things to See and Do at Goðafoss

Goðafoss offers viewpoints on both banks of the river, letting visitors admire the falls from strikingly different angles. The western bank is reached by a short walk from the car park, while the eastern bank rewards you with a wider panorama of the crescent and the rising spray. Well-trodden paths connect the areas, and the deep roar of the river follows you throughout. Photographers flock here in every season, whether the surroundings are summer green, ablaze with autumn colour, or fringed with winter ice along the churning lip of the waterfall.

Getting to Goðafoss on the Diamond Circle

Goðafoss is beautifully situated right beside Route 1, the Ring Road, between Akureyri and Lake Mývatn, making it an easy stop for almost anyone touring North Iceland. It sits only a short drive from Akureyri and often marks the start or end of a loop around the Diamond Circle, which links Goðafoss with Mývatn, Dettifoss, Ásbyrgi and Húsavík. There is a good car park and easy access on foot. Its convenient location makes Goðafoss an ideal first stop as travellers head into the heart of the north.

Best Time to Visit Goðafoss and Practical Tips

Goðafoss is worth visiting all year round. In summer the site is easily accessible and daylight lasts late into the evening, perfect for photography in soft, golden light. In winter, snow and ice create a magical setting, but the paths can be slippery, so wear warm, waterproof clothing and sturdy footwear. Spray drifting off the falls can soak clothes and cameras, so come prepared. Take care near the edges of the waterfall and stick to the marked trails. Arriving early or late in the day often means quieter viewpoints and better light.
