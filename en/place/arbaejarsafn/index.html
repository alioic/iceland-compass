Árbær Open Air Museum

An open-air museum of old houses showing how life was lived in Reykjavík in times past.

Árbær Open Air Museum, part of the Reykjavík City Museum, is laid out as a small village of historic houses relocated to the site. Visitors walk among the buildings, discover old interiors and crafts, and in summer often meet farm animals and see costumed activities. Daily guided tours run all year round.

A small village from Reykjavík's past

Árbær Open Air Museum in the Árbær district gathers old houses that have been moved together to form a small village. Visitors walk among buildings that show homes, shops and crafts of earlier centuries, gaining insight into how people lived and worked in Reykjavík long ago. Part of the Reykjavík City Museum, it is an enjoyable and educational destination for families who want to experience history in a living way, just outside the city centre.

Live programme and farm animals in summer

In summer Árbær Open Air Museum comes especially alive, when costumed staff practise traditional crafts and farm animals are often on site to delight the youngest visitors. Longer opening hours and a varied programme make the visit a fun day out for the whole family. While summer is the liveliest season, the museum is open all year and offers daily guided tours in Icelandic and English for those who want to learn more.

Opening hours and visiting

Árbær Open Air Museum is open year round, with longer hours in the summer months and shorter hours in winter. Daily guided tours are offered, and it is worth taking time to wander the grounds and soak up the atmosphere. The museum lies in the Árbær district, a few minutes' drive from central Reykjavík, and makes an ideal stop for anyone interested in the city's history and culture in beautiful, peaceful surroundings.
