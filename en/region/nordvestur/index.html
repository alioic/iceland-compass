Northwest Iceland – horses, sagas and wide-open spaces

Northwest Iceland (Norðurland vestra) stretches across the districts of Húnaþing, Skagafjörður and the Skagi peninsula, a land of broad valleys, fertile farmland and rolling mountains. This is the heartland of Icelandic horse breeding and one of the great strongholds of the Icelandic horse, where the medieval saga heritage of the Sturlung Age still feels close at hand. Sauðárkrókur is the largest town and a natural base for travellers keen to leave the crowds behind. Between the highway and the sea lie endless open spaces, glacial rivers pour down from the highlands, and ancient historic sites wait at almost every farm. For anyone seeking calm, nature and living culture, this is authentic rural Iceland.

Top places and highlights in Northwest Iceland

On the Vatnsnes peninsula, Hvítserkur rises fifteen metres out of Húnaflói bay, a basalt sea stack often likened to a drinking dragon, ringed by one of Iceland's largest seal colonies. Skagafjörður is a lush farming district where the glacial rivers Austari- and Vestari-Jökulsá offer the country's finest white-water rafting. The historic site of Hólar in Hjaltadalur served for centuries as a bishopric and centre of learning, while the turf farm at Glaumbær preserves old ways of rural life. In Víðidalur, the river tumbles into Kolugljúfur, a narrow, dramatic canyon named for the troll-woman Kola. Offshore in Skagafjörður stands Drangey, a sheer tuff cliff from Grettir's Saga, teeming with birdlife.

Getting around Northwest Iceland – routes and driving

The Ring Road runs straight across Northwest Iceland, linking the region's main stops, but it pays to turn off it to find the hidden gems. A short detour onto the Vatnsnes peninsula leads to Hvítserkur and its seals, while another route heads inland into Skagafjörður toward Hólar, Glaumbær and the rafting rivers. Most travellers give the region one to two days as they move between West Iceland and Akureyri, though those wanting to raft or ride Icelandic horses should allow more time. Roads are mostly smooth, but some side routes are gravel, so adjust your driving to the conditions and the season.

Best time to visit and practical tips

Summer, from June into September, is the prime season for white-water rafting on the glacial rivers, boat trips out to Drangey and horse-riding tours across the districts, thanks to bright nights and the most accessible roads. In winter the landscape turns quiet and snow-covered, with good chances of seeing the northern lights far from light pollution. Hvítserkur, Kolugljúfur and the museums in Sauðárkrókur are open year-round. Pack for strong winds and sudden changes in weather, fill up on fuel in the larger towns, and book rafting and accommodation early during the peak season. Country guesthouses and farm stays offer a genuine taste of the region.

Places & nature

  • Hvítserkur A 15 m sea stack rising from the sea off the Vatnsnes peninsula.
  • Skagafjörður The heartland of the Icelandic horse and glacial-river rafting.
  • Kolugljúfur A dramatic gorge with a series of waterfalls, named after the troll-woman Kola.
  • Drangey A sheer tuff cliff in Skagafjörður featured in Grettis saga.
  • Hofsós A small village by Skagafjörður with a famous swimming pool and emigration centre.
  • Glaumbær One of the best-preserved turf houses in the country, the Skagafjörður heritage museum.
  • Kálfshamarsvík A striking basalt-column cove with a lighthouse at the tip of the Skagi peninsula.
  • Blönduós A town at the mouth of the Blanda river, known for textiles and a distinctive church.

Hiking trails

  • Tindastóll A long tuff mountain above Skagafjörður with a tarn and folklore.
  • Reykjarhóll An easy walk up the wooded hill of Reykjarhóll with wide views over Skagafjörður.
  • Kerlingarfjall (Kerling) A long, demanding climb to the highest peak of the Tröllaskagi peninsula at 1,538 metres.

Pools & baths

  • Grettislaug og Reykjaströnd Natural hot pools by the sea with views towards Drangey.
  • Sundlaug Hofsóss A celebrated infinity pool perched above Skagafjörður fjord.
  • Sundlaug Sauðárkróks A renovated outdoor pool in the heart of Sauðárkrókur.
  • Hvammstangi Swimming Pool A pleasant outdoor pool in Hvammstangi on Miðfjörður.
  • Blönduós Swimming Pool A splendid modern pool with water slides right on the Ring Road.
  • Skagaströnd Swimming Pool A small friendly pool beneath Spákonufell mountain.
  • Varmahlíð Swimming Pool A popular family pool with two water slides in Skagafjörður.
  • Sólgarðar í Fljótum A country pool at Sólgarðar in Fljót, heated with water from a nearby borehole.

Attractions

  • The Icelandic Seal Center Interactive seal exhibition in the red harbour house at Hvammstangi.
  • Glaumbaer Turf Farm Museum A historic turf farm in Skagafjordur where old rural life comes alive.
  • 1238: The Battle of Iceland Interactive Sturlung-era exhibition with virtual and augmented reality.
  • Brunastadir Petting Zoo A family-run petting zoo in Fljot with many friendly farm animals.

Restaurants

  • Kaffi Krókur A cosy bistro in the heart of Sauðárkrókur.
  • Sjávarborg A restaurant by the harbour in Hvammstangi, above the Seal Center.

Cafés

  • Sauðárkróksbakarí A long-established bakery on the main street of Sauðárkrókur's old town.
  • Húnabúð Kaffihús A cozy café in Blönduós with homemade treats and a gift shop.

Local shops & makers

  • KIDKA Wool Factory One of Iceland's largest wool factories, with a factory shop in the heart of Hvammstangi.
  • Atlantic Leather Europe's only fish-leather tannery, with a visitor centre and shop in Sauðárkrókur.
  • Brúnastaðir Farm A family farm in Fljót with a farm shop, goat cheese and a petting zoo.
  • Langafit Handcraft Market A handcraft shop and coffee house in the village of Laugarbakki by Miðfjörður.
  • Staðarskáli A historic roadside stop and shop on the Ring Road in Hrútafjörður.
