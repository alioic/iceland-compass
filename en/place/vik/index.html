Vík í Mýrdal

Iceland's southernmost village and the service hub of the South Coast.

Vík í Mýrdal is Iceland's southernmost village, a community of around 750 people that serves as the natural service hub of the South Coast — fuel, food, shops and accommodation along a long stretch where little else exists. Above the village stands the white Víkurkirkja church on a hill, with views over the black sand and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks, while the Mýrdalsjökull glacier, which caps the Katla volcano, rises all around. Vík í Mýrdal – the South Coast's service hub

Vík í Mýrdal is Iceland's southernmost village and the only town along a long stretch of the South Coast. Home to around 750 people, it is the natural base for travellers crossing the region: a petrol station, a supermarket, restaurants, cafés and a range of accommodation. For many, Vík is the ideal place to stay and split an otherwise long South Coast trip into two comfortable days.

What to see in Vík

The white Víkurkirkja church stands on a hill above the village, offering a lovely view over the black sand of Víkurfjara beach and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore. Around Vík rises the Mýrdalsjökull glacier, which caps the Katla volcano, and the South Coast's best-known sights are close by — Reynisfjara, Dyrhólaey and the Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss waterfalls. The village also has a wool and craft shop worth a browse.

Good to know

Vík is one of the rainiest places in Iceland, so come prepared for wind and rain. Although Reynisfjara is captivating, treat it with the utmost caution because of its dangerous sneaker waves. From Reykjavík it is about a 180 km drive (just over 2 hours) east along the Ring Road.
