Brimketill og Gunnuhver

A natural pool in the rocks by the surf and the peninsula's most powerful geothermal area.

Brimketill is a natural pool carved into the rock by the waves at the outermost tip of Reykjanes. Nearby is Gunnuhver, the country's largest mud pool, which spews steam. The area also features Reykjanesviti, the country's oldest lighthouse.

Brimketill and Gunnuhver – Natural Wonders on the Reykjanes Peninsula

Brimketill is one of the most distinctive natural sights on the Reykjanes Peninsula, a natural rock pool carved into the lava by the pounding Atlantic surf at the peninsula's western tip. Local folklore says a giantess named Oddný once bathed here. Nearby stands Gunnuhver, Iceland's largest mud hot spring, endlessly steaming and boiling, alongside Reykjanesviti, the country's oldest lighthouse. Together these sites deliver a raw volcanic and geothermal experience in the heart of the Reykjanes UNESCO Global Geopark, just minutes from Keflavík Airport and the town of Grindavík.

What to See and Do at Brimketill and Gunnuhver

A modern viewing platform at Brimketill lets visitors watch waves crash against the cliffs and surge into the pool – a powerful spectacle in rough seas. Swimming is not permitted because of the dangerous surf. At Gunnuhver, boardwalks wind past bubbling mud pots, steam vents and vividly coloured earth. Close by are Valahnúkur, a sheer sea cliff alive with birds, and Reykjanesviti lighthouse. This corner of Reykjanes is ideal for geothermal photography, coastal walks and feeling the elemental force of Iceland's youngest volcanic landscape.

How to Get to Brimketill on the Reykjanes Peninsula

Brimketill and Gunnuhver sit on the southwestern corner of the Reykjanes Peninsula, about a 20-minute drive from Grindavík and under an hour from Keflavík Airport and Reykjavík. Follow the Suðurstrandarvegur and Nesvegur roads to the Brimketill car park; Gunnuhver and Reykjanesviti are just a few minutes further. The sites combine naturally into a Reykjanes loop often paired with the Blue Lagoon and the Fagradalsfjall eruption area. A paved road runs the whole way, and access is good throughout the year.

Best Time to Visit Brimketill and Practical Tips

Brimketill is open all year and free to visit. The most dramatic scene comes when Atlantic swells are high, often in autumn and winter, though the rocks are then slick and hazardous – stay on the viewing platform and never climb down to the pool. Dress in windproof, waterproof layers, as the site is fully exposed to sea winds. Combine your visit with Gunnuhver and Reykjanesviti, and always check for seismic activity and road closures via Icelandic authorities before touring the Reykjanes Peninsula.
