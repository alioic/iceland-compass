Stokksnes og Vestrahorn

A majestic mountain mirrored in a black sand beach — a photographer's dream.

Vestrahorn (Vestitindur) at Stokksnes is one of the most photographed mountains in the country, where the jagged peak is reflected in the wet black sand. The area also features a Viking village film set and views over Hornafjörður.

Stokksnes and Vestrahorn – every photographer's dream

Stokksnes in southeast Iceland is one of the country's most magnificent photography locations, where the jagged Vestrahorn mountain rises straight from a black sand beach along the shore. When the peak mirrors in wet sand and still water, it creates one of Iceland's most sought-after shots. The area also features a Viking village film set and sweeping views over Hornafjörður fjord. For photographers, nature lovers and anyone travelling through East Iceland, Stokksnes is an unforgettable destination where mountains, sea and black sand merge into a single dramatic scene.

Things to see and do at Stokksnes

The main reason to visit Stokksnes is photographing Vestrahorn, where the jagged peak reflects in wet black sand, especially beautiful at sunrise and sunset. Tufts of beach grass and rolling dunes create striking foregrounds for images. The area includes a Viking village set, originally built for a film and now popular with photographers. Beach walks along the shore offer views over Hornafjörður, and seals are often spotted nearby. The land is privately owned, and a small entrance fee goes toward maintaining the site and its facilities.

How to get to Stokksnes

Stokksnes lies just east of Höfn í Hornafirði in southeast Iceland, a short way off Route 1, the Ring Road. From Höfn it is only about a fifteen-minute drive to the turnoff onto the headland, passing a small lighthouse and out to the beach. The road is mostly paved, though the final stretch can be rougher. Höfn makes an ideal base for a visit to Stokksnes, and many travellers combine it with a tour of the Vatnajökull region. Its location makes Stokksnes a convenient stop for anyone travelling the Ring Road through East Iceland.

Best time to visit Stokksnes and practical tips

Stokksnes can be visited year-round, and each season offers its own magic. Summer brings mild weather and long daylight, while autumn and winter deliver more dramatic clouds, snow on the mountain and the chance of northern lights over Vestrahorn. The best photos are often captured in calm conditions at sunrise or sunset, when the peak mirrors in the wet sand. Bring windproof clothing, waterproof shoes for the wet sand and a tripod for photography. Watch the forecast, as wind on the headland can be strong, and respect the marked paths across the site.
