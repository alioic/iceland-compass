Reykjanes Peninsula – fire, geothermal heat and rugged lava

The Reykjanes Peninsula (Reykjanesskagi) is a young, active volcanic region that greets nearly every visitor to Iceland, as Keflavík International Airport sits on the peninsula itself. This raw landscape of jagged lava fields, crater rows and steaming geothermal ground forms part of the Reykjanes UNESCO Global Geopark, one of the few places on Earth where the Mid-Atlantic Ridge rises onto dry land. The area drew global attention when Fagradalsfjall erupted after centuries of dormancy. Despite being so close to the capital, Reykjanes is often underrated – it offers striking geology, empty wilderness and a dramatic coastline just minutes from the terminal, perfect for starting or ending an Iceland trip.

Top places and highlights on Reykjanes

The Blue Lagoon is the peninsula's world-famous jewel – milky-blue, mineral-rich geothermal seawater ringed by jet-black lava, where visitors relax, apply silica masks and dine by the water. At Fagradalsfjall, marked trails lead to the recent lava fields of the 2021–2023 eruptions. At the peninsula's tip, the surf carves Brimketill, a natural pool shaped into the rock, close to Gunnuhver, the country's largest mud hot spring, and Reykjanesviti, Iceland's oldest lighthouse. The colourful Seltún high-temperature area in Krýsuvík bubbles with boiling mud pots beside Lake Kleifarvatn, while at Garðskagi two lighthouses stand watch over a paradise for birdwatching and sunsets.

Getting around the Reykjanes Peninsula

Reykjanes is a small, accessible peninsula that is easy to explore on a loop from Keflavík or Reykjavík in one to two days. The peninsula's main route, along with the coastal roads, links the highlights with short drives between them. Roads are mostly paved and easy to travel year-round, though winds can be strong on open ground and winter ice calls for care. Because the area is so close to the airport, it is ideal to visit on the first or last day of a trip. It is important to check current conditions near active volcanic areas with the Icelandic Met Office, as the terrain can change quickly.

Best time to visit and practical tips

The Reykjanes Peninsula is a year-round destination. Summer, from June to September, is best for hiking to eruption sites and along the coast when the days are long and bright. Winter offers dramatic light, powerful surf and excellent chances for northern lights far from city glow. The Blue Lagoon and other geothermal baths are delightful in every season – book your entry in advance, as demand is high. Wear sturdy hiking boots on the lava, which is sharp and uneven, and stay on marked paths, especially near geothermal ground and active volcanoes. Always check the latest volcanic-activity updates before setting out.

Places & nature

  • Fagradalsfjall The country's newest eruption site, where lava flowed in 2021-2023.
  • Brimketill og Gunnuhver A natural pool in the rocks by the surf and the peninsula's most powerful geothermal area.
  • Krýsuvík og Seltún A colourful high-temperature geothermal area with boiling mud pools and steam vents.
  • Garðskagi Two lighthouses at the peninsula's northernmost tip, a paradise for birdwatchers.

Pools & baths

  • Bláa lónið World-renowned geothermal baths with milky-blue, mineral-rich water set in a lava field.
  • Vatnaveröld A lively family waterworld in downtown Keflavík with an indoor pool and slides.
  • Sundlaugin í Vogum A small, cosy pool at the edge of Vogar, only ten minutes from the airport.

Restaurants

  • Kaffi Duus Well-established restaurant by the small-boat harbour in Keflavík.

Cafés

  • Café Petite Cosy Keflavík café serving hot drinks and desserts.
  • Bryggjan Fisherman's harbour café in Grindavík known for its lobster soup.
