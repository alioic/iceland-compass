Fjaðrárgljúfur

A dramatic, winding canyon near Kirkjubæjarklaustur.

Fjaðrárgljúfur is a canyon up to 100 m deep and about 2 km long, with mossy, winding walls through which the river Fjaðrá meanders. A hiking trail runs along the rim with viewing platforms looking down into the canyon.

Fjaðrárgljúfur – a mossy canyon wonder on the South Coast

Fjaðrárgljúfur is one of Iceland's most photogenic canyons, located near Kirkjubæjarklaustur on the country's South Coast. This serpentine gorge is up to 100 metres deep and about two kilometres long, its towering moss-covered walls carved by the winding Fjaðrá river below. The canyon is thought to have formed at the end of the last Ice Age, when meltwater from retreating glaciers cut down into the soft palagonite bedrock. With its green, sculpted cliffs and twisting river, Fjaðrárgljúfur is one of the most memorable stops along the South Coast and a true paradise for photographers.

What to see and do at Fjaðrárgljúfur

The most popular way to experience Fjaðrárgljúfur is to walk the trail that runs along the upper rim of the canyon. Along the way, several viewing platforms offer breathtaking views down into the gorge and across the meandering river below. The walk is relatively easy and suitable for most visitors, though it is worth taking your time to pause and soak in the scenery. A lower path also lets you venture partway into the canyon itself. Fjaðrárgljúfur is an ideal stop for anyone wanting dramatic South Coast scenery without a long or demanding hike.

Location and getting to Fjaðrárgljúfur

Fjaðrárgljúfur sits on Iceland's South Coast, just west of Kirkjubæjarklaustur and only a short distance off the Ring Road (Route 1). From Reykjavík it is roughly a four-hour drive east along the South Coast, passing Vík í Mýrdal on the way. A gravel road leads from the Ring Road to the car park by the canyon and is usually passable for standard cars during summer. The canyon makes a convenient stop on a South Coast journey, for instance en route to Skaftafell and Jökulsárlón, and pairs well with the region's other natural attractions.

Best time to visit and practical tips for Fjaðrárgljúfur

Fjaðrárgljúfur is open year-round, though access can vary with the season and weather. Summer and autumn offer the best conditions and the greenest mossy walls, while winter visits can be stunning but depend on snow and icy paths. The trail is sometimes closed temporarily to protect the fragile vegetation, so always respect signs and stay on the marked path. Take care near the canyon edge, wear sturdy footwear and be prepared for wind. Arriving early in the day lets you enjoy the canyon in peace before the busiest flow of visitors arrives.
