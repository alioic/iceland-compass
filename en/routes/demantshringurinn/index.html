The Diamond Circle

North Iceland's answer to the Golden Circle

The Diamond Circle is a spectacular loop in Northeast Iceland, often called the north's answer to the Golden Circle. It links Goðafoss waterfall, the volcanic wonderland of Lake Mývatn with its pseudo-craters and Dimmuborgir lava field, the colourful Hverir geothermal field at Námaskarð, the Krafla volcano, the horseshoe canyon of Ásbyrgi and Europe's most powerful waterfall, Dettifoss — ending in the whale-watching town of Húsavík. Add the Mývatn Nature Baths and GeoSea for the perfect day.

The Diamond Circle – North Iceland's Spectacular Sightseeing Loop

The Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn) is North Iceland's answer to the Golden Circle: a roughly 250 km loop connecting some of the country's most dramatic natural wonders. Highlights include the powerful Goðafoss waterfall, the volcanic landscapes of Lake Mývatn with Dimmuborgir and Hverfjall, the boiling mud pools of Hverir at Námaskarð, the Krafla volcanic area, the whale-watching town of Húsavík, the horseshoe-shaped Ásbyrgi canyon and thundering Dettifoss, one of Europe's most powerful waterfalls. Based around Akureyri, Mývatn and Húsavík, the route packs volcanoes, geothermal fields, birdlife and coastal scenery into a compact circuit. It can be driven in one long summer day, but one to two days with an overnight stop lets each site truly shine.

Diamond Circle Stops in Driving Order

Most travellers start the Diamond Circle from Akureyri and drive it clockwise. The first stop is Goðafoss, the "Waterfall of the Gods" on the Skjálfandafljót river. Continue to Lake Mývatn to explore the lava labyrinth of Dimmuborgir and the vast tephra crater Hverfjall, then cross the Námaskarð pass to Hverir, an otherworldly field of bubbling mud pots and steaming vents. A short detour leads to the Krafla volcanic area with the Víti crater. Head north to Dettifoss and the basalt formations of Hljóðaklettar in the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon, then on to the green, horseshoe-shaped Ásbyrgi canyon. Finish in Húsavík, famous for whale watching and the GeoSea geothermal sea baths, before returning along the Tjörnes peninsula towards Akureyri.

Diamond Circle Practical Tips – Roads, Seasons and Timing

The Diamond Circle is at its best from late spring through autumn, when the roads are generally open and daylight is plentiful. Most of the loop is paved, but some stretches around Jökulsárgljúfur are gravel, and winter closures can affect access to Dettifoss – always check current conditions on road.is before setting out. In peak summer, visit Goðafoss and Dettifoss early in the day to stay ahead of the tour groups. Allow unhurried time around Lake Mývatn, and book Húsavík whale-watching tours in advance during high season. At the geothermal areas of Hverir and Krafla, stay strictly on marked paths – the crust can hide scalding ground – and bring layered, windproof clothing, as North Iceland's weather changes fast even in summer.

Places on this route

  • Goðafoss The waterfall of the gods, where the pagan gods were cast aside around the year 1000.
  • Mývatn A volcanic landscape of pseudo-craters, hot springs and natural baths.
  • Hverir við Námaskarð A colourful high-temperature geothermal field with mud pots and fumaroles east of Mývatn.
  • Krafla og Leirhnjúkur An active volcanic system with the crater lake Víti and steaming lava fields.
  • Jarðböðin við Mývatn Milky-blue geothermal baths set in lava fields overlooking Lake Mývatn.
  • Ásbyrgi A horseshoe-shaped canyon with cliffs up to 100 metres, a still pond and birch woodland.
  • Hljóðaklettar Striking basalt rock formations in Vesturdalur within the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon.
  • Húsavík Europe's whale-watching capital with sea baths on the cliff edge.
  • GeoSea Geothermal sea baths on the clifftop overlooking Skjálfandi bay.
  • Tjörnes A peninsula between Skjálfandi and Öxarfjörður, known for its fossils.
