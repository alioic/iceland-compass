Skagafjörður – Land of the Icelandic Horse and Living History

Skagafjörður is a fertile and historic farming region in Northwest Iceland, renowned as one of the great strongholds of the Icelandic horse and horse breeding in the country. Lush valleys, broad riverbanks and majestic mountains surround the fjord, which thrives on agriculture, culture and ancient history. The glacial rivers Austari- and Vestari-Jökulsá flow through the region, offering some of the finest white-water rafting in Iceland. The historic site of Hólar in Hjaltadalur was a bishopric and centre of learning for centuries. For travellers, Skagafjörður uniquely blends rural charm, horsemanship and adventure.

Things to See and Do in Skagafjörður

White-water rafting on the glacial rivers is among the region's headline adventures, ranging from family-friendly floats to demanding canyon runs. Horse riding is a natural fit in this heartland of the Icelandic horse, with trail rides available across the countryside. The turf farm at Glaumbær preserves traditional Icelandic architecture and offers a window into the lives of earlier generations. Hólar in Hjaltadalur, the ancient bishopric and seat of learning, is an essential stop. The island of Drangey and the saga sites of Grettir's Saga are close by, making Skagafjörður a wonderfully varied destination.

Where Is Skagafjörður and How to Get There

Skagafjörður lies in Northwest Iceland, with the ring road, Route 1, running straight through the region, making it easy to reach. The main town is Sauðárkrókur, offering a range of accommodation and services, while there is also settlement at Hofsós and elsewhere around the fjord. From Reykjavík you drive north over Holtavörðuheiði and through Blönduós, while from Akureyri it is a short trip west. Within the region, side roads lead to Hólar, Glaumbær and the rafting launch points, so it is easy to plan a full and varied itinerary.

Best Time to Visit Skagafjörður and Practical Tips

The best time to visit Skagafjörður depends on your interests, but white-water rafting on the glacial rivers is mainly available from June through September, when water levels and weather are suitable. Summer is ideal for horse riding, turf-farm visits and outdoor activities in mild weather and long daylight. Book rafting and riding tours in advance during peak season, as popular trips fill up. Bring warm, waterproof clothing, swimwear for the pools and sturdy shoes. Outside summer the region is quieter but still offers beautiful winter scenery and rich historical sites.
