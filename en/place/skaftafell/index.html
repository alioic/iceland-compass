Skaftafell – a green oasis in Vatnajökull National Park

Skaftafell is one of Iceland's most beautiful natural gems, a lush oasis within Vatnajökull National Park in the country's southeast. The area is framed by glacial tongues flowing from Vatnajökull, Europe's largest ice cap, and boasts unusually rich vegetation, birch woodland and varied wildflower meadows between black sands and white glaciers. Once a national park in its own right, Skaftafell now forms part of Vatnajökull National Park, one of the largest in Europe. The area is renowned for the striking contrasts of glaciers, waterfalls, mountains and greenery, and is among the country's most popular hiking areas for travellers on the South Coast.

Svartifoss and hiking trails in Skaftafell

Skaftafell offers a variety of trails for all ability levels. The most popular leads to Svartifoss, a striking waterfall that tumbles over dark, regular basalt columns that give it its name. Longer and more demanding routes climb to the Sjónarnípa viewpoint and all the way up to Kristínartindar, with sweeping views over glaciers and wilderness. You can also walk to the snout of Skaftafellsjökull and experience the glacier up close. For many, Skaftafell is the highlight of the journey, where glacier views, waterfalls and greenery combine into an incomparable whole within Icelandic nature.

Location and getting to Skaftafell

Skaftafell lies in southeast Iceland, right beside the Ring Road (Route 1) between Kirkjubæjarklaustur and Jökulsárlón. From Reykjavík it is roughly a four-to-five-hour drive east along the South Coast, passing Vík and Kirkjubæjarklaustur. There is easy access to a visitor centre and car park where the trails begin, and you can get information there about routes and conditions. Thanks to its location, Skaftafell is an ideal destination on a longer South Coast journey, often combined with a visit to Jökulsárlón, the Diamond Beach and the other natural gems of Vatnajökull National Park.

Best time, difficulty and tips for Skaftafell

Skaftafell is open year-round, but access and hiking are best from spring through autumn, once the snow has melted and the days are long. The trails are varied, from the short and easy walk to Svartifoss to demanding day hikes up Kristínartindar. In winter the higher routes may be impassable or dangerous due to snow, though guided glacier walks and ice cave tours are then available. Bring windproof and waterproof clothing, warm layers, sturdy boots, food and water. Check the weather and conditions at the park's visitor centre before setting out on longer hikes.
