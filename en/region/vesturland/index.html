West Iceland – mountains, glaciers and saga country

West Iceland (Vesturland) stretches from Hvalfjörður fjord out to the Snæfellsnes peninsula and inland to the fertile Borgarfjörður. Within a relatively small area lies remarkable variety: the Snæfellsjökull glacier crowning its namesake national park, lava caves, clear spring-fed waterfalls, black beaches and the ancient saga sites of Egil's Saga. Snæfellsnes is sometimes called 'Iceland in miniature' because it showcases almost everything the country offers in a single day. Conveniently close to Reykjavík, West Iceland is perfect for travellers who want dramatic scenery, quiet fishing villages and rich cultural history without straying far from the capital.

Top places and highlights in West Iceland

Snæfellsjökull, the mystical glacier immortalised by Jules Verne, presides over the Snæfellsnes peninsula, surrounded by the Vatnshellir lava cave, the black beach of Djúpalónssandur and the fishing villages of Arnarstapi and Hellnar. Kirkjufell by Grundarfjörður is Iceland's most photographed mountain, a perfect cone framed by the Kirkjufellsfoss waterfall. In Borgarfjörður, Hraunfossar streams out from beneath the lava along a kilometre-long bank beside the powerful Barnafoss. The harbour town of Stykkishólmur charms with old timber houses and views over the islands of Breiðafjörður. The accessible Grábrók crater and the tall, hidden Glymur waterfall in Hvalfjörður complete the region's variety.

Getting around West Iceland

The Ring Road passes through Borgarfjörður and links West Iceland with the rest of the country, but the signature experience is a loop around Snæfellsnes along Road 54. From Reykjavík it is a short drive to Borgarnes via the Hvalfjörður tunnel, and the full Snæfellsnes loop can be driven in one long day, though two to three days do the region far more justice. Roads are mostly paved and easy to travel, though some side roads to beaches and waterfalls are gravel. In winter, mountain passes can see snow, so check road conditions. Basing yourself in Borgarnes, Grundarfjörður or Stykkishólmur is ideal for exploring at a relaxed pace.

Best time to visit and practical tips

West Iceland is captivating year-round, though access varies with the season. Summer, from May to September, is best for glacier trips on Snæfellsjökull, cave tours and hiking, when all roads are open. In winter, Kirkjufell and the beaches often shimmer under northern lights and snow-capped peaks, but watch for ice and wind. A glacier hike on Snæfellsjökull should always be done with a guide. Bring good footwear and warm, weatherproof clothing, as coastal weather is ever-changing. Enjoy the Krauma geothermal baths beside Hraunfossar, or visit the nearby Deildartunguhver hot spring. Book accommodation early during the summer season.

Places & nature

  • Snæfellsjökull A mystical glacier at the tip of Snæfellsnes — Jules Verne's inspiration.
  • Kirkjufell Iceland's most photographed mountain, with Kirkjufellsfoss waterfall in the foreground.
  • Hraunfossar og Barnafoss Clear springs streaming out from under a lava field over a kilometre-long bank.
  • Glymur One of the country's tallest waterfalls, hidden in a gorge by Hvalfjörður.
  • Stykkishólmur A beautiful harbour town on Snæfellsnes with old timber houses.
  • Grábrók An accessible scoria crater with steps and views over Norðurárdalur.

Hiking trails

  • Arnarstapi–Hellnar An easy coastal walk along dramatic basalt columns beneath Snæfellsjökull.

Pools & baths

  • Hvammsvík Natural hot pools set right at the tideline of Hvalfjörður, framed by fjord and mountains.
  • Krauma Geothermal baths fed by Deildartunguhver, Europe's most powerful hot spring, in Borgarfjörður.
  • Guðlaug A three-tier bathing pool built into the sea wall at Langisandur beach in Akranes.
  • Landbrotalaug A tiny natural hot pot hidden in the lava on the Snæfellsnes peninsula.
  • Lýsuhólslaug A mineral-rich natural pool with distinctive green water on the south side of Snæfellsnes.
  • Guðrúnarlaug A historic stone pool in Sælingsdalur named after Guðrún Ósvífursdóttir of the Laxdæla Saga.

Restaurants

  • Narfeyrarstofa Seafood in a beautifully restored timber house in Stykkishólmur.
  • Hótel Búðir — veitingar Fine dining at an isolated hotel by black sand and lava.
  • Sjávarpakkhúsið A seafood restaurant with views over Stykkishólmur harbour.
  • Bjargarsteinn Mathús A popular Grundarfjörður restaurant built around Icelandic ingredients.
  • Sker A cosy restaurant in the heart of Ólafsvík with harbour views.

Cafés

  • Geirabakarí Kaffihús A bakery by Borgarfjörður, known from the Walter Mitty film.
  • Blómasetrið - Kaffi Kyrrð A calm, warm café in the old town of Borgarnes.
  • Gilbakki Kaffihús A charming café in one of the prettiest houses in Hellissandur.
  • Fjöruhúsið A family-run café perched on the sea cliffs at Hellnar.
