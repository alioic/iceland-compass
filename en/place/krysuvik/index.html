Krýsuvík og Seltún

A colourful high-temperature geothermal area with boiling mud pools and steam vents.

Seltún in Krýsuvík is an accessible high-temperature geothermal area with wooden boardwalks running past boiling mud pools, sulphur deposits and earth in shades of yellow, red and green. The lake Kleifarvatn lies close by.

Krýsuvík and Seltún – A Colourful Geothermal Field on Reykjanes

Krýsuvík is one of Iceland's most accessible and colourful high-temperature geothermal areas, set in the heart of the Reykjanes Peninsula within the Reykjanes UNESCO Global Geopark. At Seltún the ground boils and simmers in shades of yellow, red and green from sulphur and minerals, while steam vents rise into the air. The site is a vivid testament to the volcanic and geothermal forces that define the peninsula. Nearby lies Kleifarvatn, a deep and mysterious lake, making Krýsuvík a favourite stop on any Reykjanes tour, just half an hour from Hafnarfjörður.

What to See and Do at Krýsuvík and Seltún

At Seltún, raised wooden boardwalks lead safely past boiling mud pots, sulphur deposits and steaming fumaroles, bringing visitors close to the geothermal action. Marked trails climb the surrounding hills for sweeping views over the field and toward Kleifarvatn. Grænavatn, an explosion crater with a distinctive green hue, lies close by, and birdlife adds to the setting. Krýsuvík is perfect for geothermal photography, a short nature walk and learning about Reykjanes geology, whether on a day trip from Reykjavík or a full loop of the peninsula.

How to Get to Krýsuvík on the Reykjanes Peninsula

Krýsuvík and Seltún lie in the southern part of the Reykjanes Peninsula, about a 30-minute drive from Hafnarfjörður and under an hour from both Reykjavík and Keflavík Airport. Take the Krýsuvíkurvegur road (Route 42) past Kleifarvatn to the well-signed car park at Seltún. The road is paved and open year-round, though winter conditions can vary. Krýsuvík pairs naturally with a Reykjanes loop alongside Kleifarvatn, Grænavatn and the Fagradalsfjall eruption area, and suits both quick stops and longer day excursions.

Best Time to Visit Krýsuvík and Practical Tips

Seltún in Krýsuvík is open all year and free of charge. Summer and autumn offer the best light and colour for photography, but the area is equally striking in winter when steam rises through the frost. Always stay on the marked boardwalks and trails – the ground is scalding hot and dangerous off-path. Wear sturdy shoes and windproof, waterproof clothing. Combine your visit with Kleifarvatn and Grænavatn, and keep an eye on conditions and any closures related to seismic activity on the Reykjanes Peninsula.
