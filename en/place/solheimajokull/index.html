Sólheimajökull

An accessible glacier tongue popular for guided glacier tours.

Sólheimajökull is an outlet glacier from Mýrdalsjökull, one of the most accessible glaciers in the country and popular for guided glacier hikes and ice climbing. The glacier clearly shows the effects of climate change as it retreats year after year.

Sólheimajökull – the most accessible glacier on the South Coast

Sólheimajökull is one of Iceland's most popular and accessible outlet glaciers, a glacial tongue reaching down from the Mýrdalsjökull ice cap on the South Coast. The glacier is known for its dramatic landscape of ice towers, crevasses, ash-streaked ridges and deep blue ice, making it a perfect place for glacier hikes and ice climbing. Sólheimajökull is also a striking example of climate change, as its snout visibly retreats year after year and a lagoon has formed at its edge. Its closeness to the Ring Road makes it one of the country's favourite glacier stops for South Coast travellers.

Glacier hiking and ice climbing on Sólheimajökull

The most popular activity on Sólheimajökull is a guided glacier hike, where visitors strap on crampons and walk across the glacier's otherworldly landscape. Experienced guides lead groups through crevasse fields, past ice towers and blue ice formations, sharing the geology and history of the glacier along the way. More seasoned adventurers can also try ice climbing on its steeper walls. Glacier tours on Sólheimajökull suit both beginners and the more experienced, but you should always travel with a certified guide and proper equipment, as the glacier is ever-changing and can be dangerous without expert guidance.

Where is Sólheimajökull and how to get there

Sólheimajökull lies on the South Coast, between the hamlet of Skógar and Vík í Mýrdal, and is easily reached along the Ring Road. From Reykjavík it is about a two-and-a-half-hour drive east, passing Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss on the way. A signed side road (Route 221) leads off the Ring Road to a car park near the glacier snout, from where a short walk brings you to the ice. Thanks to its proximity to the South Coast's other main attractions, Sólheimajökull is an ideal stop on a longer journey through the region, whether on a day trip or an extended tour.

Best time to visit Sólheimajökull

Sólheimajökull can be visited year-round, but glacier tours are always run under guidance regardless of the season. Summer offers mild weather and long days, while winter often brings deeper blue tones to the ice and the chance to explore nearby ice caves. Dress in warm, windproof and waterproof layers, wear sturdy hiking boots, and always follow your guide's instructions. Never walk onto the glacier on your own, as crevasses and shifting ice can be life-threatening. Book glacier tours in advance, especially during the busy summer months when demand is at its highest.
