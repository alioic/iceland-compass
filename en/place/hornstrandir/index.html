Hornstrandir – a pristine nature reserve at the northern tip of the Westfjords

Hornstrandir is one of Iceland's greatest wilderness areas, a nature reserve at the northernmost part of the Westfjords where no one has lived permanently since the mid-20th century. The region is entirely without roads and reachable only on foot or by boat, which has preserved its untouched and rugged nature. Here you find some of the steepest bird cliffs in the country, deep fjords, abandoned settlements and lush yet wild vegetation. Hornstrandir is a dream destination for hikers and adventurers seeking solitude, silence and pristine Icelandic nature far from any services or crowds.

Things to see and do in Hornstrandir

In Hornstrandir the Arctic fox is unusually tame, being protected and unafraid of people, offering a rare chance to observe the reserve's signature animal in its natural surroundings. Hornbjarg and Hælavíkurbjarg are among the grandest and highest bird cliffs in Iceland, alive with seabirds in summer. Hikers follow ancient paths between abandoned settlements, where old houses and hayfields recall a way of life now gone. Multi-day hiking trips, wild camping and the experience of complete stillness are at the heart of a visit to Hornstrandir.

Getting to Hornstrandir – location and access

Hornstrandir lies at the northernmost tip of the Westfjords and is accessible only by boat, as no roads lead into the area. Boat trips are organised from Ísafjörður and nearby settlements during the summer months, carrying hikers to key starting points such as Hesteyri and Hornvík. The journey itself is an adventure, offering unique views over the Jökulfirðir fjords and the coastline. Since there are no services in the area, visitors must be entirely self-sufficient, carry all their gear and plan their trip carefully in advance with boat operators and park rangers.

Best time to visit Hornstrandir and practical tips

Hornstrandir is only accessible in high summer, mainly July and August, when boats run and the snow has cleared from the hiking trails. Outside this window the area is virtually impassable and no services are available. As the weather can change quickly and conditions are demanding, thorough preparation is essential: proper gear, warm and waterproof clothing, a good tent and ample supplies. Register your travel plan, respect the reserve's rules and tread lightly on the fragile nature. Hiking and navigation experience matter, as this is one of the most remote and untouched wilderness areas in the country.
