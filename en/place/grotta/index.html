Grótta

A lighthouse at the tip of Seltjarnarnes, popular for birdlife and the northern lights.

Grótta is a small island and lighthouse at the outermost point of Seltjarnarnes, connected to the mainland at low tide. The area is a bird reserve and one of the best northern-lights spots near the city. By the shore lies the warm foot bath Kvika.

Grotta – Lighthouse and Nature Reserve on the Tip of Seltjarnarnes

Grotta is a small island and lighthouse at the very tip of the Seltjarnarnes peninsula, just a stone's throw from Reykjavik yet surrounded by sea and open space. The area is a protected bird reserve and one of the greater Reykjavik area's best-kept secrets for the outdoors and natural beauty. The Grotta lighthouse stands on an island linked to the mainland at low tide, ringed by a shore rich with life. Grotta is especially popular for northern lights viewing thanks to low light pollution, and offers sweeping views over Faxafloi bay and the mountains.

Top Things to See and Do at Grotta

At Grotta, nature takes centre stage. Visitors walk out to the lighthouse at low tide, watch the varied birdlife of the reserve and soak up the calm at the water's edge. On the shore is Kvika, a small geothermal foot bath and a beloved little landmark where you can warm your feet and gaze out to sea. On dark winter evenings, Grotta is one of the best northern lights spots near Reykjavik. The location is also ideal for photography, watching the sunset and relaxed coastal walks.

Getting to Grotta – Location and Access

Grotta lies at the outer tip of Seltjarnarnes, only about a 10–15 minute drive from central Reykjavik, making it remarkably easy to reach. There is a car park by the site, and from there it is a short walk into the reserve. Straeto buses serve Seltjarnarnes, and many people cycle or walk along the shore from the city. It is important to check the tide table before setting out to the lighthouse, as the causeway to the island is passable only at low tide and closes when the tide comes in. Take your time and watch the sea.

Best Time to Visit Grotta and Practical Tips

Grotta is captivating all year. In winter, on clear dark nights, it is among the city's best northern lights spots, while during the spring and summer nesting season part of the reserve is closed to protect the birdlife – respect the signage. Summer evenings under the midnight sun and autumn sunsets are unforgettable. Always check the tide times before walking out to the lighthouse so you don't get cut off. Dress warmly against the wind, bring a camera, and enjoy a tranquillity that is rare so close to the capital.
