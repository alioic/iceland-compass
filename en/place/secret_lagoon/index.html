The Secret Lagoon (Gamla laugin) – Iceland's oldest swimming pool

Gamla laugin in Flúðir, widely known as the Secret Lagoon, is Iceland's oldest swimming pool and one of its most authentic. Built in 1891, this natural geothermal bath has retained its charm and genuine character. Bathers relax in mineral-rich water at around 38 to 40 degrees, surrounded by small hot springs that erupt regularly nearby. Unlike the country's larger, more polished spas, the Secret Lagoon offers a calm, rustic and personal experience, where steam drifts across the water's surface and the history of Icelandic bathing culture feels almost within reach.

What to experience at the Secret Lagoon

At the Secret Lagoon, visitors can relax in the warm geothermal water and enjoy the peace and natural surroundings. A small walking path circles the pool along the geothermal field, where you can see bubbling hot springs and steam vents, including a little geyser that erupts from time to time. The facilities are simple but comfortable, with changing rooms and a small café. The bath suits families, couples and anyone wanting to experience genuine Icelandic bathing culture in a tranquil setting. The Secret Lagoon is a wonderful way to unwind after a long day exploring the Golden Circle.

Location and getting to the Secret Lagoon

The Secret Lagoon is located in the village of Flúðir in South Iceland, at the heart of the Golden Circle. From Reykjavík it is roughly a one-and-a-half-hour drive along good roads, and the site sits conveniently close to the region's other natural attractions such as Geysir and Gullfoss. This makes the Secret Lagoon a perfect stop for anyone wanting to combine a soak with a loop around South Iceland's main sights. There is ample parking by the lagoon and easy access, making a visit straightforward whether you travel by car or on an organised tour.

Best time to visit the Secret Lagoon

The Secret Lagoon is open year-round and offers a soothing experience in any weather. Winter is especially magical, lying in the warm water as snowflakes fall and the northern lights sometimes dance overhead, while summer brings long, bright evenings. It is worth arriving outside the busiest hours, early in the day or in the late afternoon, to enjoy the pool in greater peace. Bring a swimsuit and towel, though both can also be rented on site. Booking ahead is recommended during peak season to secure your entry.
