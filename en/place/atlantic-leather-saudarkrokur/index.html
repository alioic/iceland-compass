Atlantic Leather

Europe's only fish-leather tannery, with a visitor centre and shop in Sauðárkrókur.

Atlantic Leather tans the skins of salmon, wolffish, cod and perch, turning a food-industry by-product into sought-after fashion leather. At the Gestastofa visitor centre beside the tannery you can view the process and buy fish-leather goods along with jewellery and accessories from Icelandic designers. The material has reached houses such as Dior and Prada.

Fish-leather tannery in Sauðárkrókur

Atlantic Leather, formerly known as Sjávarleður, is the only tannery in Europe that turns fish skin into leather. The company is based in Sauðárkrókur in Skagafjörður and uses skins that would otherwise be discarded by the food industry. The leather comes from salmon, wolffish, cod and perch, each with its own texture and colour. Its products are sought after by well-known fashion houses and show how Icelandic raw material can become valuable design.

The tanner's visitor centre

Beside the factory, Atlantic Leather runs a visitor centre where guests can learn about the tanning process and buy goods directly. On offer are wallets, bags, jewellery and accessories made of fish leather, along with pieces from Icelandic craftspeople and designers. It is a fascinating stop for anyone wanting a distinctive souvenir with a story. Sauðárkrókur is the largest town in Skagafjörður and a convenient base in Northwest Iceland.

Sustainable design from the sea

Fish leather is a fine example of sustainable use, where a by-product becomes something of value. Atlantic Leather has refined the method over decades and sells the leather both to international fashion houses and to locals who craft beautiful objects from it. For travellers in Skagafjörður, a visit to the centre is a way to support local craft and better understand the cycle of Icelandic seafood. The products are light, strong and unmistakable.
