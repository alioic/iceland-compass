Simbahöllin

Café and cultural house in a restored Norwegian building in Þingeyri.

Simbahöllin is a homely café in a beautifully restored 1915 Norwegian house in the middle of the fishing village of Þingeyri. A Danish-Belgian couple opened it in 2009, and its Belgian waffles with homemade rhubarb jam quickly became famous. The house also serves as a cultural hub with concerts, events and horse riding.

A café with soul in Þingeyri

Simbahöllin is one of the best-known cafés in the Westfjords, set in a beautifully restored 1915 Norwegian house in the heart of the village of Þingeyri. A Danish-Belgian couple opened it in 2009 and have since made it a cultural focal point in Dýrafjörður. The café serves homemade baking, soup, light dishes and its celebrated Belgian waffles. For travellers in the southern Westfjords, Simbahöllin is a must-stop where local produce and hospitality come together.

The waffles and the food

Simbahöllin's Belgian waffles, served with homemade rhubarb jam and cream, became famous in their very first summer. Alongside them the menu offers homemade cakes, soup, light meals and good coffee. The emphasis is on homemade fare in the cosy setting of the old house. The café is open through the summer, and the atmosphere is relaxed and warm. It is worth checking opening hours before visiting, especially outside high season when the schedule can vary.

Culture and life in Dýrafjörður

Simbahöllin is more than a café; the owners have turned the house into a cultural hub with concerts, art shows and events, and they also offer horse riding in the Westfjords Alps. In this way the place combines food, arts and the outdoors in one spot in a small fishing village. For those travelling through Dýrafjörður who want to experience local produce and a sense of place, it is an ideal destination. The house itself, seen in the film Nói albínói, has a long and notable history.
