Fagradalsfjall – Iceland's Newest Volcanic Area on the Reykjanes Peninsula

Fagradalsfjall on the Reykjanes Peninsula captured global attention when it erupted in 2021 after centuries of dormancy, with further eruptions following through 2021–2023. The area became a symbol of renewed volcanic activity on Reykjanes, part of the UNESCO Global Geopark, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors who witnessed a new lava field forming. Fagradalsfjall is a rare chance to experience Iceland's geology in real time – a young, active volcanic zone where the earth itself is being shaped before your eyes.

What to See and Do at Fagradalsfjall

A recent lava field blankets the valley at Fagradalsfjall, and marked trails lead to the eruption sites and to Meradalir, where part of the lava flowed. Viewpoints and built-up paths let visitors take in fresh, hardened lava and geological formations up close. The hike to the area is an experience in itself – across the rugged Reykjanes landscape with wide views over the peninsula. Geology, photography and the sheer sense of nature's power make the visit unforgettable, but conditions can change and all signage must be respected.

Getting to Fagradalsfjall – Location and Access

Fagradalsfjall lies on the Reykjanes Peninsula, near Grindavik and not far from Keflavik International Airport and the Blue Lagoon. From Reykjavik it is roughly a 40–50 minute drive to the car parks by the trailheads, from where you hike to the eruption sites along marked paths. The walk can take several hours and requires good gear. As this is an active volcanic area, it is essential to always check current conditions with the Icelandic Met Office and civil protection before setting out, as access can close at short notice.

Best Time to Visit Fagradalsfjall and Practical Tips

The best time to hike to Fagradalsfjall is during the summer months, June to September, when daylight is long and conditions are milder – though access always depends on the situation. Dress in warm, wind- and waterproof layers, bring sturdy hiking boots, water and snacks, and expect changeable weather. Keep a close eye on official announcements about gas pollution, seismic activity and closures, and stay within the marked trails. Respect for nature's power and good preparation are the keys to a safe and memorable visit.
