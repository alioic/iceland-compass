Hljóðaklettar

Striking basalt rock formations in Vesturdalur within the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon.

Hljóðaklettar (the Echo Rocks) are a cluster of dramatic basalt columns in Vesturdalur within the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon. The rock forms twisted, swirling columns that create an unusual echo, and short walking trails lead through the area — a popular stop on the Diamond Circle.

Hljóðaklettar – The Basalt Marvel of the Diamond Circle

Hljóðaklettar, whose name means "Echo Rocks," is a remarkable cluster of basalt columns and rock formations in Vesturdalur within Jökulsárgljúfur canyon in North Iceland (Norðurland). The name comes from the way sound from the river seems to rebound from unexpected directions among the cliffs. Here ancient volcanic activity and river erosion have shaped swirling, twisted and radiating columns into a rare labyrinth of stone. Hljóðaklettar is one of the Diamond Circle's (Demantshringurinn) hidden gems, prized by travellers drawn to unusual geology and quiet away from the busiest sites.

What to See and Do at Hljóðaklettar

Hljóðaklettar offers short, accessible walking trails that thread between basalt pillars displaying astonishing swirled and contorted patterns. The best known is Kirkjan ("the Church"), a cave-like, vaulted formation where the columns fan out in every direction like a natural chapel. Visitors can stroll the site, photograph the intricate forms, and soak up the calm beside the glacial river Jökulsá á Fjöllum. Nearby in Vesturdalur stand the colourful Rauðhólar crater remnants, making this a varied destination for hikers and anyone fascinated by volcanic geology.

Getting to Hljóðaklettar on the Diamond Circle

Hljóðaklettar lies in Vesturdalur, on the west side of Jökulsárgljúfur canyon in Vatnajökull National Park, and makes an ideal addition to a Diamond Circle (Demantshringurinn) tour in North Iceland. Access is via Route 862 and then a gravel side road into Vesturdalur. The site sits between Dettifoss and Ásbyrgi, so it slots easily into the loop. The road is best suited to ordinary cars in summer; outside the summer season it may be impassable. From the car park it is a short walk to the main rock formations.

Best Time to Visit and Practical Tips

Hljóðaklettar is at its finest in summer, from June into September, when Route 862 and the Vesturdalur side road are passable and the trails are dry. Dress for the weather, as the open ground beside Jökulsá can be windy, and wear sturdy footwear since paths can be rocky and uneven. Allow time to walk slowly and listen for the echoes that give the rocks their name. Respect the landscape, keep to the marked trails, and carry all litter back out with you.
