Dettifoss: Europe's most powerful waterfall

Dettifoss, on the glacial river Jökulsá á Fjöllum, is regarded as the most powerful waterfall in Europe by volume of water. It is around 44 metres high and about 100 metres wide, and its grey glacial water and thunderous roar make it deeply impressive. The river's average flow over the falls is roughly 193 cubic metres per second, rising sharply during the summer melt. Dettifoss lies within Jökulsárgljúfur in Vatnajökull National Park and is one of Northeast Iceland's defining landmarks. Take care on the wet, slippery rocks near the edge.

Selfoss and Hafragilsfoss near Dettifoss

Dettifoss does not stand alone, as the same river holds two more striking waterfalls within easy reach. About a kilometre upstream lies Selfoss, a broad, multi-tiered waterfall that spills in graceful arcs over the rock ledges. Downstream sits Hafragilsfoss, where the river plunges into a deep, colourful canyon. Together the three falls form a spectacular sequence along Jökulsárgljúfur. Walking trails connect them, particularly on the east bank, letting visitors experience the river's power from several vantage points. Allow plenty of time to take in all three waterfalls properly.

Reaching Dettifoss: Road 862 and Road 864

Dettifoss can be viewed from two banks with quite different access. The west bank is reached by the paved Road 862 off the Ring Road, with car parks, toilets, a maintained path and a viewing platform over the falls. The east bank is reached by the gravel Road 864, which is rougher and best suited to four-wheel-drive vehicles. Road 862 is usually passable from around April to December, while the gravel Road 864 is mainly open in summer and closes once snow arrives. In deep winter access can be limited, so check road conditions.

Visiting Dettifoss: seasons and where to stay

Dettifoss is one of the highlights of the Diamond Circle and lies a little over an hour's drive from Mývatn and Húsavík. From the car parks a short walk leads to the viewing platforms above the falls, about 1.5 kilometres each way on the west bank. Summer is the best time to visit, when both roads are open and the flow is at its strongest. There is no accommodation at the falls themselves, but a range of hotels, guesthouses and campsites can be found around Mývatn and in Húsavík. Dress warmly against the cold spray and wind.
