Skógafoss og Seljalandsfoss

Two magnificent waterfalls at the foot of Eyjafjallajökull.

Skógafoss is 60 m high and 25 m wide — you can climb steps to a viewing platform and continue on to Fimmvörðuháls. Seljalandsfoss is unique because you can walk behind the waterfall. Both lie along the ring road beneath Eyjafjallajökull.

Skógafoss and Seljalandsfoss – majestic waterfalls on the South Coast

Skógafoss is one of Iceland's best-known and most impressive waterfalls, sixty metres high and twenty-five metres wide, plunging over an ancient sea cliff line beneath the Eyjafjallajökull glacier. Together with its neighbour Seljalandsfoss, which you can walk behind, it forms one of the most popular waterfall pairs on the South Coast. Both falls lie right beside the Ring Road and are essential stops on any journey through South Iceland. The power of the water, the drifting spray and the lush surroundings make Skógafoss one of the most photographed and memorable destinations in the country.

Things to see and do at Skógafoss

At Skógafoss you can walk right up to the base of the falls and feel the spray, or climb the staircase to a viewing platform at the top with sweeping views over the South Coast. From there the famous Fimmvörðuháls hiking trail continues into the highlands between two glaciers. Nearby, Seljalandsfoss lets visitors walk behind the cascade, while its hidden neighbour Gljúfrabúi lurks inside a narrow canyon. On sunny days rainbows often form in the mist, making the area a true paradise for photographers and hikers alike.

Location and how to reach Skógafoss

Skógafoss stands by the small settlement of Skógar beneath the Eyjafjöll mountains on the South Coast, about two hours' drive from Reykjavík along the Ring Road. The waterfall is just a few minutes' walk from the car park and is accessible year-round by regular car. Seljalandsfoss lies a few kilometres to the west on the same road, so it is easy to see both falls on the same trip. Skógafoss features in most organised day tours of the South Coast and is equally well suited to travellers exploring independently by car.

Best time to visit Skógafoss and practical tips

Skógafoss is spectacular all year round. Summer brings the greenest surroundings and the easiest access, while winter can adorn the falls with an icy fringe and make them even more dramatic in the snow. Be prepared to get wet from the spray as you approach the base, and dress in waterproof layers and good hiking shoes, especially if you plan to climb the stairs or set off on Fimmvörðuháls. Arrive early in the day to avoid the biggest crowds and enjoy the calm. Take care on the staircase in winter, as the steps can become slippery.
