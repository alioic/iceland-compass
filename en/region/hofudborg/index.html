The Capital Region – Iceland's beating heart by the sea

The Capital Region (Höfuðborgarsvæðið) is Iceland's most densely populated area, home to roughly two-thirds of the nation. Here vibrant city life, art and history meet the coastline and a ring of mountains, with Mount Esja rising across Faxaflói bay from Reykjavík. As the world's northernmost capital, Reykjavík blends small-town warmth with colourful houses, a thriving café and dining scene and a lively nightlife. It is the natural starting point for any Iceland trip: every route into the country begins here, whether the Golden Circle, the South Coast or the road north. For travellers who want culture, design and history alongside easy access to nature, the Capital Region is an ideal year-round base.

Top places and highlights in the Capital Region

In Reykjavík, Hallgrímskirkja church towers over downtown, its viewing platform revealing a sea of colourful rooftops. Harpa Concert Hall shimmers by the old harbour, the Sun Voyager sculpture reaches toward the fjord, and Laugavegur shopping street buzzes with life. Perlan on Öskjuhlíð hill offers a viewing deck and exhibitions on Icelandic nature. Across the water lies Viðey, a peaceful historic island with the country's oldest stone house and Yoko Ono's Imagine Peace Tower. On the Seltjarnarnes peninsula, Grótta lighthouse and nature reserve is one of the best northern-lights spots near the city, complete with the warm Kvika foot bath. On the city's edge, the Heiðmörk recreation area offers the red Rauðhólar craters, woodland and walking trails.

Getting around the Capital Region

The Capital Region is compact and easy to explore; most of downtown's highlights can be covered on foot in one to two days. City buses link the neighbourhoods, and rental cars are widely available for onward travel into the countryside. Keflavík International Airport is roughly a forty-to-fifty-minute drive from the city centre. Roads are well paved and easy to drive year-round, though winter weather calls for caution. The city makes an excellent base for day trips, with the Golden Circle, the Reykjanes Peninsula and the South Coast all within easy reach. Allow two to three days to enjoy both the city and its surroundings at a relaxed pace.

Best time to visit and practical tips

The Capital Region welcomes visitors all year. Summer, from June to August, brings long bright days, outdoor concerts, festivals and milder weather – perfect for walking and city life. Winter has its own charm, with festive lights, open-air geothermal pools and good chances of northern lights on clear nights; Grótta and Heiðmörk are among the best spots to catch them near the city. Dress in layers and always carry a windproof, waterproof outer shell, as the weather changes quickly. The public swimming pools are the heart of local life and an affordable, authentic experience. Book downtown accommodation well ahead during the busy summer season.

Places & nature

  • Reykjavík The world's northernmost capital, buzzing with culture, design and nightlife.
  • Viðey A tranquil island in the sound with history, artworks and Yoko Ono's Imagine Peace Tower.
  • Grótta A lighthouse at the tip of Seltjarnarnes, popular for birdlife and the northern lights.
  • Heiðmörk The capital's largest recreation area, with woodland and lava formations.

Hiking trails

  • Esjan The city mountain — the most popular day hike among capital residents.
  • Helgafell (Hafnarfjörður) A popular tuya above Hafnarfjörður with a short but steep climb to the summit.
  • Úlfarsfell An accessible local hill between Reykjavík and Mosfellsbær with fine views over the city.
  • Búrfellsgjá and Búrfell A family-friendly walk along a dramatic lava channel from the Heiðmörk parking area up to the Búrfell crater.
  • Vífilsfell The highest mountain near Reykjavík, with a steep, rocky final scramble to the 655 m summit.

Pools & baths

  • Sky Lagoon An upscale oceanfront geothermal lagoon in Kópavogur with an infinity edge dissolving into the North Atlantic.
  • Laugardalslaug Iceland's largest swimming complex in the Laugardalur valley, packed with hot pots and slides for the whole family.
  • Sundhöll Reykjavíkur Reykjavík's oldest pool, a 1937 functionalist landmark in the heart of downtown.
  • Vesturbæjarlaug A beloved neighbourhood pool in Vesturbær, famous for its laid-back atmosphere and excellent hot pots.
  • Nauthólsvík Geothermal Beach A man-made warm beach where geothermal water is fed into a sheltered cove to heat the sea.
  • Árbæjarlaug A family-friendly pool in eastern Reykjavík overlooking the green Elliðaárdalur valley.
  • Álftaneslaug Home to Iceland's only wave pool, out on the Álftanes peninsula in Garðabær.
  • Sundlaug Seltjarnarness A calm neighbourhood pool on Seltjarnarnes that blends geothermal water with mineral-rich seawater.
  • Lágafellslaug A popular family pool in Mosfellsbær with three slides and plenty of hot pots.
  • Breiðholtslaug Spacious neighbourhood pool in Breiðholt with outdoor lanes, hot tubs and a slide.
  • Grafarvogslaug Popular family pool in the Grafarvogur suburb with a slide and great kids' facilities.
  • Dalslaug Reykjavík's newest pool in Úlfarsárdalur, famous for its big water slides.
  • Klébergslaug Small, cosy countryside pool on Kjalarnes with views over the bay.
  • Sundlaug Kópavogs Kópavogur's large main pool with a 50 m outdoor pool and water slides.
  • Salalaug Bright, modern pool in the Salir district with both indoor and outdoor areas.
  • Ásgarðslaug Garðabær's main pool at Ásgarður with an outdoor pool, hot tubs and a slide.
  • Sundhöll Hafnarfjarðar Historic 1943 swim hall by the sea in central Hafnarfjörður.
  • Suðurbæjarlaug Popular outdoor pool in south Hafnarfjörður with a slide and hot tubs.
  • Ásvallalaug One of Iceland's largest indoor pools, with a 50 m pool and kids' area.
  • Varmárlaug Mosfellsbær's older, quieter pool at the Varmá sports grounds.
  • Kvika Foot Bath A tiny geothermal foot bath on the shore by Grótta.

Attractions

  • Reykjavík Family Park and Zoo Farm animals, native Icelandic wildlife and rides in Laugardalur in the heart of Reykjavík.
  • Whales of Iceland Europe's largest whale exhibition, with life-size whale models at the Old Harbour.
  • Perlan – Wonders of Iceland Interactive nature exhibition, a man-made ice cave and an observation deck on Öskjuhlíð hill.
  • Árbær Open Air Museum An open-air museum of old houses showing how life was lived in Reykjavík in times past.
  • FlyOver Iceland A flight-simulation ride in the Grandi district that lets you soar over Iceland's landscapes.
  • Aurora Reykjavík A northern lights centre in the Grandi district where you can learn about and experience the aurora all year round.

Restaurants

  • Sægreifinn The little seaside shack by the harbour, famous for its lobster soup.
  • Kaffivagninn Said to be Iceland's oldest operating restaurant, by the old harbour.
  • Dill Renowned fine-dining restaurant focused on New Nordic cuisine.
  • Grillmarkaðurinn Award-winning downtown restaurant with a focus on grilled dishes.
  • Fiskmarkaðurinn Well-known restaurant blending Icelandic seafood with Asian influences.

Cafés

  • Mokka Kaffi Reykjavík's oldest café, nearly unchanged since 1958 and famous for its waffles.
  • Reykjavík Roasters A popular specialty roastery with a cosy atmosphere and a record player.
  • Sandholt A long-established family bakery on Laugavegur, running since 1920.
  • Brauð & Co Popular artisan bakery known for sourdough bread and cinnamon rolls.
  • Café Babalú Warm, colourful café a short walk from Hallgrímskirkja.
  • Kaffibrennslan Long-established café on Laugavegur in the heart of downtown.
  • Stofan Kaffihús Cosy café in a historic building with a living-room feel.
  • Grái kötturinn Small basement café known for its hearty breakfast.
  • Kaffi Loki Café opposite Hallgrímskirkja focused on Icelandic food.
  • Kattakaffihúsið Reykjavík's first cat café where resident cats roam between tables.
  • Súfistinn Kaffihús Long-established café in the heart of Hafnarfjörður.
  • Pallett Specialty coffee shop and small bakehouse by the Hafnarfjörður harbour.

Local shops & makers

  • Eimverk Distillery (Flóki) Iceland's first whisky distillery, made entirely from Icelandic barley.
  • Álafoss Wool Store Historic wool store in Álafosskvos selling lopi yarn and sweaters.
  • Handknitting Association of Iceland Shop of the cooperative of Icelandic hand-knitters in central Reykjavík.
  • Kolaportið Flea Market Iceland's largest weekend flea market in the old customs house.
  • Ægir Brugghús Reykjavík craft brewery with taprooms downtown and in Grandi.
  • Farmers & Friends (Farmers Market) Icelandic design brand with a flagship store in Grandi.
  • Búrið – The Icelandic Pantry Grandi cheese shop stocking specialties from small Icelandic producers.
