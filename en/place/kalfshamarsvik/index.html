Kálfshamarsvík — a basalt gem at the tip of Skagi

Kálfshamarsvík is a hidden natural gem at the northern tip of the Skagi peninsula in Northwest Iceland, where striking, regular basalt columns rise from the shore around a small, pretty lighthouse. The hexagonal formations create a dreamlike landscape that resembles nature's own sculptures, making the cove a true paradise for photographers and nature lovers. A thriving fishing village once stood here, now long abandoned, and the ruins still hint at a vanished way of life. Kálfshamarsvík is a quiet, little-visited spot for travellers who want to venture off the beaten path in Iceland.

What to see and do at Kálfshamarsvík

At Kálfshamarsvík, visitors can walk the shore and admire the dramatic basalt columns from many angles, explore the Kálfshamarsviti lighthouse and wander among the ruins of the old abandoned village. The area is rich in birdlife and ideal for birdwatching, especially during nesting season. The stillness and the sea make the cove perfect for photography, reflection and unhurried coastal walks far from the crowds. Anyone who enjoys discovering remote, unspoiled corners of Northwest Iceland will find a unique setting here, where geology, history and coastline weave together.

Getting to Kálfshamarsvík on the Skagi peninsula

Kálfshamarsvík lies at the northern end of the Skagi peninsula, between Skagafjörður and Húnaflói bay in Northwest Iceland. From the town of Skagaströnd, drive north along the peninsula on a gravel road and follow the signs to the cove; the route is long and remote, so allow plenty of time. From the Ring Road, the way out onto Skagi passes through Blönduós or Sauðárkrókur. Because of the gravel road and its distance from the main routes, the site suits travellers seeking tranquillity and pristine nature away from Iceland's busiest tourist stops.

Best time to visit Kálfshamarsvík and useful tips

Summer and late summer are the best times to visit Kálfshamarsvík, when the gravel road is easily passable, daylight is long and birdlife is at its peak. The midnight sun bathes the basalt columns and lighthouse in beautiful light through the evening and night. Dress for the weather, as wind and cool air are common at the tip of Skagi, and bring sturdy shoes for beach walking. Take care near the cliffs and shoreline. As the site is remote, it is wise to pack a picnic and fill up on fuel before heading out onto the peninsula.
