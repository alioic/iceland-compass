Saltverk

Hand-harvested sea salt from Ísafjarðardjúp, dried with geothermal heat.

Saltverk makes hand-harvested sea salt at Reykjanes by Ísafjarðardjúp, where the seawater is considered among the purest and most mineral-rich in the world. The salt is boiled and dried using only geothermal energy, making the process carbon neutral. A range of flavours is produced, from lava salt to liquorice salt.

Hand-harvested salt from the Djúp

Saltverk was founded in 2011 and produces hand-harvested sea salt at Reykjanes by Ísafjarðardjúp. The seawater of the Djúp is considered among the purest and most mineral-rich in the world and is the raw material for the salt. The method is traditional: seawater is pumped into open pans, boiled down and the crystals dried with geothermal heat. The result is a high-quality local product sold widely, and for travellers it is a fine example of Westfjords food production in harmony with nature.

Sustainable production

Saltverk's distinction lies in the fact that the whole process is powered by geothermal energy and is therefore carbon neutral. The seawater is heated with geothermal water from springs at Reykjanes, boiled into a strong brine and the salt finally dried without fossil fuels. This method ties the production directly to the nature of the Westfjords. Saltverk makes a range of flavours, including lava salt and liquorice salt, making the salt a sought-after local product and a fun gift from the fjord.

A Westfjords food experience

Saltverk is part of a growing scene of food producers in the Westfjords making the most of the region's purity. The salt graces tables around the world but has its roots by Ísafjarðardjúp, where the sea, the mountains and geothermal heat meet. For those travelling around the Djúp who want to buy local produce, the salt is a tangible way to take a taste of the Westfjords home. The products are available in shops and through the maker's web store.
