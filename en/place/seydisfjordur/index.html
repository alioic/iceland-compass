Seyðisfjörður

An artists' village by a fjord with a rainbow street and beautiful timber houses.

Seyðisfjörður is one of the most beautiful towns in the East Fjords, surrounded by steep mountains and waterfalls. The rainbow street leading to the blue church is world-famous, and the town buzzes with artistic life. This is where the Norræna ferry arrives from Europe.

Seyðisfjörður — the art town of the East Fjords with its rainbow street

Seyðisfjörður is one of the most beautiful towns in East Iceland, ringed by steep mountains and cascading waterfalls at the head of its fjord. The town is world-famous for the rainbow street leading to its blue church and for its well-preserved Norwegian timber houses, which give it a distinctive charm. Seyðisfjörður buzzes with art and culture and has earned a reputation as one of the country's leading creative towns. The Norröna ferry sails here from mainland Europe, making Seyðisfjörður a unique gateway into Icelandic nature and culture in the East Fjords.

What to see and do in Seyðisfjörður — the rainbow street and the arts

In Seyðisfjörður the rainbow street leading to the blue church is the most popular photo spot, but the town offers far more. The Tvísöngur sound sculpture up on the hillside, the lovely Vestdalsfoss waterfall, galleries, cafés and a lively arts scene make the town a delight to wander. You can kayak on the calm fjord, stroll through the old timber-house quarters, or take to the slopes on hikes into the surrounding mountains. Seyðisfjörður is perfect for anyone wanting to combine culture, history and the dramatic nature of the East Fjords in one place, in a relaxed and creative atmosphere.

Getting to Seyðisfjörður — the mountain pass and the Norröna ferry

Seyðisfjörður lies in the East Fjords, about 27 kilometres from Egilsstaðir over the Fjarðarheiði pass, the highest mountain road to any town in Iceland. The descent into the fjord is exceptionally scenic, running past waterfalls and mountainsides. From Egilsstaðir it is a short drive with good access in summer, but in winter the pass can close with snow. Seyðisfjörður is also the only harbour in Iceland where the Norröna ferry docks with cars and passengers from Denmark and the Faroe Islands, making the town an important gateway into East Iceland and the country as a whole.

Best time to visit Seyðisfjörður and practical tips

Summer is the best time to visit Seyðisfjörður, when the Fjarðarheiði pass is clear, the arts scene is in full bloom and nature is at its finest. In winter the mountain road can close due to snow, so watch road conditions and the forecast closely before setting out. If you are travelling on the Norröna, plan your arrival and departure well in advance. Take your time to enjoy the town, walk the rainbow street and browse the galleries. Dress for the weather, as conditions in the East Fjords can be changeable, and respect the tranquillity of this lovely art town.
