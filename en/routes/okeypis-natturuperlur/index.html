Free natural gems

Spectacular nature at no charge

Many of Iceland's greatest wonders don't cost a thing — the nature itself is free. Here are waterfalls like Goðafoss, Gullfoss, Skógafoss, Dynjandi, Hraunfossar and Aldeyjarfoss, the black beach of Reynisfjara, the colourful Hverir geothermal field at Námaskarð, the iconic Kirkjufell mountain, the basalt canyon Stuðlagil and the sea-stack Hvítserkur. Only parking and the odd toilet fee — otherwise free to visit.

Places on this route

  • Goðafoss The waterfall of the gods, where the pagan gods were cast aside around the year 1000.
  • Gullfoss The golden waterfall plunges in two tiers into a vast canyon.
  • Skógafoss A 60-metre waterfall beneath Eyjafjöll and the start of the Fimmvörðuháls trail.
  • Dynjandi The thundering bridal veil of the Westfjords - a roughly 100 m tiered waterfall.
  • Hraunfossar og Barnafoss Clear springs streaming out from under a lava field over a kilometre-long bank.
  • Aldeyjarfoss A powerful waterfall framed by black basalt columns in Skjálfandafljót.
  • Kolugljúfur A dramatic gorge with a series of waterfalls, named after the troll-woman Kola.
  • Reynisfjara Famous black-sand beach with basalt columns and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks.
  • Kirkjufell Iceland's most photographed mountain, with Kirkjufellsfoss waterfall in the foreground.
  • Hverir við Námaskarð A colourful high-temperature geothermal field with mud pots and fumaroles east of Mývatn.
  • Brimketill og Gunnuhver A natural pool in the rocks by the surf and the peninsula's most powerful geothermal area.
  • Hljóðaklettar Striking basalt rock formations in Vesturdalur within the Jökulsárgljúfur canyon.
  • Stuðlagil A spectacular basalt-column canyon with a blue-green glacial river in Jökuldalur.
  • Hvítserkur A 15 m sea stack rising from the sea off the Vatnsnes peninsula.
