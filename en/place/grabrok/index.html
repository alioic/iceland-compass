Grábrók – an accessible volcanic crater beside the Ring Road in West Iceland

Grábrók is one of Iceland's best-known and most accessible craters, located right beside the Ring Road in the Norðurárdalur valley in West Iceland. This scoria crater, formed in an eruption roughly 3,400 years ago, is now a popular stop on the drive through Borgarfjörður and on toward the north. Grábrók is the tallest of a small cluster of craters, with reddish scoria and a striking symmetrical shape. Its proximity to the main road and its well-built paths make it an ideal quick stop for travellers and families exploring West Iceland.

Things to see and do at Grábrók

The main attraction at Grábrók is a short but rewarding climb to the crater rim along built-up stairs and platforms that protect the fragile scoria. From the top there are sweeping views over the Norðurárdalur valley, surrounding lava fields and neighbouring craters, especially beautiful in evening light. The walk takes only about half an hour round trip and suits most visitors. Nearby lie Hreðavatn lake and the green surroundings of Bifröst, making the area a pleasant place to stretch your legs, take photographs and enjoy the natural scenery of West Iceland.

Getting to Grábrók – location in Norðurárdalur

Grábrók is remarkably easy to reach, sitting directly beside the Ring Road (Route 1) in Norðurárdalur, near Bifröst and about an hour's drive north of Borgarnes. From Reykjavík the drive takes roughly an hour and a quarter to an hour and a half. A marked car park sits at the base of the crater, with the stairway leading straight up from there. Its location makes Grábrók a perfect stop for travellers heading between West and North Iceland, whether on a day trip from the capital or a longer road trip around the country.

Best time to visit Grábrók and practical tips

Grábrók is open all year and works well as a short stop in any season. Summer and autumn offer the most comfortable walking conditions and the finest colours in the valley, while in winter the crater can be striking under snow if the stairs are passable. Dress for the weather, as the wind can be sharp on the exposed rim. Stay on the marked stairs and platforms to protect the delicate scoria, and allow a few minutes to enjoy the panorama over Norðurárdalur before continuing your journey.
