Skógar Museum

A folk museum in Skógar with thousands of artefacts, turf houses and old buildings showing Iceland's rural past.

Skógar Museum beneath Eyjafjöll is one of Iceland's largest folk museums, holding thousands of artefacts that illuminate how Icelanders lived and worked in centuries past. The grounds feature old turf houses, a church and buildings that visitors can step inside to experience earlier ways of life. There is also a transport and technology museum, making the visit varied for the whole family.

A journey back in time

Skógar Museum beneath Eyjafjöll is an ideal destination for families wanting to explore Icelandic heritage. The grounds hold old turf houses, a church and buildings that visitors can step inside to experience how people lived in harsh conditions centuries ago. Thousands of artefacts, from tools to handicrafts, paint a vivid picture of daily life. For children it is a tangible way to understand life before modern comforts, turning the visit into a genuine journey back in time.

A varied museum beneath Eyjafjöll

Skógar Museum is not a single museum but a collection of exhibitions that make the visit varied. Alongside the folk museum there is a transport and technology museum tracing the history of travel, communications and technology in Iceland. This appeals to a broad range of interests, whether in old craftsmanship or in machines and vehicles. The grounds are large, so it is worth allowing time to see everything. Its location beneath Eyjafjöll, near Skógafoss, makes it a perfect addition to a south-coast trip.

Visiting Skógar

Skógar Museum is in Skógar beneath Eyjafjöll, along Route 1 and just steps from the majestic Skógafoss waterfall. The museum is open nearly year-round and works well regardless of weather since much of it is indoors. A visit fits naturally into a south-coast journey alongside the region's waterfalls and beaches. For families it is both educational and enjoyable, a place to walk through old houses, view artefacts and experience Icelandic history in a lively way.
